SPARSE MIXTURE-OF-EXPERTS TRANSFORMERS WITH DYNAMIC ROUTING FOR LONG-CONTEXT REASONING

Submitted to NeurIPS 2024 | Received March 14, 2024 | Accepted August 22, 2024

Authors: Yuki Tanaka¹, Priya Mehrotra², Aleksandr Volkov¹, Chen Wei³, Fatima Al-Rashid²
¹ Institute for Advanced Computation, Tokyo University of Technology

³ Tsinghua University, Department of Computer Science

ABSTRACT

We present DRMOE (Dynamic Routing Mixture-of-Experts), a transformer-based architecture that achieves state-of-the-art performance on long-context reasoning benchmarks while reducing active parameter utilization by 61.3% compared to dense baseline models of equivalent total capacity. DRMOE employs a learned token-level routing mechanism that dynamically assigns input tokens to specialized expert subnetworks, enabling efficient computation across contexts up to 128,000 tokens. On the SCROLLS benchmark suite, DRMOE-47B achieves an average score of 74.8, surpassing GPT-4-Turbo (72.1) and Mixtral-8x22B (68.4) while using 38% fewer FLOPs per forward pass. We release model weights and training code at github.com/tanaka-lab/drmoe.

1. INTRODUCTION

The scaling of large language models has produced remarkable capabilities, yet dense transformer architectures impose computational costs that grow quadratically with sequence length and linearly with parameter count. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures address parameter efficiency by activating only a subset of model parameters for any given input, but prior implementations suffer from load imbalance, expert collapse, and degraded performance on tasks requiring sustained multi-step reasoning across long documents.

This paper introduces three primary contributions:

- A dynamic routing algorithm (DynRoute-v2) that conditions expert selection on both token identity and positional context within the sequence
- A novel auxiliary loss term, the Coherence Regularization Loss (CRL), that penalizes routing inconsistency across semantically related token spans
- Empirical demonstration that DRMOE-47B achieves 74.8 average SCROLLS score with a peak memory footprint of 94 GB across 8 H100 GPUs during inference

2. RELATED WORK

Standard MoE implementations, including the Switch Transformer (Fedus et al., 2022) and GLaM (Du et al., 2022), route tokens independently using a top-k gating function applied to a learned router matrix. Mixtral-8x7B (Mistral AI, January 2024) demonstrated that MoE models can match dense model quality at reduced inference cost, activating 2 of 8 experts per token. However, Mixtral's routing is stateless with respect to sequence position, which we show in Section 5.2 leads to a 14.7% performance degradation on tasks requiring cross-paragraph coreference resolution.

ST-MoE (Zoph et al., 2022) introduced router z-loss to improve training stability, reducing expert collapse events from 23 per 100K training steps to fewer than 3. We adopt z-loss in our training objective and extend it with CRL.

3. MODEL ARCHITECTURE

3.1 Overall Structure

DRMOE-47B contains 64 transformer layers. Layers 1 through 16 and 49 through 64 are dense feed-forward layers, retaining full parameter activation for positional encoding and output projection tasks. Layers 17 through 48 (32 layers) employ the MoE feed-forward replacement with 16 experts per layer, of which 3 are activated per token per layer.

Total parameter count: 47.3 billion
Active parameters per forward pass: 12.8 billion (27.1% of total)
Expert hidden dimension: 8,192
Attention heads: 64 (grouped-query attention with 8 key-value heads)
Context window: 128,000 tokens (trained with RoPE scaling factor 8.0)

3.2 DynRoute-v2 Routing Mechanism

Standard top-k routing computes expert scores as:

g(x) = Softmax(W_r · x)

where x is the token embedding and W_r is the router weight matrix. DynRoute-v2 augments this with a positional context vector p derived from a lightweight 2-layer MLP applied to the token's relative position encoding:

g(x, p) = Softmax(W_r · x + α · W_p · p)

where α = 0.23 is a learned scalar initialized to 0.1 and annealed during training. This modification increases router parameter count by only 0.4% while improving routing consistency across long sequences.

3.3 Coherence Regularization Loss

CRL penalizes cases where semantically similar tokens (measured by cosine similarity > 0.85 in embedding space) are routed to entirely disjoint expert sets. Formally:

L_CRL = λ · Σ_{(i,j): sim(x_i, x_j) > 0.85} max(0, 1 - |E_i ∩ E_j| / k)

where E_i is the set of experts selected for token i, k = 3 is the top-k value, and λ = 0.01. Adding CRL reduced routing entropy variance from 1.84 to 0.97 nats across validation sequences.

5. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS

5.1 SCROLLS Benchmark

SCROLLS evaluates long-document understanding across seven tasks: SummScreenFD, QMSum, QASPER, NarrativeQA, QuALITY, ContractNLI, and GovReport.

DRMOE-47B           47.3       12.8       74.8          2.1e12
GPT-4-Turbo         ~1,800     ~1,800     72.1          N/A
Mixtral-8x22B       141        39         68.4          3.4e12
Llama-3-70B         70         70         65.7          5.8e12
Claude-3-Sonnet     ~70        ~70        71.3          N/A

On the QuALITY multiple-choice subset, DRMOE-47B achieves 81.4% accuracy, compared to 79.2% for GPT-4-Turbo and 71.8% for Mixtral-8x22B.

5.2 Ablation Studies

We conducted ablations on a 7B-parameter version of DRMOE trained for 200B tokens to isolate the contribution of each architectural component.

Component Removed          SCROLLS Avg    Δ vs Full Model
Full DRMOE-7B              61.3           —
Remove CRL                 59.1           -2.2
Remove positional context  58.7           -2.6
Use top-2 instead of top-3 57.4           -3.9
Dense layers only (7B)     56.8           -4.5
Standard top-k routing     54.6           -6.7

The positional context augmentation in DynRoute-v2 contributes a 2.6-point improvement, confirming our hypothesis that stateless routing degrades long-context coherence.

5.3 Expert Utilization

Analysis of expert activation patterns across 10,000 validation sequences reveals that 14 of 16 experts per layer receive at least 3% of tokens on average, indicating healthy load distribution. The two least-utilized experts per layer receive 1.8% and 2.1% of tokens respectively, compared to 0.3% in baseline top-k routing without z-loss. Maximum expert load imbalance (ratio of most-loaded to least-loaded expert) is 4.2x, versus 11.7x for Mixtral-8x7B as measured on the same validation set.

6. INFERENCE EFFICIENCY

On a single 8×H100 node, DRMOE-47B generates tokens at 47.3 tokens per second for batch size 1 at 32K context length, compared to 31.8 tokens/second for a dense 13B model with equivalent active parameter count. At 128K context length, throughput decreases to 18.6 tokens per second due to attention memory bandwidth constraints.

Quantized to INT8 using GPTQ (4-bit weights, 8-bit activations), DRMOE-47B fits within 52 GB of GPU memory with less than 1.1% perplexity degradation on Pile validation data (perplexity increases from 6.84 to 6.92).

7. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK

DRMOE-47B underperforms on tasks requiring precise numerical reasoning, scoring 58.3% on MATH-500 compared to GPT-4-Turbo's 72.6%. We attribute this to insufficient representation of mathematical content in training data (5% combined STEM sources). Future work will explore expert specialization explicitly seeded for mathematical reasoning and code execution.

The CRL loss introduces a 7.3% training throughput overhead due to pairwise similarity computation. We are investigating approximate nearest-neighbor methods to reduce this cost.

8. CONCLUSION

DRMOE-47B demonstrates that dynamic, position-aware routing in mixture-of-experts architectures substantially improves long-context reasoning performance. Achieving 74.8 on SCROLLS with only 12.8 billion active parameters per forward pass, DRMOE-47B establishes a new efficiency frontier for long-context language modeling. Model weights, training code, and evaluation scripts are publicly available.
