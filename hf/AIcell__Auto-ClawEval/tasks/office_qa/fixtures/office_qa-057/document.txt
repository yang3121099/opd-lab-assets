EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania's Office of the Budget presents this Quarterly Financial Report for the third quarter of Fiscal Year 2023-2024, covering the period of October 1, 2023 through December 31, 2023. This report details revenue collections, expenditure patterns, and fund balances compared to the second quarter of Fiscal Year 2023-2024 (July 1, 2023 through September 30, 2023), as well as year-over-year comparisons where applicable.

Total General Fund revenues for Q3 FY2023-2024 reached $10.847 billion, representing an increase of $1.243 billion, or 12.9 percent, over Q2 FY2023-2024 revenues of $9.604 billion. Total General Fund expenditures for Q3 FY2023-2024 were $11.392 billion, an increase of $892 million, or 8.5 percent, over Q2 expenditures of $10.500 billion. The resulting quarterly deficit of $545 million reflects seasonal spending patterns consistent with prior fiscal years, particularly elevated Medicaid disbursements and school district payments in the October-December period.

Budget Secretary Uri Monson noted that the Commonwealth remains on track to meet its annual revenue estimate of $42.8 billion for FY2023-2024, with year-to-date collections through December 31 totaling $30.619 billion, or 71.5 percent of the annual estimate.

1.2 Key Revenue Observations

Personal Income Tax (PIT) collections of $4.307 billion in Q3 exceeded Q2 collections by $466.4 million, driven primarily by stronger-than-anticipated employer withholding payments in October and November 2023. The Department of Revenue reported that employer withholding alone accounted for $3.612 billion of total PIT collections, up from $3.201 billion in Q2.

Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT) collections showed the strongest quarter-over-quarter growth at 34.9 percent, rising from $1.103 billion to $1.487 billion. This increase reflects the statutory reduction of the CNIT rate from 8.99 percent to 8.49 percent effective January 1, 2024, which prompted accelerated estimated payments by corporations seeking to settle obligations under the higher rate before year-end.

Sales and Use Tax collections of $3.215 billion reflect robust consumer spending during the October-December holiday period. The Pennsylvania Department of Revenue attributed approximately $187 million of the quarter-over-quarter increase to online marketplace facilitator collections, which have grown 22.4 percent since the same quarter in FY2022-2023.

Realty Transfer Tax collections declined by $14.3 million, or 10.0 percent, consistent with continued softness in Pennsylvania's residential real estate market. The number of recorded deed transfers statewide fell from approximately 47,200 in Q2 to 41,800 in Q3, reflecting elevated mortgage interest rates that averaged 7.44 percent during the quarter according to Freddie Mac data.

2.2 Key Expenditure Observations

Medical Assistance expenditures of $4.202 billion in Q3 represent the largest single expenditure category and increased by $354.4 million, or 9.2 percent, over Q2. The Department of Human Services reported that total Medicaid enrollment as of December 31, 2023 stood at 3,487,214 individuals, compared to 3,441,892 at the close of Q2. The increase reflects ongoing Medicaid unwinding activities following the end of continuous enrollment protections, with the Commonwealth having processed 1.2 million redeterminations since April 2023.

Basic Education Subsidy payments increased by $364.9 million, or 22.5 percent, from Q2 to Q3. This increase is largely structural: the Commonwealth distributes a disproportionate share of annual school district subsidies in the October-December quarter to align with local school district fiscal calendars. The Q3 distribution included the second installment of the $567 million supplemental basic education funding appropriated under Act 55 of 2023, signed by Governor Josh Shapiro on December 13, 2023.

Debt service payments declined by $13.6 million, or 2.2 percent, reflecting the retirement of $425 million in General Obligation bonds that matured on November 1, 2023, partially offset by interest payments on $750 million in new bonds issued in September 2023 at a weighted average interest rate of 4.87 percent.

SECTION 3: FUND BALANCE AND RESERVE STATUS

3.1 General Fund Balance

The General Fund unappropriated balance as of December 31, 2023 was $1.847 billion, compared to $2.392 billion as of September 30, 2023. The $545 million decrease reflects the Q3 operating deficit described above.

3.2 Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund)

The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund maintained a balance of $5.024 billion as of December 31, 2023, unchanged from September 30, 2023. No transfers into or out of the Rainy Day Fund occurred during Q3. This balance represents approximately 11.7 percent of the Commonwealth's annual General Fund appropriations of $42.8 billion, exceeding the Government Finance Officers Association's recommended minimum reserve of 8.3 percent (equivalent to one month of operating expenditures).

3.3 Special Funds Overview

Motor License Fund revenues for Q3 totaled $1.127 billion, an increase of $43.2 million, or 4.0 percent, over Q2 revenues of $1.084 billion. The Lottery Fund reported Q3 revenues of $387.4 million against expenditures of $341.2 million, yielding a quarterly surplus of $46.2 million. Lottery Fund revenues increased by $28.7 million, or 8.0 percent, over Q2, driven by strong iLottery digital platform performance, which generated $94.3 million in Q3 compared to $81.6 million in Q2.

SECTION 4: YEAR-TO-DATE PERFORMANCE AND ANNUAL OUTLOOK

Year-to-date General Fund revenues through December 31, 2023 totaled $30.619 billion, representing 71.5 percent of the annual estimate of $42.8 billion. This compares favorably to the same period in FY2022-2023, when year-to-date revenues of $29.874 billion represented 70.8 percent of that year's annual estimate of $42.2 billion.

Year-to-date expenditures through December 31, 2023 totaled $31.892 billion, or 74.5 percent of total appropriations. The Office of the Budget projects that full-year revenues will meet or modestly exceed the $42.8 billion estimate, contingent on continued labor market stability. Pennsylvania's seasonally adjusted unemployment rate of 3.7 percent as of November 2023, reported by the Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry, supports continued strength in personal income tax withholding through the remainder of the fiscal year.

The Office of the Budget will present the Governor's Executive Budget for FY2024-2025 on February 6, 2024, which will incorporate updated revenue estimates reflecting Q3 performance data contained in this report.

SECTION 5: NOTES AND METHODOLOGY

All figures in this report are presented on a budgetary basis of accounting consistent with the Commonwealth's Annual Comprehensive Financial Report. Revenue figures represent collections deposited to the General Fund during the respective quarter. Expenditure figures represent warrants issued during the quarter. Figures may not sum precisely due to rounding. Comparative Q2 figures have been revised from the previously published Second Quarter Report to reflect two adjustments totaling $12.4 million identified during the quarterly reconciliation process conducted by the Office of the Comptroller.
