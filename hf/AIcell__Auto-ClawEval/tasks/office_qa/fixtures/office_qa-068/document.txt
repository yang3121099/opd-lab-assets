EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Total federal tax revenues collected during Fiscal Year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023) reached $4.44 trillion, representing a decline of 9.3 percent from the record $4.90 trillion collected in Fiscal Year 2022. This contraction marks the first year-over-year decrease in federal tax receipts since Fiscal Year 2020, when revenues fell 1.2 percent during the initial phase of the COVID-19 pandemic. The decline is attributable primarily to reduced individual income tax collections, lower capital gains realizations following equity market corrections in calendar year 2022, and a modest softening in corporate profitability across several key sectors.

Despite the overall decline, payroll tax revenues demonstrated resilience, growing 7.1 percent to $1.61 trillion, reflecting continued strength in labor markets and sustained wage growth averaging 4.4 percent nationally. This report provides a comprehensive breakdown of revenue by category, year-over-year comparisons, regional distribution, and projections for Fiscal Year 2024.

SECTION 1: TOTAL REVENUE OVERVIEW

Federal tax receipts by major category for Fiscal Year 2023 are summarized below:

REVENUE CATEGORY                          FY2023 ($B)    FY2022 ($B)    CHANGE (%)
Individual Income Taxes                   $2,176.5       $2,632.1       -17.3%
Payroll Taxes (Social Insurance)          $1,613.8       $1,506.7       +7.1%
Corporate Income Taxes                    $419.7         $424.9         -1.2%
Excise Taxes                              $88.1          $89.5          -1.6%
Estate and Gift Taxes                     $34.0          $38.8          -12.4%
Customs Duties                            $80.3          $99.8          -19.5%
Miscellaneous Receipts                    $28.6          $108.2         -73.6%
TOTAL                                     $4,441.0       $4,900.0       -9.3%

The most significant driver of the overall decline was the $455.6 billion reduction in individual income tax collections, which fell from $2.632 trillion in FY2022 to $2.177 trillion in FY2023. IRS Commissioner Daniel Werfel, in testimony before the Senate Finance Committee on November 9, 2023, attributed this primarily to a $1.1 trillion reduction in realized capital gains reported on individual returns, following the S&P 500's 19.4 percent decline in calendar year 2022.

Miscellaneous receipts experienced the sharpest percentage decline, falling 73.6 percent from $108.2 billion to $28.6 billion. This category had been artificially elevated in FY2022 due to $79.4 billion in Federal Reserve remittances to the Treasury, which collapsed to near zero in FY2023 as the Federal Reserve began operating at a net loss following its aggressive interest rate increases beginning in March 2022.

SECTION 2: INDIVIDUAL INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Individual income taxes remain the single largest source of federal revenue, comprising 49.0 percent of total receipts in FY2023, down from 53.7 percent in FY2022.

Key findings within this category include:

- Wage and salary withholding collections totaled $1.847 trillion, an increase of 6.2 percent from $1.739 trillion in FY2022, consistent with strong employment and wage growth.

- Estimated tax payments, which capture capital gains, business income, and investment income, fell 38.7 percent from $612.4 billion to $375.7 billion. This decline directly reflects the capital gains contraction noted above.

- Tax refunds issued during FY2023 totaled $659.4 billion, a decrease of 14.8 percent from $774.0 billion in FY2022. The average refund amount was $2,812, compared to $3,176 in the prior year.

- The top 1 percent of income earners, defined as those with adjusted gross income above $548,336 based on the most recent Statistics of Income data, accounted for approximately 42.3 percent of all individual income tax liability.

- Returns filed electronically accounted for 94.3 percent of all individual returns processed, up from 92.1 percent in FY2022.

SECTION 3: CORPORATE INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Corporate income tax receipts totaled $419.7 billion in FY2023, a modest decline of 1.2 percent from $424.9 billion in FY2022. This relative stability is notable given broader economic headwinds and reflects the lagged nature of corporate tax payments, which are based on prior-year profitability.

The new 15 percent Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT), enacted under the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 and effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2022, generated an estimated $4.8 billion in additional revenue during FY2023, below the Joint Committee on Taxation's initial projection of $9.0 billion for the first full year of implementation. Treasury officials indicated that the lower-than-projected yield reflects the complexity of transition rules and the availability of certain credits that offset CAMT liability.

The financial sector, including banking, insurance, and investment management, contributed $87.3 billion in corporate taxes, representing 20.8 percent of total corporate receipts. The technology sector contributed $64.1 billion, or 15.3 percent of total corporate receipts.

SECTION 4: PAYROLL TAX PERFORMANCE

Payroll taxes, which fund Social Security and Medicare programs, were the only major revenue category to post meaningful growth in FY2023. Collections of $1.614 trillion represent a 7.1 percent increase and reflect the following components:

- Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance (OASDI): $1,068.4 billion, up 6.8 percent
- Hospital Insurance (Medicare Part A): $349.2 billion, up 8.1 percent
- Unemployment Insurance taxes: $47.1 billion, down 3.2 percent
- Railroad Retirement and other: $149.1 billion, up 6.4 percent

The Social Security wage base increased from $147,000 in calendar year 2022 to $160,200 in calendar year 2023, a 8.98 percent increase, which contributed to higher OASDI collections independent of employment growth. Total nonfarm payroll employment averaged 155.8 million jobs during FY2023, compared to 151.9 million in FY2022.

SECTION 6: ENFORCEMENT AND COMPLIANCE REVENUE

The IRS collected $63.6 billion through enforcement activities in FY2023, including audits, automated underreporter programs, and collection actions. This represents a 12.4 percent increase from $56.6 billion in FY2022 and reflects the initial impact of the $80 billion in additional IRS funding authorized under the Inflation Reduction Act.

The audit rate for individual returns with income above $1 million increased to 1.1 percent in FY2023, up from 0.7 percent in FY2022. The overall individual audit rate remained at 0.44 percent. The IRS Criminal Investigation division initiated 2,676 investigations in FY2023, resulting in 1,409 prosecutions and a conviction rate of 90.2 percent.

SECTION 7: FISCAL YEAR 2024 PROJECTIONS

The Office of Management and Budget projects total federal tax revenues of $4.71 trillion for Fiscal Year 2024, representing a recovery of 6.1 percent from FY2023 levels. This projection assumes:

- Individual income tax receipts recovering to approximately $2.39 trillion, driven by partial recovery in capital gains realizations as equity markets rebounded 24.2 percent in calendar year 2023
- Corporate income taxes declining modestly to $409.0 billion as profit margins compress
- Payroll taxes continuing to grow, reaching an estimated $1.68 trillion
- Customs duties stabilizing near $82.0 billion

These projections carry significant uncertainty. A 10 percent decline in equity markets from current levels would reduce projected individual income tax receipts by an estimated $85 to $110 billion. Conversely, sustained wage growth above 4 percent would add approximately $18 billion to payroll tax collections for each additional percentage point of growth.

CONCLUSION

Fiscal Year 2023 demonstrated the sensitivity of federal revenue to capital markets performance and the structural importance of payroll taxes as a stable revenue base. The 9.3 percent overall decline, while significant, was concentrated in categories directly linked to asset valuations rather than underlying economic activity. The resilience of withholding taxes and payroll contributions confirms continued labor market strength. Policymakers should note that the revenue base remains heavily dependent on the income and investment activity of high-earning households, creating inherent volatility in year-to-year collections.
