EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of government expenditure trends for the Commonwealth of Ridgeland covering fiscal years 2019 through 2023. Total government expenditures reached $47.3 billion in FY2023, representing a 6.2 percent increase over the FY2022 figure of $44.5 billion. Over the five-year period analyzed, cumulative expenditure growth stands at 31.4 percent, driven primarily by healthcare, infrastructure, and debt service obligations. This report identifies key cost drivers, departmental variances, and forward-looking projections to inform legislative appropriations for FY2025.

---

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF TOTAL EXPENDITURES

Total government expenditures have grown consistently over the review period, with the exception of a modest contraction in FY2021 attributable to pandemic-era austerity measures and federal reimbursement offsets.

TABLE 1: TOTAL GOVERNMENT EXPENDITURES BY FISCAL YEAR

Fiscal Year | Total Expenditure | Year-over-Year Change | % Change
FY2019     | $36.0 billion     | —                     | —
FY2020     | $38.7 billion     | +$2.7 billion         | +7.5%
FY2021     | $38.2 billion     | -$0.5 billion         | -1.3%
FY2022     | $44.5 billion     | +$6.3 billion         | +16.5%
FY2023     | $47.3 billion     | +$2.8 billion         | +6.2%

The sharp increase in FY2022 reflects the expiration of federal emergency funding that had previously offset state obligations, combined with a $3.1 billion infrastructure investment package approved by the Ridgeland General Assembly under House Bill 1142 on April 7, 2021, with disbursements beginning in the second quarter of FY2022.

SECTION 2: EXPENDITURE BY DEPARTMENT

The five largest spending departments in FY2023 accounted for 78.4 percent of total expenditures, consistent with the 77.9 percent share recorded in FY2022.

TABLE 2: TOP FIVE DEPARTMENTS BY EXPENDITURE, FY2023

Department                          | FY2023 Expenditure | Share of Total | FY2022 Expenditure | Change
Department of Health Services       | $13.8 billion      | 29.2%          | $12.9 billion      | +7.0%
Department of Education             | $10.4 billion      | 22.0%          | $9.8 billion       | +6.1%
Department of Transportation        | $6.7 billion       | 14.2%          | $6.1 billion       | +9.8%
Department of Public Safety         | $3.9 billion       | 8.2%           | $3.7 billion       | +5.4%
Department of Social Services       | $2.3 billion       | 4.9%           | $2.2 billion       | +4.5%

The Department of Health Services remains the single largest expenditure category for the seventh consecutive fiscal year. Its FY2023 budget of $13.8 billion includes $4.2 billion in Medicaid matching funds, $2.9 billion in hospital reimbursement grants, and $1.6 billion in mental health and substance abuse programming, the latter representing a 22.3 percent increase over FY2022 following the passage of the Ridgeland Behavioral Health Expansion Act (Senate Bill 88) on September 19, 2022.

The Department of Transportation recorded the highest year-over-year percentage increase among major departments at 9.8 percent, largely attributable to Phase 2 construction on the Northern Corridor Highway Expansion Project, which consumed $1.4 billion in FY2023 alone. The project, managed under contract with Hargrove Infrastructure Partners LLC, is scheduled for completion in November 2025 at a total projected cost of $4.8 billion.

SECTION 3: PERSONNEL AND COMPENSATION EXPENDITURES

Personnel costs represent the largest single line item within departmental budgets, totaling $18.6 billion in FY2023, or 39.3 percent of total government expenditures. This compares to $17.4 billion in FY2022, an increase of $1.2 billion or 6.9 percent.

Key drivers of personnel cost growth include:

- A 3.5 percent cost-of-living adjustment effective July 1, 2022, applied to all classified employees, adding approximately $580 million to the annual payroll base.
- An increase in the state workforce headcount from 94,200 full-time equivalent employees in FY2022 to 96,750 in FY2023, a net addition of 2,550 positions, primarily within the Department of Health Services (1,100 positions) and the Department of Corrections (640 positions).
- Rising employer contributions to the Ridgeland State Employees Retirement System (RSERS), which increased from 18.7 percent of payroll in FY2022 to 20.1 percent in FY2023, reflecting actuarial adjustments approved by the RSERS Board of Trustees on February 28, 2023.
- Health insurance premium costs per employee rose 8.4 percent to an average of $14,220 annually, driven by increased utilization and pharmaceutical cost inflation.

---

SECTION 4: DEBT SERVICE AND CAPITAL EXPENDITURES

Debt service obligations totaled $3.4 billion in FY2023, representing 7.2 percent of total expenditures. This figure includes $2.1 billion in principal repayments and $1.3 billion in interest payments on outstanding general obligation bonds. The Commonwealth's total outstanding debt as of June 30, 2023, stood at $28.7 billion, up from $26.4 billion at the close of FY2022.

The debt-to-revenue ratio increased from 58.3 percent in FY2022 to 61.1 percent in FY2023, approaching the statutory ceiling of 65 percent established under the Ridgeland Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2015. Comptroller General Holloway has formally notified the Governor's Office and the Joint Legislative Budget Committee of this trend in a memorandum dated January 9, 2024, recommending a moratorium on new general obligation bond issuances until the ratio falls below 57 percent.

Capital expenditures, excluding debt-financed projects, totaled $2.8 billion in FY2023. The three largest capital projects by expenditure were:

1. Northern Corridor Highway Expansion (Phase 2): $1.4 billion
2. Ridgeland State University Medical Center Renovation: $340 million
3. Statewide Digital Infrastructure Modernization Initiative: $290 million

---

SECTION 5: SOCIAL SERVICES AND TRANSFER PAYMENTS

Transfer payments, including direct assistance programs, totaled $6.9 billion in FY2023, representing 14.6 percent of total expenditures. This category grew 5.3 percent from $6.5 billion in FY2022.

The Ridgeland Family Assistance Program served 312,400 households in FY2023 at a total cost of $1.8 billion, with an average monthly benefit of $479 per household. The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program state administrative match cost $412 million. The Low-Income Housing Assistance Program disbursed $680 million to 87,600 recipient households, a 12.1 percent increase in caseload over FY2022 driven by rising rental costs in the Ridgeland metropolitan area, where median rents increased 18.7 percent between January 2022 and December 2023 according to the Ridgeland Housing Authority's Annual Market Survey.

---

SECTION 6: FIVE-YEAR TREND ANALYSIS AND PROJECTIONS

Compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of total expenditures over the FY2019–FY2023 period is 7.1 percent. Healthcare and debt service have grown at above-average rates of 9.3 percent and 8.8 percent CAGR respectively, while education expenditure growth has tracked closer to the overall average at 6.9 percent CAGR.

Based on current legislative commitments, contractual obligations, and demographic projections prepared by the Ridgeland Office of Economic Analysis, total expenditures are projected to reach $50.4 billion in FY2024 and $53.1 billion in FY2025. These projections assume no new major legislative programs and incorporate a 3.0 percent general inflation assumption consistent with the Federal Reserve's medium-term outlook as of December 2023.

The primary fiscal risk identified for the projection period is continued growth in Medicaid enrollment, which increased 6.8 percent in FY2023 to 1.04 million enrollees. If enrollment growth continues at this rate, Health Services expenditures could exceed projections by $600 million to $900 million annually by FY2025.

---

SECTION 7: RECOMMENDATIONS

The Division of Fiscal Analysis offers the following recommendations to the Joint Legislative Budget Committee:

1. Implement a formal expenditure growth cap of 5.5 percent annually for non-mandated programs beginning in FY2025 to reduce pressure on the debt-to-revenue ratio.
2. Commission an independent actuarial review of RSERS funding assumptions by September 30, 2024, given that the system's funded ratio declined from 74.2 percent in FY2022 to 71.8 percent in FY2023.
3. Establish a Medicaid Cost Containment Task Force with a reporting deadline of December 1, 2024, to evaluate managed care expansion options.
4. Accelerate the Statewide Digital Infrastructure Modernization Initiative to capture projected administrative savings of $180 million annually beginning in FY2026.
