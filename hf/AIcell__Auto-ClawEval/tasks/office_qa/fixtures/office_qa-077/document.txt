EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania concluded the third quarter of Fiscal Year 2023-2024 with General Fund revenues of $11.84 billion, representing a 3.2 percent increase over the second quarter total of $11.47 billion. Year-to-date General Fund revenues through March 31, 2024 stand at $34.61 billion, tracking approximately 1.7 percent above the official estimate established by the Independent Fiscal Office in June 2023. Total expenditures for Q3 reached $12.31 billion, a 4.8 percent increase from the $11.75 billion recorded in Q2 FY2023-2024. The resulting quarterly deficit of $470 million was partially offset by a beginning fund balance of $1.92 billion carried forward from the second quarter.

Secretary of the Budget Uri Monson noted in his transmittal letter that the Commonwealth's fiscal position remains stable despite elevated spending pressures in the Medicaid and public education sectors, and that the administration projects full-year revenues will meet or exceed the $46.1 billion annual estimate.

Personal Income Tax collections of $4.507 billion in Q3 represent the strongest quarterly PIT performance in the current fiscal year, driven by higher-than-anticipated withholding receipts in January and February 2024 and robust estimated payments received in March. The Bureau of Revenue Analysis attributes the 6.8 percent quarter-over-quarter increase primarily to strong labor market conditions, with Pennsylvania's unemployment rate holding at 3.4 percent as of February 2024, and to capital gains realizations that exceeded projections by an estimated $112 million.

Corporate Net Income Tax collections of $1.943 billion reflect continued profitability in the financial services and manufacturing sectors. The CNIT rate reduction from 8.99 percent to 8.49 percent, which took effect January 1, 2024 under Act 53 of 2022's phased reduction schedule, was anticipated to reduce Q3 receipts by approximately $95 million relative to prior-year levels; actual collections nonetheless exceeded Q2 by $66.7 million, suggesting underlying corporate profitability growth stronger than modeled.

Sales and Use Tax experienced a marginal decline of $14.5 million (-0.5 percent) from Q2, consistent with seasonal patterns. January and February typically see reduced consumer spending following the holiday retail period. The Independent Fiscal Office's seasonal adjustment model had projected a Q3 SUT decline of approximately $22 million, meaning actual performance outperformed the adjusted baseline by roughly $7.5 million.

Realty Transfer Tax collections of $174.6 million declined 6.8 percent from Q2, reflecting continued softness in Pennsylvania's residential real estate market. Statewide home sales volume fell 9.1 percent year-over-year in the January-March 2024 period according to data from the Pennsylvania Association of Realtors, partially offset by a 2.3 percent increase in median sale prices to $247,500.

Medical Assistance expenditures of $4.128 billion in Q3 represent the largest single expenditure category and the most significant driver of quarter-over-quarter spending growth. The $286.4 million increase (7.5 percent) reflects two primary factors: a January 2024 rate increase for managed care organizations averaging 4.2 percent, and the continued unwinding of continuous enrollment provisions under the federal Families First Coronavirus Response Act. As of March 31, 2024, the Department of Human Services had processed redeterminations for approximately 1.84 million Medicaid enrollees since the unwinding process began in April 2023, with 312,000 individuals disenrolled and subsequently requiring transition services that generated short-term cost increases.

The General Government Operations category increased $158.2 million (17.1 percent) quarter-over-quarter, the largest percentage increase among major categories. This reflects the January 2024 implementation of the collective bargaining agreement ratified in November 2023 covering approximately 67,000 state employees represented by AFSCME Council 13, which included a 5.25 percent general wage increase effective January 1, 2024, and retroactive payments totaling an estimated $94 million processed in January and February.

Transportation expenditures declined $21.3 million (-6.7 percent) from Q2, consistent with seasonal patterns in capital project disbursements. PennDOT's construction season typically accelerates in Q4 (April through June), and several major projects including the I-95 Philadelphia reconstruction and the Route 30 Bypass expansion in Lancaster County are expected to generate significant disbursements in the final quarter.

SECTION 3: FUND BALANCE AND LIQUIDITY

The General Fund ended Q3 with a balance of $1.45 billion, compared to $1.92 billion at the close of Q2. The $470 million net decrease reflects the quarterly revenue shortfall relative to expenditures. The Commonwealth maintains a separate Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund) with a balance of $5.12 billion as of March 31, 2024, unchanged from the prior quarter. This reserve represents approximately 11.1 percent of projected annual General Fund revenues, exceeding the Government Finance Officers Association's recommended minimum of 8.3 percent (equivalent to one month of operating expenditures).

Short-term borrowing activity in Q3 consisted of $750 million in Tax Revenue Anticipation Notes (TRANs) issued February 15, 2024, at an interest rate of 4.87 percent, maturing June 28, 2024. This issuance was consistent with the Commonwealth's historical practice of managing seasonal cash flow variations and was $150 million lower than the comparable TRAN issuance in February 2023.

SECTION 4: YEAR-TO-DATE PERFORMANCE AND FULL-YEAR OUTLOOK

Year-to-date General Fund revenues of $34.61 billion through March 31, 2024 are $578 million (1.7 percent) above the prorated annual estimate of $34.03 billion. The Independent Fiscal Office's March 2024 revenue update, released March 14, 2024, revised the full-year revenue estimate upward by $310 million to $46.41 billion, citing stronger-than-expected PIT withholding and CNIT performance.

Year-to-date expenditures of $36.53 billion are $842 million (2.4 percent) above the prorated budget, driven primarily by Medicaid overruns and the retroactive wage payments described above. The Office of the Budget projects that full-year expenditures will total approximately $47.2 billion, approximately $1.1 billion above the enacted budget of $46.1 billion. The projected year-end shortfall of approximately $790 million (after accounting for the revised revenue estimate) is expected to be covered by the existing fund balance and, if necessary, a partial draw from the Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund.

SECTION 5: NOTABLE ITEMS AND RISK FACTORS

Federal Revenue Uncertainty: The Commonwealth received $218.4 million in enhanced Federal Medical Assistance Percentage (FMAP) funds in Q3. Congressional discussions regarding potential modifications to Medicaid matching rates represent a downside risk to Q4 projections estimated at between $80 million and $140 million.

Pension Obligations: The State Employees' Retirement System (SERS) and Public School Employees' Retirement System (PSERS) combined employer contribution payments of $1.84 billion are scheduled for Q4, representing the Commonwealth's largest single quarterly payment obligation of the fiscal year.

Tax Amnesty Program: The Department of Revenue's 60-day tax amnesty program, which ran from February 1 through April 1, 2024, collected $187.3 million in delinquent taxes and penalties, exceeding the $145 million projection by 29.2 percent. Approximately $94 million of these collections were received in Q3, with the remainder to be recorded in Q4.
