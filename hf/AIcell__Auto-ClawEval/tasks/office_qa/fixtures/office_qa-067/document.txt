EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania recorded total General Fund revenues of $11.84 billion in the third quarter of fiscal year 2023-2024 (Q3 FY2024), representing a quarter-over-quarter increase of $1.23 billion, or 11.6 percent, compared to the $10.61 billion collected in the second quarter (Q2 FY2024). Total General Fund expenditures for Q3 FY2024 reached $12.07 billion, a decrease of $340 million, or 2.7 percent, from the $12.41 billion recorded in Q2 FY2024. The resulting quarterly deficit narrowed from $1.80 billion in Q2 FY2024 to $230 million in Q3 FY2024, a significant improvement driven primarily by stronger-than-anticipated personal income tax collections and a seasonal uptick in sales and use tax receipts.

Budget Secretary Uri Monson noted in a statement accompanying this report that the improvement reflects "continued economic resilience in Pennsylvania's labor market and consumer spending environment, though the Commonwealth remains attentive to softening indicators in manufacturing and commercial real estate sectors."

1.2 Personal Income Tax

Personal income tax (PIT) collections of $4.40 billion in Q3 FY2024 represent the largest single-quarter PIT collection since Q3 FY2022, when $4.61 billion was recorded. The 15.5 percent quarter-over-quarter increase is attributable to three primary factors:

- Employer withholding grew by $312.4 million (9.8%) compared to Q2 FY2024, consistent with Pennsylvania's unemployment rate declining from 3.7 percent in December 2023 to 3.4 percent in March 2024.
- Quarterly estimated payments increased by $188.6 million (22.3%), reflecting strong pass-through business income reported by S-corporations and partnerships.
- Final returns and extensions contributed $88.3 million more than in Q2 FY2024, as the March 15 corporate filing deadline fell within the quarter.

1.3 Sales and Use Tax

Sales and use tax collections of $3.39 billion exceeded Q2 FY2024 collections by $284.6 million. January 2024 alone generated $1.09 billion in sales tax receipts, the highest single-month total since January 2023. Post-holiday retail activity and increased restaurant and hospitality spending contributed to the improvement. Online marketplace facilitator collections, which became fully operational under Act 43 of 2017, contributed $441.2 million in Q3 FY2024, up from $398.7 million in Q2 FY2024.

1.4 Corporate Net Income Tax

Corporate net income tax (CNIT) collections of $1.04 billion reflect continued profitability in Pennsylvania's financial services and technology sectors. The CNIT rate reduction from 8.99 percent to 8.49 percent, effective January 1, 2024 under Act 53 of 2022's phased schedule, modestly constrained growth. Without the rate reduction, the Office of the Budget estimates CNIT collections would have been approximately $62 million higher.

1.5 Realty Transfer Tax

The 16.5 percent decline in realty transfer tax collections, from $143.2 million to $119.6 million, reflects continued weakness in Pennsylvania's residential real estate market. The Pennsylvania Association of Realtors reported that existing home sales in the Commonwealth fell 8.3 percent quarter-over-quarter, with the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate averaging 6.82 percent during Q3 FY2024.

2.2 Human Services

The Department of Human Services (DHS) expenditures of $4.09 billion in Q3 FY2024 declined by $128.6 million from Q2 FY2024. The primary driver was a $97.4 million reduction in Medical Assistance (Medicaid) managed care capitation payments, resulting from the completion of the annual rate reconciliation process in December 2023. Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) administrative costs declined by $18.2 million following the expiration of enhanced federal administrative matching funds on December 31, 2023.

2.3 Education

PreK-12 education expenditures declined by $186.2 million (6.2%) quarter-over-quarter. This reflects the timing of basic education subsidy payments to school districts. Under the Commonwealth's payment schedule, Q2 FY2024 included an accelerated payment of $204.7 million to 87 school districts that had requested early disbursement under the provisions of the School Code. No comparable accelerated payments occurred in Q3 FY2024.

2.4 Debt Service

Debt service expenditures increased sharply by $257.2 million (40.6%), from $634.1 million in Q2 FY2024 to $891.3 million in Q3 FY2024. This increase is consistent with the Commonwealth's debt service calendar, as February and March represent peak bond payment months. Pennsylvania's outstanding General Obligation debt as of March 31, 2024 stood at $10.87 billion, a reduction of $412.3 million from the $11.28 billion outstanding as of June 30, 2023, reflecting the Commonwealth's continued commitment to debt reduction under the Governor's five-year capital plan.

SECTION 3: FUND BALANCE AND LIQUIDITY

As of March 31, 2024, the General Fund unappropriated balance stood at $1.94 billion, compared to $2.17 billion as of December 31, 2023. The $230 million quarterly deficit was partially offset by $3.2 million in investment earnings on fund balances held with the State Treasury.

The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund) maintained its balance of $5.07 billion as of March 31, 2024, unchanged from December 31, 2023. No transfers to or from the Rainy Day Fund occurred during Q3 FY2024. Pennsylvania's Rainy Day Fund balance represents approximately 10.3 percent of estimated annual General Fund revenues, which remains above the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) recommended minimum of two months' operating expenditures.

SECTION 4: YEAR-TO-DATE PERFORMANCE VERSUS ENACTED BUDGET

Through the first three quarters of FY2023-2024 (July 1, 2023 through March 31, 2024), total General Fund revenues of $31.96 billion represent 74.8 percent of the $42.73 billion annual revenue estimate contained in the FY2023-2024 Enacted Budget (Act 1A of 2023). Total expenditures of $35.22 billion through Q3 represent 77.4 percent of the $45.51 billion appropriated for the full fiscal year.

The year-to-date revenue shortfall relative to the prorated budget target is $487 million, or 1.5 percent below the three-quarter benchmark. The Office of the Budget projects that full-year revenues will come in approximately $310 million below the enacted estimate, primarily due to weaker-than-projected corporate net income tax collections in Q1 FY2024 and the realty transfer tax underperformance noted above.

SECTION 5: OUTLOOK FOR Q4 FY2024 (APRIL 1 – JUNE 30, 2024)

The fourth quarter historically represents the Commonwealth's strongest revenue quarter due to the April 15 personal income tax filing deadline. The Office of the Budget projects Q4 FY2024 General Fund revenues of $13.2 billion to $13.6 billion, which would bring full-year revenues to between $45.16 billion and $45.56 billion against the enacted estimate of $42.73 billion — though this projection incorporates the anticipated $2.43 billion in PIT final return payments expected to be processed between April 1 and April 30, 2024.

Key risks to the Q4 outlook include potential volatility in capital gains realizations, which contributed an estimated $618 million to PIT collections in Q4 FY2023, and uncertainty surrounding federal Medicaid reimbursement rates pending the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services' final rule expected in May 2024.
