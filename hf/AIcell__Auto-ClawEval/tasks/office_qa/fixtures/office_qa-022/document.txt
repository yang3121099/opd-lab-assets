EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comparative analysis of departmental appropriations and actual expenditures for the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania across fiscal years 2022–2023 and 2023–2024. The Commonwealth's total general fund budget for FY2023–2024 was $45.4 billion, representing an increase of $2.1 billion, or 4.85 percent, over the FY2022–2023 enacted budget of $43.3 billion. This document was prepared under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and reflects data certified by the Office of the Comptroller as of September 30, 2024.

Overall, 11 of the 17 major departments reviewed came in at or below their appropriated spending levels. Six departments exceeded their initial appropriations, with the Department of Human Services recording the largest dollar variance. The Commonwealth ended FY2023–2024 with a general fund surplus of approximately $612 million, compared to a surplus of $1.04 billion at the close of FY2022–2023.

SECTION 1: METHODOLOGY AND SCOPE

Data for this report was compiled from the Commonwealth's SAP-based financial management system, the Integrated Enterprise System (IES), and cross-referenced with quarterly expenditure reports submitted by each department's Chief Financial Officer. Appropriation figures reflect enacted budgets signed into law on July 7, 2023, for FY2023–2024 and July 8, 2022, for FY2022–2023. Supplemental appropriations approved by the General Assembly during each fiscal year are incorporated into the "Adjusted Appropriation" column.

All figures are reported in thousands of dollars unless otherwise noted. Percentages are rounded to two decimal places. Departments with total appropriations below $50 million are grouped under the "Independent Agencies and Smaller Departments" category in Section 4.

SECTION 3: NOTABLE DEPARTMENTAL FINDINGS

3.1 Department of Human Services

The Department of Human Services (DHS), led by Secretary Dr. Val Arkoosh, recorded the largest budget overrun of any Commonwealth department in FY2023–2024, spending $17.891 billion against an appropriation of $17.614 billion. The $277.45 million overage was driven primarily by a 6.3 percent increase in Medical Assistance enrollment, which reached 3.47 million recipients by June 30, 2024, compared to 3.26 million at the same point in FY2022–2023. Behavioral health services expenditures rose by $114 million year-over-year, reflecting expanded crisis intervention programming authorized under Act 54 of 2023. DHS submitted a supplemental appropriation request of $310 million on March 14, 2024, of which $265 million was approved by the General Assembly on April 29, 2024.

3.2 Department of Education

The Department of Education, under Secretary Dr. Khalid Mumin, came in $22.8 million under its $8.512 billion appropriation, finishing at $8.489 billion. The underspend was attributable largely to 214 unfilled administrative positions as of June 30, 2024, and delayed rollout of the new statewide student data platform, originally scheduled for January 2024 but pushed to September 2024. Basic Education Funding distributed to school districts totaled $7.83 billion, an increase of $841 million over FY2022–2023 and the largest single-year increase in the program's history, reflecting the General Assembly's response to the February 2023 Commonwealth Court ruling in William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education.

3.3 Department of Transportation

PennDOT's actual expenditures of $5.302 billion came in $86.25 million below its $5.388 billion appropriation, a variance of negative 1.60 percent. The department attributed the underspend to supply chain delays affecting 23 active highway construction projects, which pushed an estimated $91 million in contractor payments into FY2024–2025. Motor License Fund revenues of $4.14 billion supported the majority of PennDOT's operations, with the general fund contribution accounting for $1.16 billion of the total appropriation.

3.4 Department of Corrections

The Department of Corrections, under Secretary Dr. Laurel Harry, exceeded its appropriation by $37.9 million, or 1.25 percent. The average daily inmate population for FY2023–2024 was 37,842, compared to 36,914 in FY2022–2023, an increase of 928 individuals or 2.51 percent. Healthcare costs within the correctional system rose 8.7 percent year-over-year, reaching $612 million, driven by increased pharmaceutical costs and expanded mental health treatment capacity at SCI Graterford and SCI Muncy.

3.5 Pennsylvania State Police

The Pennsylvania State Police exceeded its $1.589 billion appropriation by $12.2 million, finishing at $1.601 billion. Overtime expenditures totaled $87.4 million, exceeding the budgeted overtime allocation of $71.0 million by $16.4 million. This was partially offset by savings in equipment procurement. Trooper headcount as of June 30, 2024, stood at 4,612, compared to 4,588 at the same date in FY2022–2023.

3.6 Department of Environmental Protection

DEP finished $11.2 million under its $521 million appropriation, a variance of negative 2.15 percent. The department processed 8,743 environmental permits during FY2023–2024, a 4.1 percent increase over the prior year's 8,399. Federal grant receipts of $143.6 million, including $38.2 million from the Inflation Reduction Act environmental justice programs, supplemented the general fund appropriation.

SECTION 4: INDEPENDENT AGENCIES AND SMALLER DEPARTMENTS

Seventeen smaller agencies with appropriations below $50 million collectively spent $487.3 million against a combined appropriation of $501.2 million, resulting in an aggregate underspend of $13.9 million, or 2.77 percent. The Pennsylvania Commission on Crime and Delinquency was the largest spender in this category at $48.7 million. The Insurance Department recorded the smallest variance in this group, spending $31.2 million against an appropriation of $31.3 million.

SECTION 5: YEAR-OVER-YEAR BUDGET GROWTH ANALYSIS

Total general fund expenditures grew from $42.26 billion in FY2022–2023 to $44.79 billion in FY2023–2024, an increase of $2.53 billion or 5.99 percent. The five fastest-growing departments by percentage increase in actual expenditures were:

- Department of Human Services: +6.23%
- Department of Education: +4.76%
- Pennsylvania State Police: +5.87%
- Department of Health: +6.50%
- Department of Military & Veterans Affairs: +5.53%

Personnel costs across all departments totaled $9.84 billion in FY2023–2024, representing 21.97 percent of total general fund expenditures. This compares to $9.31 billion, or 22.03 percent of expenditures, in FY2022–2023. The Commonwealth employed 79,412 full-time equivalent positions as of June 30, 2024, compared to 78,906 on June 30, 2023.

SECTION 6: FUND BALANCE AND FISCAL OUTLOOK

The Commonwealth's unappropriated general fund balance as of June 30, 2024, was $612.4 million. The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund, commonly referred to as the Rainy Day Fund, held a balance of $1.83 billion as of the same date, equivalent to approximately 4.09 percent of FY2023–2024 general fund expenditures. The Government Finance Officers Association recommends a minimum reserve of 5 to 15 percent of annual expenditures, indicating that Pennsylvania's reserve remains below the recommended threshold.

Revenue collections for FY2023–2024 totaled $45.40 billion, exceeding the official estimate of $44.97 billion by $430 million. Personal income tax collections of $16.82 billion and sales and use tax collections of $12.34 billion were the two largest revenue sources, together accounting for 64.0 percent of total general fund revenues.

SECTION 7: RECOMMENDATIONS

The Bureau of Financial Management and Analysis offers the following recommendations for the FY2024–2025 budget cycle:

1. The Department of Human Services should submit quarterly Medical Assistance enrollment projections no later than the 15th day following each quarter's close to enable earlier identification of potential overruns.

2. PennDOT should coordinate with the Office of the Budget to establish a construction payment reserve account of no less than $75 million to absorb supply chain-related payment timing variances.

3. The Department of Corrections should present a multi-year healthcare cost containment plan to the General Assembly's Appropriations Committees by February 1, 2025.

4. All departments with personnel vacancy rates exceeding 8 percent as of December 31, 2024, should submit workforce recruitment plans to the Office of Administration by March 15, 2025.
