EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of Commonwealth expenditure patterns across six fiscal years, from FY2018–19 through FY2023–24. Total general fund expenditures grew from $32.7 billion in FY2018–19 to $45.3 billion in FY2023–24, representing a cumulative increase of 38.5 percent over the five-year period. The average annual growth rate was 6.7 percent, exceeding the average Pennsylvania Consumer Price Index increase of 4.1 percent over the same period. This report was prepared by the Bureau of Financial Analysis under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and is intended to inform the Governor's Office, the General Assembly, and the public regarding long-term fiscal sustainability.

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF TOTAL EXPENDITURES

Pennsylvania's general fund expenditures have expanded substantially since FY2018–19, driven primarily by growth in human services, education, and debt service obligations. The following table summarizes total general fund spending by fiscal year:

Fiscal Year | Total Expenditures | Year-over-Year Change | % Change
FY2018–19   | $32.7 billion      | —                     | —
FY2019–20   | $34.1 billion      | +$1.4 billion         | +4.3%
FY2020–21   | $35.8 billion      | +$1.7 billion         | +5.0%
FY2021–22   | $39.2 billion      | +$3.4 billion         | +9.5%
FY2022–23   | $42.8 billion      | +$3.6 billion         | +9.2%
FY2023–24   | $45.3 billion      | +$2.5 billion         | +5.8%

The spike observed in FY2021–22 reflects the accelerated deployment of American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds totaling $7.3 billion allocated to Pennsylvania, a portion of which flowed through the general fund for eligible expenditure categories including broadband infrastructure, housing assistance, and public health response.

SECTION 2: EXPENDITURE BY MAJOR CATEGORY

2.1 Human Services

The Department of Human Services (DHS) remains the single largest spending category in the Commonwealth budget. DHS expenditures rose from $13.1 billion in FY2018–19 to $18.6 billion in FY2023–24, an increase of 42.0 percent. Medicaid spending alone accounted for $12.4 billion in FY2023–24, representing 27.4 percent of total general fund expenditures. The Medical Assistance program served approximately 3.4 million Pennsylvanians as of June 30, 2024, up from 2.8 million in June 2019, a 21.4 percent increase in enrollment driven in part by continuous enrollment protections that remained in effect through April 2023 under federal pandemic-era policy.

Long-term care expenditures within DHS reached $4.1 billion in FY2023–24, with nursing facility payments comprising $2.7 billion of that total. The Community HealthChoices managed care program, which serves individuals with physical disabilities and older adults, expanded its enrollment to 490,000 participants statewide by the close of FY2023–24.

2.2 Education

Total education spending, encompassing the Department of Education and the Pennsylvania Higher Education Assistance Agency (PHEAA), reached $14.9 billion in FY2023–24, compared to $11.2 billion in FY2018–19, a 33.0 percent increase. Basic education funding, the largest single line item within the education category, grew from $6.1 billion to $8.0 billion over the same period. This growth includes the $567 million increase enacted in the FY2023–24 budget signed by Governor Josh Shapiro on August 3, 2023, which represented the largest single-year increase in basic education funding in Pennsylvania history.

Special education funding increased from $1.1 billion in FY2018–19 to $1.45 billion in FY2023–24. Higher education appropriations to the State System of Higher Education (PASSHE), which comprises 10 universities, totaled $477 million in FY2023–24, a modest 3.2 percent increase from the $462 million appropriated in FY2018–19.

2.3 Corrections and Public Safety

The Department of Corrections (DOC) expended $2.9 billion in FY2023–24, compared to $2.4 billion in FY2018–19, a 20.8 percent increase. The average daily inmate population declined from 47,800 in FY2018–19 to 37,200 in FY2023–24, a reduction of 22.2 percent. Despite this population decrease, per-inmate costs rose from approximately $50,200 annually in FY2018–19 to $77,900 in FY2023–24, driven by staffing shortages, increased healthcare costs within the correctional system, and facility maintenance backlogs estimated at $1.2 billion statewide.

2.4 Debt Service

General obligation debt service expenditures totaled $1.07 billion in FY2023–24, compared to $985 million in FY2018–19, an increase of 8.6 percent. The Commonwealth's total outstanding general obligation debt stood at $10.8 billion as of June 30, 2024. Moody's Investors Service affirmed Pennsylvania's Aa3 bond rating in March 2024, citing improved reserve fund balances and structural budget improvements, while noting continued pension liability as a long-term risk factor.

SECTION 3: PENSION EXPENDITURES AND LONG-TERM OBLIGATIONS

Pennsylvania's two primary public pension systems, the Public School Employees' Retirement System (PSERS) and the State Employees' Retirement System (SERS), continue to represent a significant fiscal pressure. Combined employer contributions from the general fund totaled $4.8 billion in FY2023–24, compared to $4.1 billion in FY2018–19.

As of December 31, 2023, PSERS reported a funded ratio of 68.4 percent, with unfunded actuarial accrued liabilities of $44.2 billion. SERS reported a funded ratio of 73.1 percent, with unfunded liabilities of $14.6 billion. The combined unfunded pension liability of $58.8 billion represents the Commonwealth's most significant long-term fiscal challenge.

Act 5 of 2017, which established a hybrid pension plan for new state and school employees hired after January 1, 2019, is projected to reduce long-term pension costs by approximately $15 billion over a 30-year horizon, according to actuarial analysis conducted by Milliman, Inc. However, the full fiscal benefit of this reform will not materialize until the mid-2040s.

SECTION 4: RESERVE FUND POSITION

The Commonwealth's Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund, commonly referred to as the Rainy Day Fund, held a balance of $6.1 billion as of June 30, 2024. This represents a dramatic improvement from the $29.5 million balance recorded on June 30, 2019. The fund's growth was enabled by consecutive years of revenue outperformance relative to official estimates, with FY2021–22 alone producing a $2.9 billion surplus driven by stronger-than-anticipated personal income tax and corporate net income tax collections.

The $6.1 billion Rainy Day Fund balance equals approximately 13.5 percent of FY2023–24 general fund expenditures, placing Pennsylvania above the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) recommended minimum of two months of operating expenditures, or approximately 16.7 percent. The Office of the Budget projects that maintaining current deposit policies will bring the fund to $7.4 billion by June 30, 2026.

SECTION 5: EXPENDITURE EFFICIENCY INDICATORS

The Office of the Budget tracks several efficiency metrics to assess the relationship between expenditure growth and service delivery outcomes.

- Cost per Medicaid enrollee: $3,647 in FY2023–24, compared to $4,679 in FY2018–19, a 22.0 percent decrease attributable to managed care expansion and value-based payment reforms.

- Per-pupil basic education expenditure (state share): $6,154 in FY2023–24, compared to $4,878 in FY2018–19, a 26.2 percent increase.

- Administrative overhead as a percentage of total DHS expenditures: 4.2 percent in FY2023–24, down from 5.1 percent in FY2018–19, reflecting consolidation of county assistance office operations and expanded use of the COMPASS online benefits portal, which processed 2.1 million applications in FY2023–24.

SECTION 6: FIVE-YEAR EXPENDITURE OUTLOOK

Based on current program enrollment trends, contractual obligations, and legislative commitments, the Office of the Budget projects general fund expenditures will reach $52.7 billion by FY2028–29, representing a 16.3 percent increase from FY2023–24 levels. Key cost drivers over the projection period include:

- Medicaid enrollment normalization following post-pandemic redeterminations, with net enrollment projected to stabilize at approximately 3.1 million by FY2025–26.
- Continued growth in long-term care costs at an estimated 4.8 percent annually.
- Basic education funding commitments under the adequacy framework established following the William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education ruling issued by Commonwealth Court on February 7, 2023.
- Debt service on new capital borrowing authorized under the Redevelopment Assistance Capital Program (RACP), which carries a current authorization ceiling of $5.75 billion.

CONCLUSION

Pennsylvania's expenditure trajectory over the FY2019–2024 period reflects both deliberate policy investments and structural cost pressures. The 38.5 percent cumulative growth in general fund spending has been accompanied by meaningful improvements in reserve fund strength and select efficiency metrics. However, the $58.8 billion combined pension liability, rising per-inmate costs, and projected Medicaid growth require sustained fiscal discipline. The Office of the Budget recommends continued adherence to the Rainy Day Fund deposit schedule, acceleration of pension liability reduction strategies, and rigorous program evaluation across all major spending categories.
