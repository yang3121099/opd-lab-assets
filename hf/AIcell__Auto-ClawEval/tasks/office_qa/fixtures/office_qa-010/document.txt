EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of Commonwealth expenditure patterns across major budget categories for Fiscal Year 2023-2024 (July 1, 2023 through June 30, 2024), with comparative data extending to FY 2019-2020. Total general fund expenditures for FY 2023-2024 reached $42.8 billion, representing a 6.3 percent increase over the FY 2022-2023 total of $40.3 billion. This report was prepared under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and is intended to inform legislative appropriations deliberations for FY 2025-2026.

SECTION 1: FIVE-YEAR EXPENDITURE OVERVIEW

Total general fund spending has grown by 28.7 percent over the five-year period from FY 2019-2020 through FY 2023-2024. The following table summarizes aggregate expenditures by fiscal year:

Fiscal Year     Total Expenditures     Year-over-Year Change
FY 2019-2020    $33.3 billion          —
FY 2020-2021    $35.1 billion          +5.4%
FY 2021-2022    $38.6 billion          +10.0%
FY 2022-2023    $40.3 billion          +4.4%
FY 2023-2024    $42.8 billion          +6.3%

The spike observed in FY 2021-2022 reflects the drawdown of federal American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds totaling $7.3 billion allocated to Pennsylvania, which temporarily inflated program-level spending across health, education, and infrastructure categories. Excluding ARPA-related expenditures, the adjusted compound annual growth rate for the five-year period is 4.1 percent.

SECTION 2: EXPENDITURE BY MAJOR CATEGORY — FY 2023-2024

2.1 Health and Human Services

Health and Human Services (HHS) remains the single largest expenditure category, accounting for $18.9 billion or 44.2 percent of total general fund spending in FY 2023-2024. This represents an increase of $1.4 billion (8.0 percent) over the FY 2022-2023 HHS total of $17.5 billion.

Key drivers within HHS include:

- Medical Assistance (Medicaid): $12.6 billion, up 9.1 percent from $11.5 billion in FY 2022-2023. Enrollment as of June 30, 2024 stood at 3.47 million individuals, compared to 3.31 million one year prior.
- Children and Youth Services: $1.8 billion, reflecting a 4.7 percent increase driven by expanded foster care placements and the implementation of Act 64 of 2023, which extended child welfare services to individuals up to age 21.
- Mental Health and Substance Use Disorder Programs: $1.2 billion, up 14.3 percent, consistent with the Governor's opioid response initiative announced in February 2023 and funded through a combination of state appropriations and the $1.07 billion Pennsylvania opioid settlement fund.
- Long-Term Care Services: $2.1 billion, reflecting a 5.0 percent increase attributable to rising nursing facility per-diem rates, which averaged $287 per day in FY 2023-2024 compared to $271 per day in FY 2022-2023.

2.2 Education

Education expenditures totaled $14.1 billion in FY 2023-2024, representing 33.0 percent of general fund spending and an increase of 7.6 percent over the FY 2022-2023 figure of $13.1 billion.

- Basic Education Funding (BEF): $8.2 billion, an increase of $567 million (7.4 percent) over the prior year. This increase reflects the first year of implementation following the Commonwealth Court's ruling in William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education (January 2023), which found the existing school funding system unconstitutional. The BEF increase represents the largest single-year increase in the program's history.
- Special Education: $1.4 billion, up 5.3 percent.
- Higher Education (State System of Higher Education and state-related universities): $1.9 billion, essentially flat at a 0.5 percent increase, reflecting continued enrollment pressures at institutions including Cheyney University, which received a targeted stabilization grant of $14.2 million in FY 2023-2024.
- Early Childhood Education (Pre-K Counts and Head Start Supplemental): $425 million, up 11.8 percent, serving an estimated 52,400 children statewide.

2.3 Transportation

The Department of Transportation (PennDOT) received $4.6 billion in combined state and federal pass-through expenditures in FY 2023-2024, a 3.2 percent increase over the prior year's $4.46 billion. State-only transportation expenditures from the Motor License Fund totaled $2.9 billion. Capital program obligations under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) accounted for $1.1 billion of federal expenditures, with projects concentrated on bridge rehabilitation (412 structures addressed in FY 2023-2024) and Interstate highway resurfacing covering 847 lane-miles.

2.4 Public Safety and Corrections

The Department of Corrections operated with a budget of $2.8 billion in FY 2023-2024, a modest 1.9 percent increase over the prior year. The average daily inmate population declined to 37,842, down from 38,614 in FY 2022-2023, continuing a trend that has reduced the population by approximately 14 percent from the pre-pandemic peak of 44,013 recorded in FY 2018-2019. The average annual cost per inmate rose to $73,980, compared to $69,450 in FY 2022-2023, a 6.5 percent increase driven primarily by healthcare and staffing costs.

The Pennsylvania State Police received $1.6 billion, a 5.3 percent increase, reflecting a cadet class expansion that added 312 new troopers during the fiscal year.

2.5 General Government Operations

General government operations, including the Governor's Office, Office of Administration, and Treasury functions, totaled $1.4 billion, representing 3.3 percent of general fund expenditures and a 2.1 percent increase over FY 2022-2023.

SECTION 3: DEBT SERVICE AND PENSION OBLIGATIONS

Debt service on general obligation bonds totaled $1.03 billion in FY 2023-2024, a decrease of 3.7 percent from $1.07 billion in FY 2022-2023, reflecting the retirement of older bond series and favorable refinancing completed in November 2022 that generated $84 million in present-value savings.

Pension contributions to the State Employees' Retirement System (SERS) and the Public School Employees' Retirement System (PSERS) combined for $4.2 billion in FY 2023-2024. PSERS contributions alone totaled $3.1 billion, with the employer contribution rate set at 34.94 percent of payroll for FY 2023-2024. The funded ratio for PSERS as of December 31, 2023 stood at 57.8 percent, while SERS reported a funded ratio of 67.3 percent as of the same date. Both systems continue to operate below the 80 percent threshold generally considered actuarially sound.

SECTION 4: NOTABLE EXPENDITURE TRENDS AND RISK FACTORS

4.1 Medicaid Unwinding Impact

The conclusion of the federal continuous enrollment requirement under the Families First Coronavirus Response Act resulted in 487,000 Pennsylvanians being disenrolled from Medicaid between April 2023 and June 2024. Despite this reduction, per-enrollee costs increased 11.4 percent, partially offsetting enrollment savings and contributing to the overall Medicaid cost increase noted in Section 2.1.

4.2 Personnel Costs

Total personnel costs across all agencies reached $9.3 billion in FY 2023-2024, a 7.8 percent increase over the prior year. This increase reflects the implementation of the collective bargaining agreement ratified in March 2023 covering approximately 67,000 AFSCME-represented state employees, which included a 5.0 percent base wage increase in the first year and a 3.5 percent increase in the second year.

4.3 Inflation Adjustment

Using the Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI-U index, real (inflation-adjusted) expenditure growth in FY 2023-2024 was 2.9 percent, as the 6.3 percent nominal increase was partially offset by a 3.4 percent average annual inflation rate for the period.

SECTION 5: OUTLOOK FOR FY 2024-2025

The Governor's proposed budget for FY 2024-2025, submitted to the General Assembly on February 6, 2024, projects total general fund expenditures of $48.3 billion, a 12.9 percent increase over FY 2023-2024 actuals. The proposed increase is concentrated in Basic Education Funding ($1.1 billion additional) and Medicaid ($900 million additional). The Independent Fiscal Office projects a structural deficit of approximately $3.4 billion in FY 2024-2025 absent new revenue measures or expenditure reductions, based on projected revenue growth of 3.2 percent against the proposed spending trajectory.

SECTION 6: METHODOLOGY AND DATA SOURCES

Expenditure data in this report is drawn from the Commonwealth's SAP-based financial management system (SMART) as of August 31, 2024, following the close of the FY 2023-2024 fiscal year. All figures represent actual obligations and disbursements unless otherwise noted. Federal pass-through expenditures are included in agency totals where the Commonwealth serves as the primary recipient and administrator. Figures may not sum precisely due to rounding. Prior-year figures have been restated in two instances to reflect audit adjustments issued by the Auditor General's Office in July 2024.
