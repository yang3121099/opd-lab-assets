EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The United States federal government collected $4.44 trillion in total tax revenues during Fiscal Year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023), representing a decline of 9.3 percent from the record $4.90 trillion collected in Fiscal Year 2022. This report, prepared by the Office of Revenue Analysis under Deputy Commissioner Douglas O'Donnell, provides a comprehensive breakdown of revenue streams, year-over-year comparisons, and contributing economic factors that shaped collection outcomes across all major tax categories.

Despite the overall decline, individual income tax receipts remained the dominant revenue source, and payroll tax collections reached their highest nominal level in recorded history. The decline was primarily attributable to reduced capital gains realizations, lower corporate profits, and the expiration of pandemic-era tax provisions that had temporarily elevated collections in FY2022.

SECTION 1: TOTAL REVENUE OVERVIEW

Total federal tax receipts for FY2023 were $4.439 trillion, compared to $4.896 trillion in FY2022 and $4.047 trillion in FY2021. The three-year compound annual growth rate from FY2019 through FY2023 stands at 7.2 percent, reflecting the significant revenue expansion that followed the economic recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic.

Federal outlays for FY2023 totaled $6.134 trillion, producing a budget deficit of $1.695 trillion — an increase of $320 billion over the FY2022 deficit of $1.375 trillion. The deficit-to-GDP ratio rose to 6.3 percent in FY2023, up from 5.4 percent in FY2022.

SECTION 2: INDIVIDUAL INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Individual income taxes constituted 49.0 percent of total federal revenues in FY2023, down from 53.8 percent in FY2022. The $455.6 billion decline represents the largest single-year nominal drop in individual income tax receipts since the Great Recession year of FY2009, when collections fell by $431 billion.

Key contributing factors include:

- Capital gains realizations fell sharply as equity markets declined. The S&P 500 index lost approximately 19.4 percent of its value during calendar year 2022, reducing taxable gains reported on FY2023 returns. Estimated capital gains tax revenue declined by approximately $180 billion year-over-year.

- Withholding tax receipts, which reflect wages and salaries, actually increased by 4.1 percent to $1,847.3 billion, consistent with continued labor market strength. The national unemployment rate averaged 3.6 percent during FY2023.

- Non-withheld payments (estimated taxes and final payments) fell by 38.2 percent, from $784.9 billion in FY2022 to $484.8 billion in FY2023, driven primarily by the capital gains reversal and lower pass-through business income.

- Refunds issued to individual taxpayers totaled $334.8 billion in FY2023, an increase of $14.2 billion over FY2022 refunds of $320.6 billion.

The top marginal individual income tax rate remained at 37 percent throughout FY2023, applicable to taxable income above $578,125 for single filers and $693,750 for married couples filing jointly, as adjusted for inflation under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017.

SECTION 3: PAYROLL TAX ANALYSIS

Payroll taxes — comprising Social Security (OASDI) and Medicare (HI) contributions — reached $1.6138 trillion in FY2023, the highest level ever recorded and an increase of $130.2 billion (8.8 percent) over FY2022. This growth reflects:

- Wage growth averaging 4.4 percent across the private sector during the fiscal year, as reported by the Bureau of Labor Statistics Employment Cost Index.

- The Social Security taxable wage base increased from $147,000 in calendar year 2022 to $160,200 in calendar year 2023, a 9.0 percent increase — the largest single-year adjustment since 1983.

- Total employment subject to FICA taxes averaged 161.4 million workers during FY2023.

Social Security (OASDI) contributions accounted for $1,234.7 billion of total payroll receipts, while Medicare (HI) contributions totaled $379.1 billion. The employee and employer OASDI rate each remained at 6.2 percent, and the Medicare rate remained at 1.45 percent each, with the Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9 percent applying to wages above $200,000 for individual filers.

SECTION 4: CORPORATE INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Corporate income tax receipts totaled $419.7 billion in FY2023, a modest decline of $5.2 billion (1.2 percent) from FY2022's $424.9 billion. The relative stability of corporate receipts contrasts with the sharp individual income tax decline and reflects several offsetting dynamics:

- The statutory corporate income tax rate of 21 percent, established by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, remained unchanged.

- The new 15 percent Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT), enacted as part of the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 and effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2022, contributed an estimated $4.8 billion in additional receipts during FY2023 — its first partial year of implementation.

- The 1 percent excise tax on corporate stock buybacks, also enacted under the Inflation Reduction Act and effective January 1, 2023, generated approximately $2.35 billion in FY2023 receipts.

- Corporate refunds totaled $98.4 billion in FY2023, compared to $86.7 billion in FY2022.

The effective corporate tax rate across all C-corporations filing returns was estimated at 16.8 percent for tax year 2022 (the most recent year for which complete data is available), compared to 16.4 percent for tax year 2021.

SECTION 5: EXCISE TAXES AND OTHER RECEIPTS

Excise tax collections totaled $87.4 billion in FY2023, a decrease of $1.8 billion from FY2022. Major components included:

- Highway Trust Fund excise taxes (motor fuels): $41.2 billion
- Tobacco products: $12.1 billion
- Alcohol: $10.4 billion
- Air transportation: $16.8 billion
- Other excise taxes: $6.9 billion

Customs duties fell by $19.7 billion (19.7 percent) to $80.1 billion, reflecting reduced import volumes and the partial rollback of Section 301 tariffs on certain Chinese goods following trade negotiations conducted through the Office of the United States Trade Representative during calendar year 2022.

Estate and gift tax receipts declined to $34.0 billion from $37.0 billion in FY2022. The unified estate and gift tax exemption was $12.92 million per individual in calendar year 2023, up from $12.06 million in 2022.

SECTION 6: COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT METRICS

The IRS processed approximately 271.4 million tax returns and supplemental documents during FY2023. Individual income tax returns filed electronically accounted for 94.3 percent of all individual filings, up from 92.1 percent in FY2022.

The gross tax gap — defined as the difference between taxes legally owed and taxes voluntarily and timely paid — was most recently estimated at $688 billion annually (based on the IRS Tax Gap Estimates for Tax Years 2014–2016, updated in October 2021). The net tax gap, after enforcement and late payments, was estimated at $584 billion.

Enforcement revenue collected in FY2023 totaled $63.6 billion, including:

- Examination (audit) assessments: $30.2 billion
- Collection activities: $22.1 billion
- Document matching programs: $11.3 billion

The IRS audit rate for individual returns was 0.44 percent in FY2023, compared to 0.38 percent in FY2022, reflecting the initial deployment of resources funded by the $79.6 billion in supplemental IRS funding authorized under the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022.

SECTION 7: OUTLOOK FOR FISCAL YEAR 2024

The Congressional Budget Office January 2024 Budget and Economic Outlook projects total federal revenues of $4.664 trillion for FY2024, representing a projected increase of $225 billion (5.1 percent) over FY2023 actuals. This projection assumes:

- Continued labor market strength with unemployment remaining below 4.0 percent through September 2024
- Partial recovery in capital gains realizations as equity markets rebounded during calendar year 2023
- Full-year impact of the Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax
- Individual income tax bracket adjustments for inflation averaging 5.4 percent for tax year 2023

The Office of Tax Analysis within the Department of the Treasury will publish updated revenue projections in conjunction with the President's FY2025 Budget submission, expected on or around March 11, 2024.
