COMMONWEALTH OF PENNSYLVANIA
OFFICE OF THE BUDGET
QUARTERLY FINANCIAL REPORT
Third Quarter Fiscal Year 2023-2024 (January 1 – March 31, 2024)
Compared to Second Quarter Fiscal Year 2023-2024 (October 1 – December 31, 2023)

Published: April 22, 2024
Prepared by: Bureau of Financial Management, Secretary Uri Monson

---

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania concluded the third quarter of fiscal year 2023-2024 with General Fund revenues of $11.84 billion, representing a quarter-over-quarter increase of $1.27 billion, or 12.0 percent, compared to the $10.57 billion collected during the second quarter. Total General Fund expenditures for Q3 reached $10.93 billion, up from $10.41 billion in Q2, reflecting a 5.0 percent increase. The resulting quarterly surplus of $910 million in Q3 compares favorably to the $160 million surplus recorded in Q2, driven primarily by stronger-than-anticipated personal income tax receipts and improved corporate net income tax collections following the January 15, 2024 estimated payment deadline.

The Commonwealth's cumulative General Fund revenue through the first three quarters of fiscal year 2023-2024 stands at $32.18 billion, placing collections 2.3 percent above the official estimate of $31.45 billion established in the Governor's Executive Budget presented on February 6, 2024.

Personal Income Tax (PIT): The $796.2 million quarter-over-quarter increase in PIT collections reflects the January 15, 2024 fourth-quarter estimated payment deadline for calendar year 2023 filers. Withholding collections grew modestly by 3.1 percent, consistent with continued labor market stability. Pennsylvania's unemployment rate held at 3.4 percent in March 2024, according to data released by the Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry on April 18, 2024.

Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT): The 33.1 percent increase in CNIT collections from Q2 to Q3 is attributable to the January 15 estimated payment deadline and reflects improved corporate profitability across the financial services and energy sectors. Pennsylvania's CNIT rate was reduced from 8.99 percent to 8.49 percent effective January 1, 2024, as part of the phased reduction schedule enacted under Act 53 of 2022. Despite the rate reduction, collections exceeded the Q3 estimate of $1,312.0 million by $77.7 million.

Realty Transfer Tax: The 10.5 percent decline in Realty Transfer Tax collections from $147.3 million in Q2 to $131.8 million in Q3 reflects continued softness in Pennsylvania's residential real estate market. Existing home sales in the Commonwealth fell 6.2 percent year-over-year in the first quarter of 2024, consistent with national trends driven by mortgage rates averaging 6.82 percent during the quarter.

Non-Tax Revenue: The $71.4 million increase in non-tax revenue reflects higher-than-anticipated investment income from the Commonwealth's short-term investment pool, which benefited from the Federal Reserve's maintained benchmark rate of 5.25–5.50 percent throughout the quarter.

Department of Human Services (DHS): DHS expenditures increased by $171.5 million quarter-over-quarter, driven primarily by Medical Assistance (Medicaid) spending, which rose to $2,814.3 million in Q3 from $2,698.1 million in Q2. The increase reflects enrollment growth and the continued unwinding of the federal continuous enrollment requirement, which concluded in Pennsylvania on December 31, 2023. As of March 31, 2024, Pennsylvania's Medicaid enrollment stood at approximately 3.47 million individuals, down from a peak of 3.71 million in June 2023.

Department of Education: The $114.3 million increase in education spending reflects the timing of Basic Education Funding subsidy payments to school districts. Pennsylvania distributed $1,847.6 million in Basic Education Funding during Q3, compared to $1,733.3 million in Q2. The Q3 distribution included a supplemental payment of $88.4 million to 76 school districts identified as adequacy-deficient under the Commonwealth Court's February 2023 ruling in William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education.

Debt Service: General obligation debt service payments of $352.4 million in Q3 reflect the February 1, 2024 semi-annual interest payment on outstanding bonds. The Commonwealth's total outstanding general obligation debt as of March 31, 2024 was $10.84 billion, down from $11.12 billion one year prior, reflecting the administration's continued commitment to debt reduction.

SECTION 3: CASH POSITION AND FUND BALANCE

As of March 31, 2024, the General Fund unappropriated balance stood at $3.21 billion, compared to $2.30 billion at December 31, 2023. The $910 million improvement reflects the strong Q3 revenue performance described above.

The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund) maintained its balance of $1.83 billion throughout Q3, unchanged from Q2. No transfers into or out of the Rainy Day Fund occurred during the quarter. At $1.83 billion, the fund represents approximately 4.2 percent of projected annual General Fund expenditures, below the administration's stated target of 6.0 percent.

The Commonwealth's short-term borrowing activity during Q3 consisted of one Tax Revenue Anticipation Note (TRAN) issuance of $1.0 billion on January 4, 2024, which was fully repaid on March 28, 2024. The TRAN carried an interest rate of 3.87 percent and generated total interest costs of $9.7 million.

---

SECTION 4: YEAR-TO-DATE PERFORMANCE VERSUS ESTIMATE

Through the first three quarters of fiscal year 2023-2024:

- Cumulative General Fund revenue: $32.18 billion (2.3% above estimate)
- Cumulative General Fund expenditures: $31.27 billion (0.8% below estimate)
- Year-to-date surplus versus estimate: $730 million favorable

The Office of the Budget projects that full-year General Fund revenues will reach approximately $43.1 billion, approximately $650 million above the official estimate of $42.45 billion, assuming Q4 collections perform in line with historical seasonal patterns. This projection is subject to revision based on April 15 final income tax return payments, which represent the single largest revenue event of the fiscal year.

---

SECTION 5: NOTABLE RISKS AND OUTLOOK

Several factors may affect Q4 performance:

1. Federal Medicaid reimbursement rates are under review by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, and any reduction in the Federal Medical Assistance Percentage (FMAP) could increase Commonwealth costs by an estimated $180–240 million annually.

2. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court's pending decision in Mariner East Pipeline litigation could result in a one-time revenue impact of up to $125 million if the Commonwealth prevails on its royalty claims.

3. Consumer spending indicators suggest modest deceleration in Q4, which may constrain Sales and Use Tax growth to approximately 1.0–1.5 percent above Q3 levels rather than the 2.8 percent assumed in the official estimate.
