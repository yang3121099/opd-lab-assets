EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania's third quarter financial results for fiscal year 2023-2024 reflect continued revenue stabilization following the elevated collections of the post-pandemic period. Total General Fund revenues for the quarter ending March 31, 2024 reached $11.84 billion, representing a decrease of 2.3 percent compared to the second quarter (October 1 – December 31, 2023), which recorded $12.12 billion. Year-over-year, third quarter revenues declined 1.7 percent from the $12.05 billion collected in Q3 of fiscal year 2022-2023.

Total General Fund expenditures for Q3 FY2023-2024 were $12.31 billion, an increase of 4.1 percent over Q2 expenditures of $11.83 billion. The resulting quarterly deficit of $470 million was partially offset by the Commonwealth's Rainy Day Fund balance, which stood at $1.83 billion as of March 31, 2024.

Secretary of the Budget Uri Monson noted in the accompanying transmittal letter that the quarter's performance "reflects anticipated seasonal patterns in personal income tax collections and increased Medicaid expenditures consistent with enrollment projections approved in the Governor's Executive Budget."

Personal Income Tax (PIT): The $327.2 million quarter-over-quarter decline in PIT collections is attributable primarily to the seasonal reduction in quarterly estimated payments. Q2 typically captures the September 15 estimated payment deadline, while Q3 captures the January 15 deadline, which historically yields lower remittances. Withholding collections remained relatively stable, declining only 1.2 percent from $2,891.4 million in Q2 to $2,856.7 million in Q3.

Sales and Use Tax: The 5.9 percent increase reflects strong holiday retail activity captured in January remittances, as Pennsylvania's sales tax is remitted on a one-month lag for most vendors. December 2023 retail sales in Pennsylvania were estimated at $18.4 billion by the Department of Revenue's economic analysis unit, up 3.1 percent from December 2022.

Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT): The 17.7 percent decline represents the steepest quarter-over-quarter drop among major revenue categories. The Bureau of Revenue Analysis attributes this to the phase-down of the CNIT rate from 8.99 percent to 8.49 percent effective January 1, 2024, as mandated under Act 53 of 2022, combined with lower-than-projected corporate profitability in the manufacturing and financial services sectors.

Department of Human Services: The $453.5 million increase represents the largest quarter-over-quarter expenditure change and is driven almost entirely by Medicaid. Medical Assistance enrollment as of March 1, 2024 stood at 3,487,214 individuals, compared to 3,401,876 as of October 1, 2023, an increase of 85,338 enrollees. The average monthly per-enrollee cost rose to $512.40 in Q3 from $498.70 in Q2, reflecting increased utilization of behavioral health services and the implementation of enhanced rates for personal care home providers effective January 1, 2024.

Department of Transportation: The 10.6 percent decline reflects the seasonal reduction in highway maintenance and construction activity during winter months. PennDOT's capital program expenditures fell from $687.4 million in Q2 to $541.2 million in Q3, consistent with historical patterns.

SECTION III: YEAR-TO-DATE PERFORMANCE AND BUDGET COMPARISON

Through nine months of fiscal year 2023-2024 (July 1, 2023 – March 31, 2024):

- Total YTD General Fund revenues: $35,287.4 million
- Total YTD General Fund expenditures: $35,614.8 million
- YTD net position: -$327.4 million
- Revenues as a percentage of annual estimate: 74.8% (annual estimate: $47,174.0 million)
- Expenditures as a percentage of annual appropriation: 73.2% (total appropriation: $48,651.0 million)

The revenue shortfall relative to the annual estimate is 1.4 percentage points below the pace required to meet the full-year projection. The Independent Fiscal Office (IFO), in its March 2024 Revenue Update released March 14, 2024, revised its full-year General Fund revenue forecast downward by $412 million to $46,762 million, citing softening labor market conditions and the CNIT rate reduction.

SECTION IV: SPECIAL FUNDS AND FEDERAL RECEIPTS

Motor License Fund: Q3 revenues totaled $1,187.4 million, up 2.8 percent from Q2's $1,154.9 million. Liquid fuels tax collections of $487.3 million accounted for the largest share, reflecting stable vehicle miles traveled data from PennDOT's traffic monitoring system.

Lottery Fund: Net revenue available for older adult programs in Q3 was $284.7 million, a decrease of $9.3 million (3.2 percent) from Q2's $294.0 million. Instant ticket sales declined 4.1 percent quarter-over-quarter, while online game sales increased 1.7 percent.

Federal Funds: Total federal reimbursements received in Q3 reached $6,814.2 million, an increase of $398.7 million (6.2 percent) over Q2's $6,415.5 million. The increase is attributable to higher Medicaid Federal Medical Assistance Percentage (FMAP) reimbursements, with Pennsylvania's FMAP rate holding at 55.87 percent for the quarter.

SECTION V: CASH POSITION AND DEBT MANAGEMENT

General Fund Cash Balance: As of March 31, 2024, the General Fund maintained a cash balance of $2,147.6 million, compared to $2,891.3 million as of December 31, 2023. The $743.7 million reduction reflects the Q3 operating deficit and timing of federal reimbursements.

Rainy Day Fund: The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund balance remained at $1,832.7 million as of March 31, 2024, unchanged from December 31, 2023. No transfers were made to or from the fund during Q3. This balance represents approximately 3.9 percent of the FY2023-2024 General Fund appropriation.

Outstanding General Obligation Debt: As of March 31, 2024, the Commonwealth's outstanding GO debt totaled $10,847.3 million, a decrease of $214.6 million from the December 31, 2023 balance of $11,061.9 million, reflecting scheduled principal payments. The debt service coverage ratio for Q3 was 18.5x based on available revenues.

SECTION VI: OUTLOOK AND RISK FACTORS

The fourth quarter (April 1 – June 30, 2024) is historically the Commonwealth's strongest revenue quarter due to final personal income tax settlements and the April 15 filing deadline. The Bureau of Revenue Analysis projects Q4 General Fund revenues of approximately $13.1 billion, which would bring the full-year total to approximately $48.4 billion if realized—above the IFO's revised estimate but below the Administration's original projection.

Key risk factors identified by the Bureau include:

- Continued softening in corporate income tax collections if Pennsylvania-apportioned corporate profits decline further in Q4 filings
- Potential Medicaid expenditure pressure if the Department of Human Services' redetermination process results in fewer disenrollments than projected (current projection: 127,000 disenrollments by June 30, 2024)
- Interest rate sensitivity on the Commonwealth's $3.2 billion variable-rate debt portfolio
