EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Department of Defense received a total enacted budget of $886.3 billion for Fiscal Year 2024, representing a 3.2 percent increase over the FY2023 enacted level of $858.5 billion. This report provides a comprehensive analysis of defense appropriations, expenditure trends, program-level allocations, and comparative international benchmarks. The analysis draws on data submitted by the Office of the Secretary of Defense, the military departments, and the Defense Finance and Accounting Service (DFAS) through December 31, 2023.

Defense spending as a share of Gross Domestic Product stands at 3.1 percent for FY2024, consistent with the prior fiscal year and within the range recommended by the NATO alliance standard of 2.0 percent minimum. The United States remains the world's largest defense spender in absolute terms, accounting for approximately 37 percent of total global military expenditure as measured by the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI) in its 2023 annual report.

SECTION 1: APPROPRIATIONS OVERVIEW

The FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), signed into law by President Biden on December 22, 2023, authorized $886.3 billion in total defense-related spending. This figure encompasses both discretionary and mandatory spending components distributed across the following primary accounts:

TABLE 1: FY2024 Defense Appropriations by Category

Category                              Amount ($ Billions)    % of Total
Operation and Maintenance             $295.4                 33.3%
Military Personnel                    $176.2                 19.9%
Procurement                           $168.7                 19.0%
Research, Development, Test & Eval    $145.9                 16.5%
Military Construction                 $18.3                  2.1%
Family Housing                        $2.1                   0.2%
Revolving and Management Funds        $12.6                  1.4%
Defense Health Program                $57.1                  6.4%
Other Defense Programs                $10.0                  1.1%
TOTAL                                 $886.3                 100.0%

The Operation and Maintenance (O&M) account, at $295.4 billion, represents the single largest category and reflects sustained operational tempo across all combatant commands, including ongoing support to U.S. European Command (EUCOM) operations related to security assistance for Ukraine, which totaled $7.9 billion in direct drawdown authority during FY2024.

SECTION 2: FIVE-YEAR SPENDING TREND ANALYSIS

Defense spending has grown at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 4.7 percent over the five-year period from FY2020 through FY2024. The following table illustrates enacted budget levels across this period:

TABLE 2: Defense Budget Trend, FY2020–FY2024

Fiscal Year    Enacted Budget ($ Billions)    Year-over-Year Change
FY2020         $738.0                         +3.3%
FY2021         $753.5                         +2.1%
FY2022         $782.0                         +3.8%
FY2023         $858.5                         +9.8%
FY2024         $886.3                         +3.2%

The notable 9.8 percent increase in FY2023 reflected emergency supplemental appropriations totaling $44.2 billion passed under the Ukraine Supplemental Appropriations Act of 2022 and subsequent legislative packages. Excluding supplemental appropriations, the baseline growth rate from FY2022 to FY2023 was approximately 4.1 percent.

SECTION 3: SERVICE-LEVEL BUDGET DISTRIBUTION

Funding is distributed across the four military departments and defense-wide accounts as follows:

TABLE 3: FY2024 Budget by Military Department

Department                    FY2024 Budget ($ Billions)    Change from FY2023
Department of the Army        $185.7                        +2.9%
Department of the Navy        $230.8                        +3.7%
Department of the Air Force   $258.4                        +4.1%
Defense-Wide Accounts         $211.4                        +1.8%
TOTAL                         $886.3                        +3.2%

The Department of the Air Force, which includes the United States Space Force established on December 20, 2019, received the largest single-department allocation at $258.4 billion. The Space Force component within this figure totaled $29.4 billion, an increase of $3.1 billion or 11.8 percent over FY2023, reflecting continued investment in satellite communications, missile warning systems, and space domain awareness capabilities.

The Department of the Navy's allocation of $230.8 billion includes $4.2 billion designated for the AUKUS submarine industrial base initiative, consistent with the trilateral security agreement signed by the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia in September 2021.

SECTION 4: MAJOR PROCUREMENT PROGRAMS

The Procurement account of $168.7 billion funds acquisition of weapons systems, platforms, and equipment. The ten largest individual program expenditures in FY2024 are as follows:

1. F-35 Lightning II Joint Strike Fighter Program — $15.6 billion
   (Covers procurement of 83 aircraft across all three variants; prime contractor: Lockheed Martin Corporation)

2. Virginia-Class Submarine Program — $9.8 billion
   (Funds construction of 2.3 boats per year on average; prime contractors: General Dynamics Electric Boat and Huntington Ingalls Industries)

3. B-21 Raider Long-Range Strike Bomber — $5.3 billion
   (Low-rate initial production phase; prime contractor: Northrop Grumman Corporation)

4. Columbia-Class Ballistic Missile Submarine — $6.2 billion
   (Lead ship USS Columbia under construction at Groton, Connecticut)

5. CH-47F Chinook Block II Helicopter — $1.4 billion

6. Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System (GMLRS) — $2.1 billion

7. Javelin Anti-Tank Missile System — $1.8 billion

8. Patriot Advanced Capability-3 (PAC-3) Missile Segment Enhancement — $2.4 billion

9. DDG-51 Arleigh Burke-Class Destroyer — $4.7 billion
   (Funds procurement of 3 destroyers in FY2024)

10. MQ-9 Reaper Unmanned Aerial Vehicle — $0.9 billion

Combined, these ten programs account for $50.2 billion, or 29.8 percent of the total Procurement account.

SECTION 5: RESEARCH, DEVELOPMENT, TEST AND EVALUATION

The RDT&E account totaling $145.9 billion represents a record high in inflation-adjusted terms and reflects the Department's emphasis on next-generation capabilities. Key investment areas include:

• Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning: $1.8 billion across 685 individual projects, coordinated through the Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office (CDAO) established in February 2022

• Hypersonic Weapons Development: $4.7 billion, including the Army's Long-Range Hypersonic Weapon (LRHW) and the Navy's Conventional Prompt Strike (CPS) program

• Directed Energy Weapons: $1.1 billion, including the High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance (HELIOS) system

• Microelectronics and Semiconductor Research: $3.3 billion, partially leveraging provisions of the CHIPS and Science Act signed August 9, 2022

• Quantum Computing Applications: $0.7 billion

SECTION 6: PERSONNEL COSTS AND WORKFORCE

Military Personnel costs of $176.2 billion support an active-duty end strength of 1,328,500 personnel as authorized by the FY2024 NDAA, a reduction of 3,000 from the FY2023 authorized level. The FY2024 NDAA also authorized a 5.2 percent pay raise for all uniformed service members, the largest such increase since 2002, effective January 1, 2024.

The Defense Health Program at $57.1 billion provides healthcare to approximately 9.6 million eligible beneficiaries through the TRICARE system, including active-duty personnel, retirees, and their dependents. Per-beneficiary costs have increased at an average annual rate of 3.8 percent over the past decade.

SECTION 7: INTERNATIONAL COMPARISONS

The United States defense budget of $886.3 billion substantially exceeds the combined defense spending of the next nine largest military spenders. For reference, China's official defense budget for 2023 was announced at approximately $225 billion (1.553 trillion yuan), though independent analysts including SIPRI estimate actual Chinese military expenditure at between $296 billion and $340 billion when accounting for off-budget items. Russia's defense budget for 2024 was set at 10.8 trillion rubles, approximately $117 billion at current exchange rates, representing 6.0 percent of Russian GDP.

Among NATO allies, only Poland (4.0 percent of GDP), Greece (2.9 percent), Estonia (2.7 percent), and Latvia (2.4 percent) exceeded the United States' 3.1 percent GDP share in FY2024. Germany allocated 2.1 percent of GDP to defense, meeting the NATO threshold for the first time since 1992.

SECTION 8: AUDIT STATUS AND FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

The Department of Defense has now completed six consecutive annual financial statement audits since the first-ever DOD-wide audit was initiated in November 2017. The FY2023 audit, completed November 15, 2023, resulted in a disclaimer of opinion for the seventh consecutive year, though the number of material weaknesses declined from 28 in FY2022 to 26 in FY2023. The Department has remediated 1,143 findings since the audit program's inception.

The DOD Comptroller, Michael McCord, testified before the Senate Armed Services Committee on January 30, 2024, that full auditability remains a long-term goal with a target of achieving a clean opinion by FY2028.

CONCLUSION AND OUTLOOK

The FY2024 defense budget of $886.3 billion reflects sustained bipartisan commitment to national security investment amid a complex global threat environment. The Congressional Budget Office projects that under current policy assumptions, defense spending will reach approximately $1.0 trillion by FY2028, driven primarily by personnel cost growth, continued recapitalization of aging platforms, and expanded investment in emerging technology domains.

This report will be updated upon completion of the FY2024 mid-year execution review, anticipated for release in August 2024.
