EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comparative analysis of appropriated and expended budgets across major Commonwealth departments for Fiscal Year 2023–2024 (July 1, 2023 – June 30, 2024) relative to Fiscal Year 2022–2023. The analysis was conducted under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and reflects final audited expenditure data submitted by department secretaries no later than August 31, 2024. Total General Fund expenditures for FY 2023–2024 reached $42.8 billion, representing an increase of $1.94 billion, or 4.75 percent, over the FY 2022–2023 total of $40.86 billion.

The Commonwealth ended FY 2023–2024 with a General Fund balance of $3.1 billion, down from $4.2 billion at the close of FY 2022–2023, reflecting increased investments in education, public health infrastructure, and transportation maintenance. Revenue collections totaled $45.9 billion, exceeding the official estimate by $312 million, or 0.68 percent.

SECTION 1: METHODOLOGY AND DATA SOURCES

Budget figures presented in this report are drawn from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES), the official financial management platform administered by the Office of Comptroller Operations. Expenditure data reflects all obligations incurred and payments processed through June 30, 2024. Supplemental appropriations enacted by the General Assembly between July 2023 and May 2024 are incorporated into the FY 2023–2024 appropriated totals.

Percentage change calculations compare final FY 2022–2023 audited expenditures against FY 2023–2024 appropriated budgets, with actual expenditures noted where final figures have been certified. Departments with variances exceeding 5 percent between appropriation and actual expenditure are flagged for narrative explanation.

SECTION 3: NOTABLE BUDGET DEVELOPMENTS BY DEPARTMENT

3.1 DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION

The Department of Education received the largest single-year dollar increase among all Commonwealth departments, with actual expenditures rising $546.3 million, or 6.97 percent, to $8.388 billion. This increase reflects the third year of implementation of the Basic Education Funding formula revision enacted under Act 55 of 2023, which directed an additional $350 million toward the 100 lowest-wealth school districts. Secretary Khalid Mumin confirmed that 487 of Pennsylvania's 500 school districts received increased state subsidies in FY 2023–2024. Special education funding increased by $112.4 million, reaching a total of $1.27 billion. The department came in $22.4 million under its appropriated ceiling, primarily due to delayed hiring in the Office of School Improvement.

3.2 DEPARTMENT OF HUMAN SERVICES

Human Services remained the Commonwealth's single largest department by expenditure, with actual spending of $14.901 billion, marginally exceeding its appropriation of $14.890 billion by $10.8 million. The overage was attributable to higher-than-projected Medicaid enrollment, which reached 3.4 million recipients as of March 31, 2024, compared to the budgeted estimate of 3.27 million. Secretary Dr. Val Arkoosh requested and received a $15 million emergency supplemental appropriation in April 2024 to cover the shortfall. The Long-Term Living program, which funds nursing home and home-based care for elderly Pennsylvanians, accounted for $4.1 billion of total department spending, up 3.2 percent from FY 2022–2023.

3.3 DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH

The Department of Health recorded the highest percentage increase of any major department, with actual expenditures rising 13.89 percent to $1.372 billion. This growth was driven by two primary factors. First, the Commonwealth allocated $98.7 million in new funding for the Opioid Settlement Remediation program, drawing on funds received from the national opioid litigation settlements with distributors AmerisourceBergen, Cardinal Health, and McKesson. Second, the department expanded its network of community health centers by 14 locations, bringing the statewide total to 312 certified sites. Secretary Dr. Debra Bogen noted that the department's public health workforce grew by 618 full-time equivalent positions during the fiscal year, the largest single-year staffing increase since 2009.

3.4 DEPARTMENT OF CORRECTIONS

The Department of Corrections showed the smallest percentage increase among major departments at 1.72 percent, with actual expenditures of $3.040 billion. The restrained growth reflects a continued decline in the state prison population, which stood at 37,842 inmates as of June 30, 2024, down from 39,104 at the same date in 2023, a reduction of 1,262 individuals or 3.23 percent. Secretary Dr. Laurel Harry attributed the population decline to expanded pre-trial diversion programs and accelerated parole processing. The department closed the State Correctional Institution at Cresson effective January 15, 2024, generating annualized savings of approximately $31 million.

3.5 DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION

PennDOT's actual expenditures of $3.276 billion reflected a 5.09 percent increase over FY 2022–2023, driven largely by rising construction material costs. Asphalt prices increased an average of 8.4 percent statewide during the fiscal year, and steel costs for bridge projects rose 6.1 percent. The department completed 1,847 lane-miles of resurfacing during FY 2023–2024, compared to 1,792 lane-miles in the prior year. Secretary Mike Carroll reported that 3,412 of Pennsylvania's 25,477 state-maintained bridges remained in poor condition as of June 30, 2024, representing 13.4 percent of the total bridge inventory, down from 14.1 percent in FY 2022–2023.

SECTION 4: REVENUE PERFORMANCE AND FUND BALANCE

Total General Fund revenue collections for FY 2023–2024 were $45.9 billion, exceeding the certified revenue estimate of $45.588 billion by $312 million. Personal income tax collections of $17.4 billion were the largest single revenue source, representing 37.9 percent of total collections and exceeding the estimate by $204 million. Sales and use tax collections reached $12.8 billion, $67 million above estimate. Corporate net income tax collections of $5.1 billion fell $41 million short of the official estimate, reflecting lower-than-anticipated corporate profitability in the manufacturing sector.

The General Fund unappropriated balance as of June 30, 2024 stood at $3.1 billion. The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund, commonly known as the Rainy Day Fund, held $1.83 billion as of the same date, unchanged from FY 2022–2023 as no transfers were made in either direction during the fiscal year.

SECTION 5: WORKFORCE AND PERSONNEL COSTS

Personnel costs across all General Fund departments totaled $9.87 billion in FY 2023–2024, representing 23.1 percent of total General Fund expenditures. This compares to $9.41 billion, or 23.0 percent of expenditures, in FY 2022–2023. The increase of $460 million, or 4.89 percent, reflects a 3.25 percent general wage increase effective July 1, 2023, applicable to approximately 67,000 Commonwealth employees covered under collective bargaining agreements with AFSCME Council 13 and other unions. Pension contributions to the State Employees' Retirement System (SERS) totaled $1.44 billion, representing an employer contribution rate of 34.97 percent of covered payroll, down from 36.12 percent in FY 2022–2023.

SECTION 6: OUTLOOK FOR FISCAL YEAR 2024–2025

Governor Josh Shapiro's proposed FY 2024–2025 budget, submitted to the General Assembly on February 6, 2024, requests total General Fund appropriations of $48.3 billion, an increase of $5.5 billion or 12.9 percent over FY 2023–2024 actual expenditures. The proposal includes $1.1 billion in new education funding, a $500 million investment in the Pennsylvania CHIPS and Science Act implementation fund, and $275 million for expanded child care subsidies. Final budget negotiations between the Governor's Office and the General Assembly were ongoing as of the date of this report.
