EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive comparison of departmental budget allocations and expenditures for the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania across fiscal years 2022-2023 and 2023-2024. The analysis covers seventeen major executive departments, highlighting year-over-year changes, areas of significant growth or reduction, and compliance with the General Appropriations Act signed by Governor Josh Shapiro on July 11, 2023. Total general fund appropriations for FY2023-2024 reached $45.8 billion, representing an increase of $2.1 billion, or approximately 4.8 percent, over the prior fiscal year's enacted budget of $43.7 billion.

The Bureau of Financial Management, under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson, conducted this review using certified expenditure data submitted by each department through the Commonwealth's SAP-based financial management system. All figures reflect final audited expenditures for FY2022-2023 and enacted appropriations for FY2023-2024, with actual FY2023-2024 expenditures to be finalized in the December 2024 closeout report.

SECTION 1: STATEWIDE BUDGET OVERVIEW

The Commonwealth's total operating budget for FY2023-2024, inclusive of federal funds, special funds, and augmentations, stands at $112.4 billion. The general fund portion, which represents the primary measure of state-controlled discretionary spending, increased from $43.7 billion in FY2022-2023 to $45.8 billion in FY2023-2024.

Key statewide figures:

- General Fund Revenue (FY2023-2024 estimate): $46.1 billion
- General Fund Expenditures (FY2023-2024 enacted): $45.8 billion
- Projected Year-End Surplus: $312 million
- Rainy Day Fund Balance as of June 30, 2024: $6.23 billion
- Total State Workforce (FY2023-2024): 78,412 full-time equivalent positions
- Personnel costs as percentage of general fund: 31.4 percent

SECTION 3: NOTABLE BUDGET CHANGES AND ANALYSIS

3.1 Department of Human Services

The Department of Human Services, led by Secretary Dr. Val Arkoosh, received the largest single appropriation in the Commonwealth at $17.614 billion, accounting for 38.5 percent of total general fund spending. The $772.4 million increase reflects mandatory growth in Medical Assistance (Medicaid) enrollment, which reached 3.47 million enrollees as of March 31, 2024, up from 3.31 million in the prior year. The Long-Term Care line item alone increased by $218.6 million to accommodate rising nursing facility costs, which averaged $387 per patient day in FY2023-2024 compared to $361 in FY2022-2023.

3.2 Department of Education

The Department of Education, under Secretary Dr. Khalid Mumin, saw the second-largest dollar increase at $522.4 million, bringing its total to $8.503 billion. This increase was driven primarily by the Basic Education Funding formula, which distributed $7.1 billion to 500 school districts statewide. The Adequacy Supplement, a new funding mechanism introduced in the FY2023-2024 budget, directed $100 million specifically to the 100 lowest-wealth school districts, with individual district allocations ranging from $487,000 to $4.2 million.

3.3 Department of Health

The Department of Health recorded the highest percentage increase among major departments at 13.82 percent, adding $144.2 million to reach a total of $1.187 billion. Secretary Dr. Debra Bogen directed $62.4 million of new funding toward the expansion of community health centers in rural counties, with Bradford, Cameron, and Sullivan counties identified as priority service areas. An additional $38.7 million was allocated to maternal and infant health initiatives following a legislative mandate enacted through Act 24 of 2023.

3.4 Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs

The Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs recorded the highest percentage increase of any department at 16.10 percent, rising from $47.2 million to $54.8 million. This increase reflects the ongoing response to opioid-related overdose deaths, which totaled 5,158 in calendar year 2023 according to the Pennsylvania Coroners Association. The department expanded its network of certified recovery specialist programs to 48 counties, up from 39 counties in FY2022-2023.

3.5 Pennsylvania State Police

The Pennsylvania State Police budget increased by $44.3 million to $1.531 billion. The increase covers personnel costs associated with the graduation of Cadet Class 183 in March 2024, which added 246 new troopers to the force. As of June 30, 2024, total authorized trooper strength stood at 4,718, compared to 4,512 in the prior fiscal year.

SECTION 5: FEDERAL FUND ACTIVITY

Federal funds flowing through Commonwealth departments totaled $48.6 billion in FY2023-2024, a decrease of $3.1 billion from the prior year's $51.7 billion. The decline reflects the expiration of pandemic-era American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) allocations. The Department of Human Services accounted for $29.4 billion of federal receipts, primarily through the Federal Medical Assistance Percentage (FMAP) reimbursement rate of 55.63 percent applicable to Pennsylvania in FY2023-2024.

SECTION 6: RECOMMENDATIONS

The Bureau of Financial Management offers the following recommendations for consideration in the FY2024-2025 budget development process:

1. The Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs should receive continued above-average funding growth given documented demand for recovery services and the Commonwealth's commitment to reducing overdose mortality by 15 percent by December 31, 2025.

2. Departments with lapse rates exceeding 2.5 percent, specifically the Office of Administration and the Department of Agriculture, should conduct internal reviews of budget estimation practices prior to the submission of FY2024-2025 budget requests on October 15, 2024.

3. The transition of $412 million in ARPA-funded programs to general fund support should be evaluated on a program-by-program basis to identify which initiatives demonstrate sufficient return on investment to warrant permanent appropriation.
