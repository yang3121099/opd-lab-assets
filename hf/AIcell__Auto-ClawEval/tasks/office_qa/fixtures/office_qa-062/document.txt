EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comparative analysis of appropriated versus actual expenditures across major Commonwealth departments for Fiscal Year 2023-2024 (July 1, 2023 through June 30, 2024). The analysis identifies departments that operated within budget, those that required supplemental appropriations, and those that returned unspent funds to the General Fund. Total General Fund appropriations for FY 2023-2024 stood at $47.6 billion, representing a 4.2 percent increase over the FY 2022-2023 enacted budget of $45.7 billion. Actual expenditures totaled $46.9 billion, leaving an unspent balance of approximately $700 million, or 1.47 percent of total appropriations.

Budget Secretary Uri Monson certified these figures on September 30, 2024, following the close of the fiscal year and completion of preliminary reconciliation by the Office of the Comptroller.

SECTION 1: METHODOLOGY AND SCOPE

This comparison report draws from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES), the official financial management platform maintained by the Office of Administration. Data was extracted on August 12, 2024, following the 45-day close period ending August 15, 2024. All figures reflect final appropriations including any mid-year transfers authorized by the General Assembly under Act 7A of 2024, signed by Governor Josh Shapiro on February 28, 2024.

The report covers 22 executive branch departments and 4 independent agencies that receive General Fund appropriations. Federal funds, restricted receipts, and augmentations are reported separately in Appendix C and are not included in the primary comparison tables below.

SECTION 3: NOTABLE VARIANCES AND ANALYSIS

3.1 Department of Human Services — Supplemental Appropriation Required

The Department of Human Services required the largest supplemental appropriation of any agency in FY 2023-2024, totaling $214.3 million. This was driven primarily by a 6.8 percent increase in Medical Assistance enrollment compared to projections established during the FY 2022-2023 budget cycle. As of June 30, 2024, Medical Assistance enrollment stood at 3,412,000 individuals, compared to the projected enrollment of 3,195,000 used in the original budget model. Secretary Dr. Val Arkoosh requested the supplemental funding on January 9, 2024, and the General Assembly approved the transfer on February 14, 2024.

Despite the supplemental, the department finished $35.6 million under its total available appropriation, largely due to lower-than-anticipated utilization of long-term care services in the fourth quarter of the fiscal year.

3.2 Department of Transportation — Largest Percentage Underspend

The Department of Transportation recorded the largest percentage underspend among major departments at 2.91 percent, or $56.5 million. Secretary Mike Carroll attributed this variance to two primary factors: (1) the delayed procurement of 14 highway maintenance vehicles originally scheduled for delivery in May 2024, which slipped to the first quarter of FY 2024-2025 due to supply chain constraints from manufacturer Volvo Financial Services; and (2) lower-than-projected fuel costs, which came in at $3.47 per gallon on average versus the budgeted assumption of $3.89 per gallon. The fuel savings alone accounted for approximately $22.1 million of the variance.

3.3 Department of Corrections — Staffing and Overtime Pressures

The Department of Corrections required a supplemental appropriation of $88.1 million, the second largest in the fiscal year. Secretary Dr. Laurel Harry reported that correctional officer overtime costs reached $143.7 million for the year, exceeding the budgeted amount of $97.4 million by $46.3 million. This was driven by a statewide correctional officer vacancy rate of 14.2 percent as of December 31, 2023. The department operated 23 state correctional institutions during the fiscal year, with SCI Phoenix in Montgomery County and SCI Rockview in Centre County recording the highest overtime expenditures at $18.4 million and $14.2 million respectively.

3.4 Department of Military and Veterans Affairs — Capital Project Delays

The Department of Military and Veterans Affairs underspent its appropriation by $7.2 million, or 3.29 percent. The primary driver was the delayed start of renovation work at the Hollidaysburg Veterans' Home in Blair County. The project, budgeted at $9.8 million, did not receive final contractor approval until June 17, 2024, meaning only $2.6 million in expenditures were recorded before fiscal year close. The remaining $7.2 million was re-appropriated in the FY 2024-2025 enacted budget signed by Governor Shapiro on July 11, 2024.

SECTION 4: YEAR-OVER-YEAR COMPARISON

TABLE 2: TOTAL GENERAL FUND EXPENDITURES — THREE-YEAR TREND

  FY 2021-2022 Actual:    $43,114.6 million
  FY 2022-2023 Actual:    $45,288.3 million   (+5.04% year-over-year)
  FY 2023-2024 Actual:    $46,900.1 million   (+3.56% year-over-year)

The three-year compound annual growth rate in General Fund expenditures stands at 4.29 percent. The slowdown in growth from FY 2022-2023 to FY 2023-2024 reflects the expiration of several one-time federal pandemic relief programs that had been supplementing state spending in prior years. Specifically, the enhanced Federal Medical Assistance Percentage (FMAP) rate of 6.2 percentage points, which had provided Pennsylvania with approximately $1.1 billion in additional federal Medicaid matching funds annually since March 2020, was fully phased out by December 31, 2023.

SECTION 5: PERSONNEL COSTS ACROSS DEPARTMENTS

Personnel costs, including salaries, wages, and benefits, represented 38.4 percent of total General Fund expenditures in FY 2023-2024, totaling approximately $18.0 billion. This compares to 37.1 percent in FY 2022-2023. The increase reflects the implementation of the collective bargaining agreement ratified on April 3, 2023, between the Commonwealth and AFSCME Council 13, which represents approximately 42,000 state employees. The agreement provided a 3.0 percent general pay increase effective July 1, 2023, and an additional 2.75 percent increase effective January 1, 2024.

The Department of Corrections had the highest personnel cost as a share of its total budget at 82.7 percent. The Department of Transportation had the lowest among major departments at 31.4 percent, reflecting its significant capital and infrastructure expenditure obligations.

SECTION 6: RECOMMENDATIONS

The Bureau of Financial Management and Analysis offers the following recommendations based on FY 2023-2024 results:

1. The Department of Human Services should revise its Medical Assistance enrollment projection methodology to incorporate real-time enrollment data from the COMPASS eligibility system on a quarterly basis, rather than relying solely on annual actuarial estimates. This change is projected to reduce supplemental appropriation requests by an estimated 40 to 60 percent in future fiscal years.

2. The Department of Corrections should present a comprehensive staffing plan to the General Assembly by December 31, 2024, addressing the 14.2 percent vacancy rate and its impact on overtime expenditures. Continued reliance on overtime at current levels is fiscally unsustainable and represents a structural budget risk.

3. Capital project appropriations for the Department of Military and Veterans Affairs should include a 90-day pre-procurement requirement to ensure contractor selection is completed before the start of the fiscal year in which funds are appropriated, reducing year-end lapses.
