EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents comprehensive commodity price statistics for fiscal year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023), covering energy, agricultural, metals, and livestock sectors. Data was compiled from the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME), the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX), the Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), and direct producer surveys conducted by the BLS. The findings reflect significant price volatility across most commodity categories, driven by geopolitical disruptions, shifting monetary policy, and post-pandemic supply chain normalization.

Overall, the Producer Price Index (PPI) for all commodities declined 4.7 percent year-over-year in fiscal year 2023, following an exceptional 14.2 percent increase recorded in fiscal year 2022. This correction represents the largest single-year decline in the PPI commodity index since fiscal year 2009, when the index fell 6.1 percent during the global financial crisis.

SECTION 1: ENERGY COMMODITIES

1.1 Crude Oil

West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil averaged $78.42 per barrel during fiscal year 2023, compared to $97.86 per barrel in fiscal year 2022, representing a decline of 19.9 percent. The annual high of $93.68 per barrel was recorded on September 27, 2023, while the annual low of $66.74 per barrel occurred on March 17, 2023.

Brent crude, the international benchmark, averaged $83.17 per barrel for the fiscal year, down from $103.54 per barrel in fiscal year 2022, a decrease of 19.7 percent. The spread between Brent and WTI averaged $4.75 per barrel, slightly narrower than the $5.68 average spread recorded in fiscal year 2022.

OPEC+ production cuts announced on April 2, 2023, totaling approximately 1.66 million barrels per day, contributed to a price recovery in the second half of the fiscal year. U.S. domestic crude production averaged 12.4 million barrels per day, the highest annual average since 2019.

1.2 Natural Gas

Henry Hub natural gas spot prices averaged $2.67 per million British thermal units (MMBtu) in fiscal year 2023, a dramatic decline of 57.4 percent from the fiscal year 2022 average of $6.27 per MMBtu. The fiscal year 2023 average represents the lowest annual average price since fiscal year 2020, when prices averaged $2.14 per MMBtu.

The price collapse was attributed to record U.S. production levels of 103.8 billion cubic feet per day, mild winter temperatures across the continental United States, and elevated storage inventories that remained approximately 12 percent above the five-year average throughout the spring and summer months.

1.3 Refined Products

Retail gasoline prices averaged $3.53 per gallon nationally in fiscal year 2023, down from $4.07 per gallon in fiscal year 2022, a decrease of 13.3 percent. Diesel fuel averaged $4.02 per gallon, compared to $5.14 per gallon in fiscal year 2022, a decline of 21.8 percent.

Corn prices declined sharply following the July 2023 USDA World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates (WASDE) report, which projected U.S. corn production at 15.1 billion bushels, up 9.4 percent from the prior year. Wheat prices fell in part due to the resumption of Black Sea grain exports under the UN-brokered agreement, though the subsequent suspension of that agreement on July 17, 2023, caused a brief price spike of approximately 8.3 percent over a three-day trading period.

Rough rice was the notable exception to the downward trend, rising 14.1 percent following India's export restrictions announced on July 20, 2023, which affected approximately 40 percent of global rice trade.

2.2 Soft Commodities

Coffee arabica futures averaged $1.74 per pound in fiscal year 2023, down 18.3 percent from $2.13 per pound in fiscal year 2022. Cocoa futures, however, surged to an average of $3,187 per metric ton, up 26.4 percent from $2,521 per metric ton in fiscal year 2022, driven by poor harvests in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana, which together account for approximately 65 percent of global cocoa production.

Cotton futures averaged $0.83 per pound, declining 27.0 percent from $1.14 per pound in fiscal year 2022, as global demand softened amid reduced consumer spending on apparel.

SECTION 3: METALS

3.1 Precious Metals

Gold averaged $1,928 per troy ounce in fiscal year 2023, an increase of 5.3 percent from $1,831 per troy ounce in fiscal year 2022. Gold reached its fiscal year high of $2,048 per troy ounce on May 4, 2023, amid banking sector stress following the failures of Silicon Valley Bank on March 10, 2023, and Signature Bank on March 12, 2023.

Silver averaged $23.41 per troy ounce, up 3.8 percent from $22.55 per troy ounce in fiscal year 2022. The gold-to-silver ratio averaged 82.4 during fiscal year 2023, compared to 81.2 in fiscal year 2022.

Platinum averaged $1,002 per troy ounce, down 6.1 percent from $1,067 in fiscal year 2022. Palladium experienced the most dramatic decline among precious metals, falling 38.2 percent from an average of $2,114 per troy ounce in fiscal year 2022 to $1,305 per troy ounce in fiscal year 2023, as electric vehicle adoption reduced catalytic converter demand and Russian supply constraints eased.

Aluminum's 29.7 percent decline reflected reduced energy costs in European smelting operations and weaker-than-expected Chinese construction activity. Copper, often cited as a leading economic indicator, fell 10.0 percent but remained above its pre-pandemic five-year average of $6,847 per metric ton, suggesting continued underlying demand from electrification and infrastructure investment.

SECTION 4: LIVESTOCK AND DAIRY

Live cattle futures averaged $1.6742 per pound in fiscal year 2023, an increase of 14.8 percent from $1.4584 per pound in fiscal year 2022, driven by historically tight cattle supplies. The U.S. cattle herd as of January 1, 2023, stood at 89.3 million head, the smallest inventory since 1962.

Lean hog futures averaged $0.7831 per pound, down 18.4 percent from $0.9596 per pound in fiscal year 2022, as pork production expanded and export demand from China moderated.

Class III milk futures averaged $16.42 per hundredweight (cwt) in fiscal year 2023, a decline of 28.9 percent from $23.09 per cwt in fiscal year 2022, the steepest annual decline in dairy prices since fiscal year 2016.

SECTION 5: MARKET VOLATILITY AND RISK INDICATORS

The CFTC's Commodity Volatility Index (CVI), a composite measure of implied volatility across 24 major commodity futures contracts, averaged 28.3 in fiscal year 2023, down from 41.7 in fiscal year 2022 but still above the pre-pandemic ten-year average of 22.6 recorded between fiscal years 2010 and 2019.

Total open interest across all CFTC-regulated commodity futures and options contracts averaged 14.7 million contracts per trading day in fiscal year 2023, compared to 15.2 million contracts per day in fiscal year 2022. Managed money net long positions in energy commodities declined by approximately $47.3 billion from their fiscal year 2022 peak, reflecting reduced speculative interest as price trends moderated.

SECTION 6: OUTLOOK AND POLICY CONSIDERATIONS

The CFTC and BLS project continued price normalization across most commodity sectors in fiscal year 2024, contingent on stable geopolitical conditions and continued easing of supply chain constraints. Key risk factors include:

• Potential escalation of Middle East conflicts affecting crude oil transit through the Strait of Hormuz, through which approximately 21 million barrels per day transited in fiscal year 2023
• El Niño weather patterns projected to affect agricultural production in Southeast Asia and South America through mid-2024
• Federal Reserve monetary policy trajectory and its effect on the U.S. dollar, which maintained an inverse correlation of -0.67 with the Bloomberg Commodity Index during fiscal year 2023
• Chinese economic recovery pace, given that China accounted for approximately 53 percent of global copper consumption and 28 percent of global crude oil imports in calendar year 2023

METHODOLOGY NOTE

Price data presented in this report reflects front-month futures contract settlement prices unless otherwise noted. Spot prices for physical commodities were sourced from USDA Agricultural Marketing Service daily reports, Platts energy price assessments, and LME official settlement prices. All averages are calculated as arithmetic means of daily settlement prices over the applicable fiscal year period. Currency conversions used the Federal Reserve's H.10 statistical release average annual exchange rates.
