EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania's financial performance for the second quarter of Fiscal Year 2023-2024 (Q2 FY2024), covering the period October 1 through December 31, 2023, with a detailed comparison against the first quarter (Q1 FY2024, July 1 – September 30, 2023). Total General Fund revenues for Q2 FY2024 reached $10.84 billion, representing a 6.3 percent increase over Q1 FY2024 revenues of $10.20 billion. Total General Fund expenditures for Q2 FY2024 were $9.97 billion, compared to $10.61 billion in Q1 FY2024, a decrease of 6.0 percent. The resulting Q2 FY2024 surplus of $870 million marks a significant improvement over the Q1 FY2024 deficit of $410 million.

1.2 Key Revenue Observations

Personal Income Tax (PIT) collections of $4.487 billion in Q2 FY2024 exceeded Q1 collections by $174.5 million. The Bureau attributes this increase primarily to stronger-than-anticipated withholding receipts in October and November 2023, driven by continued labor market stability in the Commonwealth. Pennsylvania's unemployment rate held at 3.6 percent in November 2023, consistent with the prior quarter.

Sales and Use Tax demonstrated the strongest quarter-over-quarter growth among major revenue categories, rising 10.8 percent to $3.205 billion. This increase reflects elevated consumer spending during the October–December holiday retail period, consistent with historical seasonal patterns. Retail trade receipts in Pennsylvania grew an estimated 7.2 percent compared to the same period in the prior fiscal year.

Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT) collections rose 15.2 percent to $1.388 billion, the largest percentage gain among major categories. This increase is partially attributable to the scheduled reduction in the CNIT rate from 8.99 percent to 8.49 percent, which took effect January 1, 2024, prompting some corporations to accelerate estimated payments into Q2 FY2024 to take advantage of the higher deductibility environment.

Inheritance and Estate Tax collections declined 15.9 percent to $241.6 million, reflecting normal volatility in estate settlement timing rather than a structural trend. Realty Transfer Tax fell 11.1 percent to $176.3 million, consistent with continued softness in Pennsylvania residential real estate transaction volume, which declined 8.4 percent year-over-year according to data from the Pennsylvania Association of Realtors.

2.2 Key Expenditure Observations

The Department of Human Services (DHS) recorded the largest absolute expenditure decrease, falling $234.8 million (6.1 percent) to $3.612 billion in Q2 FY2024. This reduction reflects the completion of several one-time Medicaid redetermination-related administrative costs that were concentrated in Q1 FY2024 following the end of the federal continuous enrollment requirement. Pennsylvania completed its Medicaid redetermination process for approximately 2.1 million enrollees during the July–September 2023 period, resulting in elevated administrative processing costs in Q1.

PreK-12 Education expenditures declined $217.3 million (9.9 percent) to $1.987 billion. This decrease is primarily a timing artifact: the Commonwealth's largest Basic Education Funding payment cycle concentrates disbursements in July and August, inflating Q1 figures. The Q2 FY2024 education spending figure remains 3.1 percent above the comparable Q2 FY2023 figure of $1.927 billion, reflecting the $567 million increase in Basic Education Funding appropriated in the FY2023-2024 enacted budget signed by Governor Josh Shapiro on December 13, 2023.

Department of Corrections expenditures increased $15.5 million (2.1 percent) to $756.8 million, driven by a 1.8 percent increase in average daily population at state correctional institutions, which reached 37,214 inmates in Q2 FY2024 compared to 36,552 in Q1 FY2024. Additionally, contracted healthcare costs within the correctional system rose 4.7 percent quarter-over-quarter.

SECTION 3: FUND BALANCE AND FISCAL POSITION

3.1 General Fund Balance

The General Fund ended Q2 FY2024 (December 31, 2023) with an unappropriated surplus of $3.14 billion, compared to $2.27 billion at the close of Q1 FY2024 (September 30, 2023). This $870 million improvement reflects the combined effect of revenue growth and expenditure timing.

The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund) balance remained unchanged at $1.83 billion as of December 31, 2023. No transfers into or out of the Rainy Day Fund were authorized during Q2 FY2024.

3.2 Year-to-Date Position

Through the first half of FY2023-2024 (July 1 – December 31, 2023), total General Fund revenues reached $21.041 billion against total expenditures of $20.580 billion, yielding a year-to-date surplus of $461 million. The enacted FY2023-2024 budget projects full-year revenues of $42.8 billion and full-year expenditures of $44.4 billion, with the anticipated year-end shortfall to be addressed through authorized use of prior-year surplus balances.

SECTION 4: SPECIAL FUNDS OVERVIEW

4.1 Motor License Fund

Motor License Fund revenues for Q2 FY2024 totaled $1.127 billion, an increase of $43.2 million (4.0 percent) over Q1 FY2024 revenues of $1.084 billion. Liquid fuels tax receipts, which account for approximately 61 percent of Motor License Fund revenues, increased 3.2 percent quarter-over-quarter, consistent with higher vehicle miles traveled during the fall months.

4.2 Lottery Fund

The Pennsylvania Lottery reported Q2 FY2024 net revenues of $312.4 million, compared to $298.7 million in Q1 FY2024, an increase of $13.7 million (4.6 percent). Instant ticket sales remained the dominant product category, generating $198.6 million in net revenue. All Lottery Fund net revenues are dedicated to programs benefiting older Pennsylvanians, including the PACE prescription drug program and property tax and rent rebates.

SECTION 5: OUTLOOK AND RISK FACTORS

The Independent Fiscal Office (IFO) projects that full-year FY2023-2024 General Fund revenues will reach approximately $42.6 billion, approximately $200 million below the enacted estimate. Key downside risks include:

- Continued weakness in residential real estate activity, which could further suppress Realty Transfer Tax collections in Q3 and Q4 FY2024.
- Potential moderation in consumer spending if national economic conditions soften, which would reduce Sales and Use Tax growth.
- The scheduled CNIT rate reduction to 8.49 percent effective January 1, 2024, is projected to reduce annual CNIT collections by approximately $185 million in FY2024-2025.

Upside factors include stronger-than-projected labor market performance and the potential for additional federal reimbursements under the American Rescue Plan Act, of which Pennsylvania has $412 million in unobligated funds as of December 31, 2023.
