COMMONWEALTH OF PENNSYLVANIA
OFFICE OF THE BUDGET
ANNUAL EXPENDITURE TREND ANALYSIS
FISCAL YEARS 2019–2024

Prepared by the Bureau of Financial Management
Published: September 15, 2024
Report Reference: OB-FY2024-ETR-007

---

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of Commonwealth expenditure patterns across six fiscal years, from FY2018–19 through FY2023–24. Total general fund expenditures grew from $32.7 billion in FY2018–19 to $45.3 billion in FY2023–24, representing a cumulative increase of 38.5 percent over the five-year period. The average annual growth rate across all major spending categories was 6.7 percent, driven primarily by increases in Medical Assistance, public education subsidies, and debt service obligations. This report identifies areas of accelerating cost pressure, highlights departments that achieved spending reductions, and provides projections for FY2024–25 based on current appropriations.

---

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF TOTAL EXPENDITURES

Pennsylvania's general fund budget has expanded substantially since FY2018–19, reflecting both deliberate policy investments and structural cost pressures in entitlement programs. The following table summarizes total general fund expenditures by fiscal year:

Fiscal Year | Total Expenditures | Year-over-Year Change
FY2018–19   | $32.7 billion      | Baseline
FY2019–20   | $34.1 billion      | +4.3%
FY2020–21   | $36.8 billion      | +7.9%
FY2021–22   | $39.2 billion      | +6.5%
FY2022–23   | $42.6 billion      | +8.7%
FY2023–24   | $45.3 billion      | +6.3%

The spike observed in FY2020–21 reflects the Commonwealth's response to the COVID-19 pandemic, including emergency health expenditures totaling $1.4 billion in supplemental appropriations authorized under Governor Tom Wolf's emergency declaration. Federal reimbursements offset approximately $890 million of those costs, leaving a net state impact of $510 million.

SECTION 2: EXPENDITURE BY MAJOR CATEGORY

2.1 Health and Human Services

The Department of Human Services (DHS) remains the single largest spending category, accounting for $18.9 billion in FY2023–24, or 41.7 percent of total general fund expenditures. Medical Assistance (Medicaid) alone consumed $14.2 billion, up from $10.1 billion in FY2018–19, a 40.6 percent increase over five years. Enrollment in the Medical Assistance program reached 3.4 million Pennsylvanians as of June 30, 2024, compared to 2.8 million in June 2019.

Long-term care services, including nursing facility payments and home- and community-based waiver programs, accounted for $4.1 billion of the DHS total in FY2023–24. The Community HealthChoices waiver program, which transitioned to statewide implementation in January 2020, has seen per-participant costs rise from an average of $38,400 annually in FY2019–20 to $47,200 in FY2023–24, an increase of 22.9 percent.

2.2 Education

The Department of Education received $14.8 billion in general fund appropriations in FY2023–24, representing 32.7 percent of total expenditures. Basic education funding, the largest line item within the education budget, totaled $8.1 billion, reflecting a $567 million increase from the prior fiscal year following the landmark Commonwealth Court ruling in William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education, decided February 7, 2023, which found the existing school funding formula unconstitutional.

Special education funding reached $1.27 billion in FY2023–24, up 11.4 percent from $1.14 billion in FY2022–23. Higher education subsidies to the State System of Higher Education (PASSHE) totaled $477 million, a modest 2.1 percent increase from the prior year, continuing a pattern of relatively flat support for the 14-university system.

2.3 Transportation

The Pennsylvania Department of Transportation (PennDOT) received $4.6 billion in combined state and federal funding in FY2023–24. State motor license fund expenditures totaled $2.9 billion, while federal highway and transit funds contributed an additional $1.7 billion. Capital expenditures for bridge and highway reconstruction totaled $1.8 billion, with 1,247 bridge projects either completed or underway as of the end of the fiscal year. Administrative and operational costs for PennDOT, including personnel, totaled $612 million.

2.4 Corrections and Public Safety

The Department of Corrections operated with a general fund appropriation of $2.8 billion in FY2023–24, a 3.2 percent increase from $2.71 billion in FY2022–23. The state prison population stood at 37,400 as of June 30, 2024, down from a peak of 50,700 in FY2011–12, yet per-inmate costs have risen to $74,900 annually, compared to $48,200 in FY2018–19, an increase of 55.4 percent driven by healthcare costs, staffing wages, and facility maintenance.

The Pennsylvania State Police received $1.4 billion in FY2023–24, including $218 million in municipal police services reimbursements from municipalities that rely on state police coverage.

2.5 Debt Service

Debt service on general obligation bonds totaled $1.02 billion in FY2023–24, representing 2.25 percent of total general fund expenditures. The Commonwealth's outstanding general obligation debt stood at $11.4 billion as of June 30, 2024. Moody's Investors Service affirmed Pennsylvania's Aa3 bond rating in March 2024, citing improved pension funding progress and stable revenue growth, though analysts noted that pension liabilities remain a long-term fiscal risk.

---

SECTION 3: PENSION OBLIGATIONS

Pennsylvania's two major public pension systems, the Public School Employees' Retirement System (PSERS) and the State Employees' Retirement System (SERS), continue to represent significant budgetary pressure. Combined employer contributions from the general fund totaled $4.1 billion in FY2023–24.

PSERS reported a funded ratio of 58.4 percent as of December 31, 2023, with unfunded actuarial accrued liabilities of $43.7 billion. SERS reported a funded ratio of 67.2 percent with unfunded liabilities of $14.9 billion. The combined unfunded liability of $58.6 billion represents the Commonwealth's most significant long-term fiscal challenge.

Employer contribution rates for FY2023–24 were 34.94 percent of payroll for PSERS and 31.02 percent of payroll for SERS, compared to rates of 32.57 percent and 28.72 percent respectively in FY2021–22.

---

SECTION 4: AREAS OF SPENDING REDUCTION

Not all departments experienced expenditure growth. The Department of Agriculture reduced its general fund appropriation from $178 million in FY2021–22 to $161 million in FY2023–24, a 9.6 percent reduction achieved through consolidation of grant programs and administrative restructuring. The Office of Administration reduced technology infrastructure costs by $34 million through renegotiated vendor contracts with IBM and Unisys, effective July 1, 2023.

The Department of Revenue improved tax collection efficiency, reducing per-return processing costs from $4.12 in FY2021–22 to $3.47 in FY2023–24 through expanded e-filing adoption, which reached 91.3 percent of all personal income tax returns filed in calendar year 2023.

---

SECTION 5: FIVE-YEAR EXPENDITURE PROJECTIONS

Based on current appropriations, enacted legislation, and actuarial projections, the Bureau of Financial Management projects total general fund expenditures will reach $48.1 billion in FY2024–25, a 6.2 percent increase from FY2023–24. Medical Assistance is projected to grow by $780 million, driven by continued enrollment growth and pharmaceutical cost inflation averaging 8.3 percent annually. Basic education funding is projected to increase by at least $350 million pursuant to the ongoing remediation plan required by the William Penn court ruling.

Debt service is projected to decline marginally to $990 million in FY2024–25 as several older bond series mature, providing modest fiscal relief. Pension contributions are projected to increase to $4.35 billion as actuarial adjustments reflect updated mortality tables adopted by both PSERS and SERS boards in June 2024.

---

SECTION 6: CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The Commonwealth's expenditure trajectory reflects both the growing demand for public services and the structural weight of legacy obligations in pension and debt service. Several conclusions emerge from this analysis:

- Health and human services spending, at 41.7 percent of the general fund, will continue to drive budget growth absent significant policy changes to eligibility or reimbursement rates.
- The education funding remediation process, stemming from the February 2023 court ruling, introduces a multi-year spending commitment that the Governor's Budget Office estimates will require between $4.5 billion and $6.0 billion in additional education funding over seven years.
- Per-inmate corrections costs rising 55.4 percent over five years warrant a comprehensive review of healthcare contracting, currently managed under a $1.1 billion contract with Centurion Health Services expiring June 30, 2026.
- Pension funded ratios, while improved from lows of 51.3 percent (PSERS) and 59.4 percent (SERS) recorded in FY2019–20, remain well below the 80 percent threshold generally considered actuarially sound.

The Bureau of Financial Management recommends that the Office of the Budget commission a dedicated review of Medical Assistance managed care contract performance and that the General Assembly consider establishing a formal expenditure growth cap tied to the five-year average of nominal personal income growth, which stood at 4.8 percent annually for the period FY2019–24.
