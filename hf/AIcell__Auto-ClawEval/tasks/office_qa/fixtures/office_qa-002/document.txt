EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comparative analysis of appropriated and expended budgets across major Commonwealth departments for fiscal year 2023–2024 (July 1, 2023 through June 30, 2024) against the prior fiscal year 2022–2023. The analysis was prepared under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and reflects final certified expenditure data submitted by departmental comptrollers as of September 30, 2024. Total general fund expenditures for FY2023–2024 reached $42.7 billion, representing an increase of $1.84 billion, or 4.5 percent, over the FY2022–2023 total of $40.86 billion.

Key findings include significant spending growth in the Department of Human Services, driven by Medicaid enrollment increases, and a notable reduction in the Department of Transportation's capital expenditure budget following the completion of the Interstate 78 corridor improvement project in November 2023.

SECTION 1: METHODOLOGY AND DATA SOURCES

Budget figures presented in this report are drawn from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES), the official financial management platform administered by the Office of Comptroller Operations. Expenditure data reflects actual disbursements recorded through the close of the fiscal year on June 30, 2024. Appropriation figures reflect enacted budgets as signed by Governor Josh Shapiro on July 11, 2023, including any supplemental appropriations approved by the General Assembly through March 2024.

Percentage variances are calculated against prior-year actuals rather than prior-year appropriations to provide a more accurate measure of real spending change. Departments with variances exceeding plus or minus 8 percent are flagged for narrative explanation in Section 3.

SECTION 3: DEPARTMENTAL NARRATIVES — SIGNIFICANT VARIANCES

3.1 DEPARTMENT OF HUMAN SERVICES (+7.1%)

The Department of Human Services, led by Secretary Dr. Val Arkoosh, recorded the largest absolute dollar increase of any department, with actual expenditures of $17.388 billion in FY2023–2024 compared to $16.240 billion in the prior year. This $1.148 billion increase was driven primarily by three factors:

— Medicaid (Medical Assistance) enrollment grew by 94,000 individuals between July 2023 and June 2024, reaching a total enrolled population of 3.1 million Pennsylvanians as of June 30, 2024. Per-member costs increased by an average of 2.8 percent due to pharmaceutical price adjustments and expanded behavioral health service utilization.

— The Child Care Works subsidy program expanded following a $78.4 million supplemental appropriation approved by the General Assembly on February 14, 2024, serving an additional 11,200 families statewide.

— Long-term care expenditures increased by $214.6 million, or 9.3 percent, reflecting both an aging population and the phase-out of temporary federal enhanced matching funds that had offset state costs during the COVID-19 public health emergency period.

The department's actual expenditures of $17.388 billion exceeded its appropriated amount of $17.105 billion by $283.4 million, or 1.7 percent. This overrun was covered through a deficiency appropriation approved on June 28, 2024.

3.2 DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION (-8.5%)

The Department of Transportation recorded the only significant spending decrease among major departments, with actual expenditures of $2.632 billion in FY2023–2024 compared to $2.876 billion in FY2022–2023, a reduction of $244.5 million. This 8.5 percent decline exceeded the flagging threshold and is explained as follows:

The Interstate 78 Lehigh Valley Corridor Improvement Project, a $612 million multi-year capital initiative, reached substantial completion on November 17, 2023, with final contractor payments totaling $189.3 million disbursed in FY2022–2023. No comparable large capital project reached the disbursement phase in FY2023–2024.

Additionally, the department reduced its fleet replacement program by $31.2 million due to supply chain delays affecting vehicle delivery timelines. Approximately 847 vehicles originally scheduled for delivery in FY2023–2024 are now expected in the first quarter of FY2024–2025.

3.3 DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH (+7.2%)

The Department of Health, under Secretary Dr. Debra Bogen, recorded expenditures of $1.595 billion, an increase of $106.5 million or 7.2 percent over the prior year. The primary driver was the launch of the Pennsylvania Opioid Misuse and Addiction Abatement Trust distribution program, through which $88.7 million in settlement funds were disbursed to county health departments and treatment providers during the fiscal year. This represented the first full year of distributions following the trust's establishment in October 2022.

SECTION 4: PERSONNEL COST ANALYSIS

Personnel costs, including salaries, wages, and benefits, represented 38.2 percent of total general fund expenditures in FY2023–2024, totaling $14.707 billion. This compares to $14.012 billion, or 38.0 percent of total expenditures, in FY2022–2023, an increase of $695 million or 4.96 percent.

The increase reflects a negotiated wage settlement with AFSCME Council 13, representing approximately 42,000 Commonwealth employees, which took effect on July 1, 2023, and provided a 3.25 percent general pay increase in year one of a three-year agreement. Employer-paid healthcare contributions increased by 6.1 percent, consistent with the statewide actuarial projection published by the Pennsylvania Employees Benefit Trust Fund in January 2023.

Total authorized full-time equivalent (FTE) positions across all departments stood at 78,412 as of June 30, 2024, compared to 77,891 on June 30, 2023, an increase of 521 positions or 0.67 percent. The Department of Corrections added the largest number of new positions at 214 FTEs, primarily corrections officers at SCI Phoenix and SCI Rockview.

SECTION 5: BUDGET EXECUTION PERFORMANCE

Overall, Commonwealth departments demonstrated strong budget execution discipline in FY2023–2024. Of the 12 major departments reviewed, 9 came in within 2 percent of their appropriated amounts. The aggregate lapse rate — the percentage of appropriated funds not expended — was 0.44 percent, or approximately $169 million, slightly below the five-year average lapse rate of 0.61 percent.

The Department of Environmental Protection recorded the largest positive lapse at $15.2 million, or 2.6 percent of its appropriation, due to delays in filling 38 environmental scientist positions that remained vacant through the end of the fiscal year.

SECTION 6: OUTLOOK FOR FISCAL YEAR 2024–2025

Governor Shapiro's proposed budget for FY2024–2025, submitted to the General Assembly on February 6, 2024, projects total general fund expenditures of $48.3 billion, an increase of 13.3 percent over FY2023–2024 actuals. The proposed increase is concentrated in education funding, where the Governor has proposed an additional $1.1 billion for basic education subsidies in response to the Commonwealth Court's February 2023 ruling in William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education, which found the existing school funding system unconstitutional.

The Office of the Budget projects general fund revenues of $47.9 billion for FY2024–2025, leaving a projected structural gap of approximately $400 million that will require resolution through the budget negotiation process.
