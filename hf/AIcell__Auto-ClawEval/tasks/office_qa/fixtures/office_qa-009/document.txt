EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents comprehensive commodity price statistics for fiscal year 2023 (January 1 through December 31, 2023), covering energy, agricultural, metals, and soft commodities traded on U.S. exchanges and in global markets with significant U.S. participation. Data has been compiled from the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME), the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX), the Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), and supplementary data from the World Bank Commodity Price Index.

Overall commodity prices declined by an average of 11.3 percent in 2023 compared to 2022, following the extraordinary price volatility experienced in 2021 and 2022 driven by post-pandemic supply chain disruptions and the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Despite this broad decline, significant variation existed across commodity classes, with certain agricultural products posting year-over-year gains while energy commodities experienced the sharpest corrections.

SECTION 1: ENERGY COMMODITIES

1.1 Crude Oil

West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil, the U.S. benchmark, averaged $77.58 per barrel in 2023, representing a decline of 17.4 percent from the 2022 annual average of $93.97 per barrel. WTI reached its annual high of $93.68 per barrel on September 27, 2023, driven by OPEC+ production cuts announced by Saudi Arabia and Russia in June 2023. The annual low of $66.74 per barrel was recorded on March 17, 2023, amid banking sector instability following the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank.

Brent crude, the international benchmark, averaged $82.17 per barrel in 2023, down 16.6 percent from the 2022 average of $98.49 per barrel. The spread between Brent and WTI averaged $4.59 per barrel throughout the year, slightly wider than the $4.52 average spread recorded in 2022.

U.S. crude oil production averaged 12.9 million barrels per day in 2023, surpassing the previous record of 12.8 million barrels per day set in 2019, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA).

1.2 Natural Gas

Henry Hub natural gas spot prices averaged $2.57 per million British thermal units (MMBtu) in 2023, a dramatic decline of 59.8 percent from the 2022 average of $6.39 per MMBtu. This represented the lowest annual average price since 2020. The price collapse was attributed to record domestic production levels, mild winter temperatures across the continental United States, and reduced European demand for U.S. liquefied natural gas (LNG) exports compared to the emergency-driven surge of 2022.

The annual high for Henry Hub was $3.52 per MMBtu, recorded on January 13, 2023. The annual low of $1.73 per MMBtu was recorded on March 3, 2023.

1.3 Refined Products

Retail gasoline prices in the United States averaged $3.53 per gallon in 2023, down from $3.99 per gallon in 2022, a decrease of 11.5 percent. Diesel fuel averaged $4.05 per gallon, compared to $5.07 per gallon in 2022, a decline of 20.1 percent.

SECTION 2: AGRICULTURAL COMMODITIES

2.1 Grains

Corn prices fell sharply in 2023, averaging $4.83 per bushel on the CBOT (Chicago Board of Trade), down 26.1 percent from 2022. The U.S. corn harvest for 2023 was estimated at 15.34 billion bushels by the USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service (NASS), the second-largest crop on record. Wheat prices declined 29.9 percent to an average of $6.22 per bushel, as Black Sea grain corridor shipments partially resumed and global supply concerns eased compared to the immediate post-invasion period of 2022.

Rice was the notable exception among grains, rising 8.2 percent to $17.80 per hundredweight (cwt), driven by India's export restrictions implemented in July 2023 and reduced output from key Asian producers due to El Niño weather patterns.

2.2 Oilseeds and Vegetable Oils

Soybean oil averaged 60.2 cents per pound in 2023, down from 71.4 cents per pound in 2022, a decline of 15.7 percent. Palm oil, a key global substitute, averaged $879 per metric ton on international markets, down 28.3 percent from the 2022 average of $1,226 per metric ton.

2.3 Livestock

Live cattle futures averaged $176.42 per hundredweight in 2023, an increase of 14.8 percent from the 2022 average of $153.68 per hundredweight. This marked the highest annual average price for live cattle since CME records began in their current form. Lean hog futures averaged $79.34 per hundredweight, down 12.6 percent from $90.78 in 2022.

SECTION 3: METALS

3.1 Precious Metals

Gold averaged $1,940.54 per troy ounce in 2023, an increase of 8.2 percent from the 2022 average of $1,793.12 per troy ounce. Gold reached an all-time intraday high of $2,135.39 per troy ounce on December 4, 2023, surpassing the previous record set in August 2020. The annual low of $1,811.75 per troy ounce was recorded on February 28, 2023.

Silver averaged $23.35 per troy ounce in 2023, essentially flat compared to the 2022 average of $21.73 per troy ounce, representing a modest gain of 7.5 percent. The gold-to-silver ratio averaged 83.1:1 in 2023, compared to 82.5:1 in 2022.

3.2 Base Metals

Copper, traded on the COMEX division of NYMEX, averaged $3.85 per pound in 2023, down 4.2 percent from the 2022 average of $4.02 per pound. Copper prices were weighed down by slower-than-expected economic recovery in China, which accounts for approximately 55 percent of global copper consumption. Aluminum averaged $1,073 per metric ton on the London Metal Exchange (LME), down 17.3 percent from the 2022 average of $1,297 per metric ton.

3.3 Steel

U.S. hot-rolled coil (HRC) steel averaged $1,024 per short ton in 2023, down 28.6 percent from the 2022 average of $1,434 per short ton. Domestic steel production totaled 80.7 million short tons in 2023, according to the American Iron and Steel Institute (AISI), down 1.4 percent from 81.8 million short tons in 2022.

SECTION 4: SOFT COMMODITIES

4.1 Coffee and Cocoa

Arabica coffee futures averaged $1.72 per pound in 2023 on ICE Futures U.S., down 7.0 percent from $1.85 per pound in 2022. Cocoa futures experienced extraordinary appreciation, averaging $3,387 per metric ton in 2023, up 29.4 percent from the 2022 average of $2,617 per metric ton. Cocoa prices were driven by severe crop failures in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana, which together account for approximately 60 percent of global cocoa production.

4.2 Cotton and Sugar

Cotton averaged 84.2 cents per pound in 2023, down 16.8 percent from the 2022 average of $1.012 per pound. Raw sugar (No. 11 contract) averaged 24.1 cents per pound in 2023, up 21.7 percent from 19.8 cents per pound in 2022, reaching a 12-year high of 27.5 cents per pound on November 9, 2023.

SECTION 5: PRODUCER PRICE INDEX — COMMODITY COMPONENTS

The BLS Producer Price Index (PPI) for crude goods, which tracks commodity prices at the producer level, declined 9.7 percent on an annual basis in 2023. The energy component of the PPI fell 22.4 percent, while the foodstuffs and feedstuffs component declined 6.8 percent. The crude nonfood materials component declined 3.1 percent.

The PPI for processed goods for intermediate demand declined 4.3 percent in 2023, following a 14.2 percent increase in 2022. These figures reflect the broad normalization of commodity input costs across U.S. manufacturing and processing industries.

SECTION 6: OUTLOOK AND RISK FACTORS

The CFTC and BLS identify the following key risk factors for commodity price volatility in 2024:

• Continued OPEC+ production management, with Saudi Arabia's voluntary production cut of 1 million barrels per day extended through at least March 2024
• Ongoing El Niño weather pattern expected to persist through the first quarter of 2024, affecting agricultural output in South America, Southeast Asia, and sub-Saharan Africa
• Geopolitical risk premium in energy markets related to Middle East tensions following the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack on Israel and subsequent regional escalation
• Copper and lithium demand projections tied to electric vehicle adoption, with the IEA forecasting global EV sales of 14 million units in 2024
• Federal Reserve monetary policy trajectory and its effect on the U.S. dollar index, which averaged 103.5 in 2023 and exerts significant inverse pressure on dollar-denominated commodity prices

DATA SOURCES AND METHODOLOGY

Price data for exchange-traded commodities was sourced from CME Group, ICE Futures U.S., and NYMEX daily settlement prices. Annual averages represent arithmetic means of daily front-month contract settlement prices unless otherwise noted. Physical commodity price data was sourced from the USDA Agricultural Marketing Service, the EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report, and the LME official settlement prices. All prices are denominated in U.S. dollars. Data revisions may occur as source agencies finalize annual statistics.
