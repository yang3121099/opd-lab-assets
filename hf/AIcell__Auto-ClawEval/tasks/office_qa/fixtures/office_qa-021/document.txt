UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF DEFENSE
OFFICE OF THE UNDER SECRETARY OF DEFENSE (COMPTROLLER)
FISCAL YEAR 2024 DEFENSE SPENDING ANALYSIS
Published: February 14, 2025

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of United States defense expenditures for Fiscal Year 2024 (October 1, 2023 through September 30, 2024), including comparisons to prior fiscal years, programmatic breakdowns, and forward-looking projections through FY2029. The analysis draws on data certified by the Defense Finance and Accounting Service (DFAS), the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), and the Congressional Budget Office (CBO). Total national defense outlays for FY2024 reached $874.2 billion, representing a 4.7 percent increase over the FY2023 enacted level of $835.0 billion.

SECTION 1: TOTAL DEFENSE BUDGET OVERVIEW

The FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), signed into law by President Biden on December 22, 2023, authorized $886.3 billion in total defense-related spending. Of this authorization, $874.2 billion was obligated and outlaid by fiscal year end, reflecting a 1.4 percent under-execution rate consistent with historical norms.

The $874.2 billion total is divided across two primary budget functions:

Function 050 – National Defense: $849.8 billion
Function 054 – Defense-Related Activities (non-DoD): $24.4 billion

Within Function 050, the Department of Defense base budget accounted for $713.1 billion, while Overseas Contingency Operations (OCO) and emergency supplemental appropriations, primarily related to Ukraine Security Assistance, contributed an additional $136.7 billion.

Year-over-Year Comparison (in billions):

FY2020: $738.0
FY2021: $753.5
FY2022: $782.0
FY2023: $835.0
FY2024: $874.2

This represents a cumulative 18.5 percent increase in defense spending over the five-year period from FY2020 to FY2024, outpacing the general inflation rate of approximately 21.3 percent over the same period as measured by the Consumer Price Index, indicating a modest real-dollar decline in purchasing power when adjusted for inflation.

SECTION 2: BREAKDOWN BY MILITARY DEPARTMENT

Appropriations by service branch for FY2024 were distributed as follows:

Department of the Army: $192.4 billion (22.0% of DoD base budget)
Department of the Navy (including Marine Corps): $230.8 billion (26.4%)
Department of the Air Force (including Space Force): $258.3 billion (29.6%)
Defense-Wide Agencies and Activities: $168.3 billion (19.3%)
Military Construction and Family Housing: $16.9 billion (1.9%)
Defense Working Capital Funds: $6.4 billion (0.7%)

The Department of the Air Force received the largest single-service allocation for the third consecutive fiscal year, driven primarily by continued investment in the F-35A procurement program, B-21 Raider development, and the Ground Based Strategic Deterrent (GBSD) program, now formally designated the LGM-35A Sentinel.

SECTION 3: APPROPRIATION CATEGORY ANALYSIS

3.1 Military Personnel (MILPERS)

Total MILPERS appropriations reached $183.6 billion in FY2024, a 5.2 percent increase from the FY2023 level of $174.5 billion. This increase reflects the 5.2 percent military pay raise effective January 1, 2024, the largest such increase since 2002. Active duty end strength across all services totaled 1,328,400 personnel as of September 30, 2024, a decrease of 11,200 from the FY2023 level, reflecting ongoing recruitment challenges particularly in the Army, which fell 7,500 soldiers short of its authorized end strength of 452,000.

3.2 Operations and Maintenance (O&M)

O&M appropriations totaled $298.7 billion, representing 34.2 percent of the total DoD base budget and the single largest appropriation category. Key O&M expenditures included:

- Depot maintenance: $28.4 billion
- Base operations support: $41.2 billion
- Training and recruiting: $19.8 billion
- Healthcare (Defense Health Program): $57.3 billion
- Fuel and energy costs: $14.6 billion

The Defense Health Program alone grew 6.1 percent year-over-year, driven by increased utilization of TRICARE Prime and expanded mental health services mandated under the FY2023 NDAA.

3.3 Procurement

Total procurement spending reached $168.4 billion, a 7.3 percent increase from FY2023's $156.9 billion. Major procurement programs by expenditure:

F-35 Joint Strike Fighter (all variants): $15.6 billion (141 aircraft)
Virginia-class submarine: $9.8 billion (2 boats)
DDG-51 Arleigh Burke-class destroyer: $5.4 billion (2 ships)
CH-53K King Stallion helicopter: $2.1 billion (14 aircraft)
AH-64E Apache helicopter: $1.4 billion (24 aircraft)
Patriot Advanced Capability-3 (PAC-3) missiles: $3.2 billion
Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System (GMLRS): $2.8 billion

The GMLRS procurement figure represents a 94 percent increase over FY2022 levels, directly attributable to replenishment requirements following transfers to Ukraine under Presidential Drawdown Authority.

3.4 Research, Development, Test and Evaluation (RDT&E)

RDT&E appropriations totaled $145.9 billion, a record high and a 9.1 percent increase over FY2023's $133.7 billion. The top five RDT&E programs by funding level were:

1. Next Generation Air Dominance (NGAD): $11.2 billion
2. LGM-35A Sentinel ICBM: $8.7 billion
3. Hypersonic weapons development (multiple programs): $7.4 billion
4. Columbia-class ballistic missile submarine: $6.9 billion
5. Integrated Air and Missile Defense: $5.1 billion

SECTION 4: UKRAINE SECURITY ASSISTANCE

From February 2022 through September 30, 2024, the United States committed a cumulative $113.4 billion in total assistance to Ukraine across all categories. Of this total, $67.2 billion was defense-related, including:

- Presidential Drawdown Authority transfers: $24.8 billion
- Ukraine Security Assistance Initiative (USAI): $19.6 billion
- Foreign Military Financing (FMF): $14.3 billion
- Intelligence support and other security cooperation: $8.5 billion

In FY2024 alone, Ukraine-related defense assistance totaled $28.9 billion, funded through supplemental appropriations enacted under the National Security Supplemental signed April 24, 2024. The Department of Defense Inspector General, under Acting Inspector General Steven Stebbins, conducted 47 oversight audits of Ukraine assistance programs during FY2024.

SECTION 5: COMPARATIVE INTERNATIONAL CONTEXT

U.S. defense spending as a percentage of Gross Domestic Product stood at 3.1 percent in FY2024, based on a nominal GDP of $28.2 trillion. This compares to the following NATO ally expenditures as a percentage of GDP (2024 estimates per NATO Headquarters):

United Kingdom: 2.3%
Poland: 4.1%
Germany: 2.1%
France: 2.1%
Canada: 1.4%
NATO Alliance Average (excluding U.S.): 2.0%

The United States accounted for approximately 68 percent of total NATO defense expenditures in 2024, a figure that has remained relatively stable since 2019.

SECTION 6: COST GROWTH AND ACQUISITION PERFORMANCE

The Government Accountability Office (GAO) Major Defense Acquisition Programs (MDAP) report, published March 2024, identified 86 major programs with a combined total acquisition cost of $1.97 trillion. Of these programs, 34 experienced cost growth exceeding 10 percent from their original baseline estimates. The average cost growth across all MDAPs was 14.3 percent, with schedule delays averaging 29 months.

The most significant cost growth was recorded in the Columbia-class submarine program, where total acquisition cost estimates increased by $17.4 billion (12.8 percent) from the FY2022 baseline, attributed primarily to shipyard workforce shortages at General Dynamics Electric Boat in Groton, Connecticut, and Huntington Ingalls Industries in Newport News, Virginia.

SECTION 7: FORWARD PROJECTIONS FY2025–FY2029

The FY2025 President's Budget Request, submitted February 12, 2024, proposed $849.8 billion for national defense, which Congress subsequently increased to $895.2 billion through the FY2025 NDAA. CBO projects the following defense spending trajectory under current policy assumptions:

FY2025: $895.2 billion
FY2026: $921.4 billion
FY2027: $948.3 billion
FY2028: $976.1 billion
FY2029: $1,004.8 billion

These projections assume annual growth averaging 2.9 percent in nominal terms, representing approximately flat real spending when adjusted for projected defense-specific inflation. The projected crossing of the $1 trillion threshold in FY2029 would mark a historic milestone in U.S. defense appropriations history.

CONCLUSION

FY2024 defense spending of $874.2 billion reflects sustained investment across all appropriation categories, with particular emphasis on modernization of the nuclear triad, hypersonic weapons development, and replenishment of conventional munitions stocks. Persistent challenges include recruitment shortfalls, cost growth in major acquisition programs, and inflationary pressure on operations and maintenance accounts. The Under Secretary of Defense (Comptroller), Michael McCord, has directed all military departments to submit zero-based reviews of O&M accounts exceeding $500 million by March 31, 2025, as part of the Department's ongoing efficiency initiative.
