EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comparative analysis of appropriated and expended budgets across major Commonwealth departments for fiscal year 2023-2024 (July 1, 2023 through June 30, 2024) against the prior fiscal year 2022-2023. Total general fund expenditures for FY2023-2024 reached $42.7 billion, representing an increase of $1.84 billion, or 4.5 percent, over the FY2022-2023 total of $40.86 billion. This document was prepared under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and reflects data certified by the Office of the Comptroller as of September 30, 2024.

SECTION 1: METHODOLOGY AND DATA SOURCES

Budget figures presented in this report are drawn from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES), the official financial management platform maintained by the Office of the Comptroller. All figures reflect final certified expenditures unless otherwise noted. Appropriations data is sourced from the General Appropriations Act of 2023 (Act 1A of 2023) and the General Appropriations Act of 2024 (Act 1A of 2024), both signed into law by Governor Josh Shapiro.

Variance calculations represent the difference between FY2023-2024 actual expenditures and FY2022-2023 actual expenditures. Percentage changes are rounded to the nearest tenth of one percent. Figures marked with an asterisk (*) include supplemental appropriations approved by the General Assembly after the initial budget enactment date.

SECTION 3: NOTABLE BUDGET DEVELOPMENTS BY DEPARTMENT

3.1 Department of Human Services

The Department of Human Services (DHS) remained the Commonwealth's single largest spending department in FY2023-2024, accounting for 34.6 percent of total general fund expenditures at $14.76 billion. This represents an increase of $557.5 million over the prior year. The primary driver of growth was the Medical Assistance program, which saw enrollment increase by approximately 87,000 individuals between July 2023 and June 2024, largely attributable to the conclusion of federal continuous enrollment protections under the Families First Coronavirus Response Act. DHS Secretary Dr. Val Arkoosh reported that Medicaid redetermination processing costs alone accounted for $34.2 million in administrative expenditures not present in the prior fiscal year.

DHS actual expenditures came in $129.3 million below the appropriated level, reflecting successful implementation of managed care cost controls and a lower-than-projected average cost per enrollee in the HealthChoices program.

3.2 Department of Education

The Department of Education received its largest single-year funding increase in over a decade, with actual expenditures rising $354.1 million to $8.196 billion. This increase was directly tied to the Commonwealth Court's February 7, 2023 ruling in William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education, which found the state's school funding system unconstitutional. In response, the General Assembly directed an additional $567 million in basic education funding through Act 55 of 2024, though a portion of those funds were distributed through categorical grants not captured in the general fund comparison above.

The basic education subsidy line item alone totaled $7.03 billion in FY2023-2024, up from $6.71 billion in the prior year, an increase of $320 million or 4.8 percent.

3.3 Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs

The Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs (DDAP) recorded the highest percentage increase of any cabinet-level agency, with expenditures rising 11.9 percent from $62.4 million to $69.8 million. Secretary Dr. Latika Davis-Jones attributed the increase to expanded funding for naloxone distribution programs, which received $8.1 million in dedicated appropriations under Act 12 of 2024, and to a 22 percent increase in residential treatment bed subsidies. Pennsylvania recorded 5,158 drug overdose deaths in calendar year 2022 according to the Pennsylvania Coroners Association, and the expanded DDAP budget reflects the administration's stated commitment to reducing that figure by 15 percent by December 31, 2026.

3.4 Pennsylvania State Police

The Pennsylvania State Police budget grew by $75.8 million, or 5.3 percent, reaching $1.499 billion in FY2023-2024. The largest single expenditure driver was personnel costs, which account for 78.4 percent of the total PSP budget. A new collective bargaining agreement ratified on March 14, 2024 with the Pennsylvania State Troopers Association provided for a 4.25 percent base salary increase effective April 1, 2024, contributing an estimated $31.7 million in additional personnel costs in the final quarter of the fiscal year alone.

3.5 Department of Health

The Department of Health saw the second-highest percentage increase among major departments at 7.6 percent, with expenditures rising from $1.104 billion to $1.188 billion. Key expenditure increases included a $41.2 million expansion of the Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) outreach and enrollment activities, and $18.7 million in new funding for rural hospital infrastructure grants authorized under Act 7 of 2024. The department also absorbed $12.4 million in costs related to the statewide electronic health records interoperability initiative launched in January 2024.

SECTION 5: REVENUE CONTEXT

Total general fund revenues for FY2023-2024 were $44.1 billion, exceeding expenditures by approximately $1.4 billion and resulting in a year-end general fund balance of $3.2 billion as of June 30, 2024. Personal income tax collections totaled $16.8 billion, representing 38.1 percent of total revenues and an increase of 2.3 percent over FY2022-2023. Sales and use tax collections reached $12.4 billion, up 3.1 percent year-over-year. Corporate net income tax revenues declined by 6.7 percent to $5.1 billion, reflecting the first phase of the corporate tax rate reduction from 9.99 percent to 8.99 percent that took effect January 1, 2024 under Act 53 of 2022.

SECTION 6: FORWARD OUTLOOK

The Governor's proposed FY2024-2025 budget, submitted to the General Assembly on February 6, 2024, projects total general fund expenditures of $48.3 billion, an increase of $5.6 billion or 13.1 percent over FY2023-2024 actuals. The largest proposed increases are in the Department of Education ($1.1 billion) and the Department of Human Services ($890 million). Budget negotiations are ongoing as of the date of this report.
