EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania recorded total General Fund revenues of $11.84 billion in the third quarter of fiscal year 2023-2024 (Q3 FY2023-24), representing an increase of $1.23 billion, or 11.6 percent, compared to the $10.61 billion collected in the second quarter (Q2 FY2023-24). Total General Fund expenditures for Q3 reached $10.97 billion, a decrease of $412 million, or 3.6 percent, from the $11.38 billion recorded in Q2. As a result, the Commonwealth posted a quarterly operating surplus of $870 million in Q3, compared to a deficit of $770 million in Q2, representing a swing of $1.64 billion between the two periods.

The improved revenue performance in Q3 was driven primarily by stronger personal income tax collections, increased corporate net income tax receipts, and a seasonal uptick in sales and use tax revenues following the holiday retail period. The reduction in expenditures reflects the completion of several large one-time capital disbursements that occurred in Q2 and lower-than-anticipated Medicaid claims processing in January and February.

1.2 Personal Income Tax

Personal income tax (PIT) collections of $4.891 billion in Q3 represented the largest single revenue source and the strongest quarter-over-quarter growth among major tax categories. The 15.9 percent increase was attributable to two primary factors. First, the April 15 estimated payment deadline falls within Q3, capturing a significant volume of self-employed and investment-income filers. Second, year-end employer withholding reconciliations submitted in January contributed an estimated $318 million in additional receipts compared to the prior quarter. The PIT rate remains at 3.07 percent, unchanged since 2004.

1.3 Corporate Net Income Tax

Corporate net income tax (CNIT) collections rose 23.5 percent to $1.342 billion in Q3. This increase reflects the continued phase-down of the CNIT rate from 9.99 percent to 8.99 percent effective January 1, 2024, which paradoxically generated a short-term surge in estimated payments as corporations accelerated deductions in Q2 and remitted larger-than-expected Q3 installments. The Department of Revenue's Large Business Audit Division completed 47 audit settlements during Q3, generating $89 million in additional assessments and interest, compared to 31 settlements and $54 million in Q2.

1.4 Sales and Use Tax

Sales and use tax (SUT) collections declined 4.6 percent to $2.619 billion in Q3, following the elevated Q2 figure that captured holiday season retail activity in November and December 2023. The Q2 SUT total of $2.744 billion was the highest single-quarter SUT collection in the Commonwealth's history. The Q3 figure remains 3.2 percent above the Q3 FY2022-23 result of $2.537 billion, indicating underlying economic growth. Online marketplace facilitator collections, which became reportable under Act 13 of 2019, contributed $412 million in Q3, up from $388 million in Q2.

2.2 Department of Human Services

DHS expenditures declined $223 million, or 4.8 percent, to $4.389 billion in Q3. The primary driver was a reduction in Medical Assistance (Medicaid) fee-for-service claims, which fell from $1.87 billion in Q2 to $1.64 billion in Q3. Secretary Dr. Valerie Arkoosh attributed the reduction in part to the completion of the federally mandated Medicaid redetermination process, which concluded in Pennsylvania on December 31, 2023. Approximately 312,000 individuals were disenrolled from the program during the redetermination period, reducing the active Medical Assistance caseload from 3.6 million to 3.29 million enrollees as of January 1, 2024.

2.3 Pennsylvania Department of Transportation

PennDOT expenditures fell sharply by $217 million, or 18.0 percent, to $987 million in Q3. This decline reflects the completion of Q2 capital project disbursements, including a $178 million payment to the I-95 Philadelphia reconstruction project consortium in November 2023 and a $94 million disbursement for the Route 422 Schuylkill River bridge rehabilitation project in December 2023. Winter weather conditions also slowed construction activity in January and February, deferring approximately $65 million in planned project payments to Q4.

2.4 Department of Education

PDE expenditures increased modestly by $53 million, or 1.8 percent, to $2.944 billion in Q3. The increase reflects the distribution of the second installment of the Basic Education Funding subsidy to Pennsylvania's 500 school districts, totaling $1.89 billion, compared to $1.84 billion in Q2. The Q3 distribution incorporated the first year of adjustments under the Level Up supplement established by Act 55 of 2022, which directed an additional $100 million annually to the 100 lowest-wealth school districts.

SECTION 3: FUND BALANCE AND RESERVES

As of March 31, 2024, the General Fund unappropriated balance stood at $3.14 billion, compared to $2.27 billion as of December 31, 2023, an increase of $870 million consistent with the Q3 operating surplus. The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund) maintained its balance of $1.83 billion, unchanged from Q2, as no transfers were authorized by the General Assembly during the quarter.

The Commonwealth's total outstanding General Obligation bond debt as of March 31, 2024 was $10.87 billion, a reduction of $143 million from the $11.01 billion recorded at December 31, 2023, reflecting scheduled principal payments made in January 2024. The debt service coverage ratio for Q3 was 16.9 times, well above the 1.25 times minimum established in the Commonwealth's debt management policy.

SECTION 4: YEAR-TO-DATE PERFORMANCE AND OUTLOOK

Through the first three quarters of FY2023-24 (July 1, 2023 through March 31, 2024), total General Fund revenues of $32.18 billion are running 2.4 percent above the Governor's Office of the Budget's certified revenue estimate of $31.42 billion for the same period. Total expenditures of $32.08 billion are 1.1 percent below the year-to-date appropriation target of $32.44 billion.

Budget Secretary Uri Monson stated in the accompanying transmittal letter dated April 26, 2024, that the Commonwealth remains on track to close FY2023-24 with a positive General Fund balance, contingent on Q4 revenues meeting or exceeding the certified estimate of $10.89 billion. The primary risk to this projection is a potential softening in personal income tax withholding if regional employment conditions deteriorate, as Pennsylvania's seasonally adjusted unemployment rate of 3.4 percent as of February 2024 (per the Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry) remains near historic lows but showed a 0.2 percentage point increase from December 2023.

SECTION 5: NOTES AND METHODOLOGY

All figures in this report are presented on a cash basis consistent with the Commonwealth's Comprehensive Annual Financial Report methodology. Intergovernmental transfers between the General Fund and special funds, including the Motor License Fund and the Lottery Fund, are excluded from revenue and expenditure totals to avoid double-counting. Federal reimbursements received through the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services are recorded as revenues in the period of receipt.

Comparative figures for Q2 FY2023-24 have been restated in two instances: (1) a $22 million reclassification of inheritance tax receipts from Other Revenues to the Inheritance and Estate Tax line, and (2) a $31 million correction to DHS expenditures related to a duplicate payment entry identified during the January 2024 internal audit cycle. These restatements do not affect the previously reported Q2 total revenue or total expenditure figures, as they represent internal reclassifications only.
