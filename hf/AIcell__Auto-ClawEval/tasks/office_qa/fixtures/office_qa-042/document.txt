EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comparative analysis of departmental appropriations and actual expenditures for the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania across the fiscal years ending June 30, 2023, and June 30, 2024. The total General Fund budget for FY 2023–2024 was $45.8 billion, representing an increase of $2.1 billion, or 4.8 percent, over the FY 2022–2023 enacted budget of $43.7 billion. This document was prepared under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and is intended to provide legislators, agency directors, and the public with a transparent accounting of how Commonwealth resources were allocated and spent during the comparative period.

Overall, 14 of 22 major departments came in at or under their approved appropriations for FY 2023–2024. The remaining 8 departments recorded expenditures exceeding their appropriations, with the Department of Human Services accounting for the largest overage in absolute dollar terms.

SECTION 1: METHODOLOGY AND SCOPE

This comparison is based on data extracted from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES) as of September 30, 2024. Figures reflect final audited expenditures for FY 2022–2023 and preliminary (unaudited) expenditures for FY 2023–2024. Final audited figures for FY 2023–2024 are expected to be published by the Auditor General's office no later than March 31, 2025.

All figures are presented in millions of U.S. dollars unless otherwise noted. Appropriations reflect enacted budgets as signed by Governor Josh Shapiro. Supplemental appropriations approved by the General Assembly after the initial budget enactment are included in the "Revised Appropriation" column. Federal funds administered through state departments are excluded from General Fund totals but are noted where relevant.

SECTION 3: NOTABLE FINDINGS AND ANALYSIS

3.1 Department of Human Services — Medicaid Expenditure Pressure

The Department of Human Services recorded the largest budget overage in FY 2023–2024, spending $342.6 million above its approved appropriation of $14.905 billion. The primary driver was an unanticipated 7.3 percent increase in Medical Assistance (Medicaid) enrollment, which grew from approximately 3.1 million enrollees in July 2023 to 3.32 million enrollees by June 2024. Secretary Val Arkoosh attributed the overage primarily to the conclusion of the federal continuous enrollment requirement under the Families First Coronavirus Response Act, which resulted in redetermination backlogs and temporary coverage extensions that were costlier than projected. A supplemental appropriation of $200 million was approved by the General Assembly on March 14, 2024, partially offsetting the shortfall.

3.2 Department of Education — Basic Education Funding Increase

The Department of Education received its largest single-year appropriation increase in over a decade, rising $471.4 million in basic education funding alone. This increase was a direct result of the Commonwealth Court's February 2023 ruling in William Penn School District et al. v. Pennsylvania Department of Education, which found the existing school funding system unconstitutional. The department finished $24.3 million under its revised appropriation, reflecting conservative spending patterns in newly established grant programs that had not yet reached full disbursement capacity by June 30, 2024.

3.3 Department of Corrections — Population and Staffing Costs

The Department of Corrections exceeded its appropriation by $83.3 million, driven by two concurrent pressures: a 4.1 percent increase in the incarcerated population (from 37,400 to 38,930 individuals between July 2023 and June 2024) and mandatory overtime costs totaling $61.7 million, which were 22 percent higher than the $50.6 million projected at the time of budget enactment. Secretary Laurel Harry reported that 14 of the Commonwealth's 23 state correctional institutions recorded overtime expenditures above their individual facility projections.

3.4 Department of Health — Behavioral Health Initiative

The Department of Health's 8.4 percent year-over-year budget increase was the largest percentage growth among major departments. Of the $2.043 billion appropriation, $187.5 million was designated for the newly established Behavioral Health and Opioid Response Initiative, announced by Governor Shapiro on August 22, 2023. The department spent $155.7 million of those designated funds by fiscal year-end, leaving $31.8 million in unspent appropriations that will be carried forward under a legislative authorization approved on June 27, 2024.

SECTION 5: RECOMMENDATIONS

The Bureau of Financial Management offers the following recommendations for the FY 2024–2025 budget cycle:

1. The Department of Human Services should submit a revised Medicaid enrollment projection methodology to the Office of the Budget no later than December 1, 2024, incorporating post-redetermination enrollment trend data.

2. The Department of Corrections should present a staffing optimization plan by January 15, 2025, addressing the structural overtime cost drivers identified in this report.

3. All departments with unspent appropriations exceeding $20 million should submit written justifications and revised spending timelines to the Office of the Budget by November 30, 2024.

4. The Office of Administration should complete its planned upgrade of the IES financial reporting module by February 28, 2025, to improve real-time expenditure tracking and reduce the lag between transaction recording and management reporting.
