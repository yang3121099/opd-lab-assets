Federal Highway Trust Fund: Compound Annual Growth Rate Analysis

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive compound annual growth rate (CAGR) analysis of the Federal Highway Trust Fund (HTF) for the ten-year period spanning fiscal year 2014 through fiscal year 2024. The analysis encompasses both the Highway Account and the Mass Transit Account, examining revenue inflows, expenditure patterns, and net balance trajectories. The findings are intended to inform congressional budget deliberations, support actuarial assessments, and provide transparency to the American public regarding the long-term fiscal health of one of the federal government's most critical infrastructure financing mechanisms.

The Highway Trust Fund recorded a total balance of $52.3 billion as of September 30, 2024, compared to $15.4 billion on September 30, 2014. This represents a ten-year CAGR of 12.97 percent, driven primarily by legislative transfers authorized under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act of 2021 (Public Law 117-58) and sustained growth in motor fuel excise tax receipts.

---

SECTION 1: BACKGROUND AND FUND STRUCTURE

The Federal Highway Trust Fund was established under the Highway Revenue Act of 1956 and is funded primarily through excise taxes on motor fuels, truck and trailer sales, heavy vehicle use taxes, and tire taxes. The Fund is divided into two accounts:

1. The Highway Account, which finances federal-aid highway programs administered by the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA).
2. The Mass Transit Account, which finances transit capital and operating assistance programs administered by the Federal Transit Administration (FTA).

As of fiscal year 2024, the federal excise tax on gasoline stands at 18.4 cents per gallon, a rate that has remained unchanged since 1993. Diesel fuel is taxed at 24.4 cents per gallon. These rates form the primary organic revenue base for the Fund, though their purchasing power has eroded substantially due to inflation and improvements in vehicle fuel efficiency.

---

SECTION 2: METHODOLOGY FOR CAGR COMPUTATION

The compound annual growth rate was calculated using the standard financial formula:

CAGR = [(Ending Value / Beginning Value) ^ (1 / Number of Years)] - 1

For the primary ten-year analysis:
- Beginning Value (FY2014 closing balance): $15,400,000,000
- Ending Value (FY2024 closing balance): $52,300,000,000
- Number of Years: 10

CAGR = [($52,300,000,000 / $15,400,000,000) ^ (1/10)] - 1
CAGR = [3.3961] ^ (0.10) - 1
CAGR = 1.1297 - 1
CAGR = 0.1297, or 12.97 percent

Separate CAGR computations were performed for the Highway Account and Mass Transit Account individually, as well as for revenue receipts and disbursements independently. All figures are presented in nominal dollars unless otherwise noted. Inflation-adjusted (real) CAGR figures using the Bureau of Economic Analysis GDP deflator are provided in Section 4.

SECTION 4: REVENUE AND DISBURSEMENT CAGR ANALYSIS

4.1 Revenue Receipts

Total revenue deposited into the Highway Trust Fund in FY2014 was $39.7 billion. By FY2024, total receipts reached $53.1 billion, yielding a ten-year revenue CAGR of 2.95 percent. This modest growth rate reflects the static nature of the per-gallon excise tax rates and the gradual improvement in average vehicle fuel economy from 24.1 miles per gallon in 2014 to 28.6 miles per gallon in 2024, as reported by the Environmental Protection Agency.

Organic tax receipts (excluding general fund transfers) grew from $39.7 billion in FY2014 to $46.8 billion in FY2024, a CAGR of 1.65 percent. This figure underscores the structural revenue challenge facing the Fund as electric vehicle adoption accelerates and fuel efficiency standards tighten.

4.2 Disbursements

Total disbursements from the Highway Trust Fund in FY2014 were $50.1 billion. By FY2024, disbursements reached $57.4 billion, representing a CAGR of 1.37 percent. The slower growth in disbursements relative to the balance increase reflects the infusion of general fund transfers that have temporarily stabilized the Fund's solvency position.

4.3 Inflation-Adjusted CAGR

Using the GDP deflator, with FY2014 as the base year (index = 100) and FY2024 index at 128.4, the real CAGR of the total HTF balance is calculated as follows:

Real FY2024 Balance = $52,300 million / 1.284 = $40,732 million
Real CAGR = [($40,732 / $15,400) ^ (1/10)] - 1 = 10.22 percent

The real CAGR of 10.22 percent still reflects substantial growth, attributable almost entirely to the legislative transfers rather than organic revenue performance.

SECTION 5: ACCOUNT-LEVEL CAGR COMPARISON

Highway Account:
- FY2014 Balance: $10,840 million
- FY2024 Balance: $39,480 million
- Ten-Year CAGR: 13.80 percent

Mass Transit Account:
- FY2014 Balance: $4,560 million
- FY2024 Balance: $12,820 million
- Ten-Year CAGR: 10.90 percent

The Highway Account outpaced the Mass Transit Account in CAGR terms by 290 basis points over the ten-year period. This differential reflects the proportional allocation of Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act transfers, which directed approximately 80 percent of HTF-related transfers to the Highway Account and 20 percent to the Mass Transit Account, consistent with the historical funding split established under the Safe, Accountable, Flexible, Efficient Transportation Equity Act of 2005.

---

SECTION 6: FIVE-YEAR SUB-PERIOD ANALYSIS

To assess whether growth was consistent across the decade or concentrated in specific periods, the ten-year window was divided into two five-year sub-periods.

Sub-Period 1: FY2014 to FY2019
- Beginning Balance: $15,400 million
- Ending Balance: $24,270 million
- Five-Year CAGR: 9.51 percent

Sub-Period 2: FY2019 to FY2024
- Beginning Balance: $24,270 million
- Ending Balance: $52,300 million
- Five-Year CAGR: 16.59 percent

The acceleration in the second sub-period is directly attributable to the $118 billion in general fund transfers authorized beginning in FY2022. Without these transfers, the Congressional Budget Office projects the Fund would have reached insolvency by the fourth quarter of FY2022, based on the CBO's July 2021 baseline projections published in report number 57218.

---

SECTION 7: FORWARD PROJECTIONS AND SUSTAINABILITY ASSESSMENT

The Office of Management and Budget projects that organic HTF revenues will grow at a CAGR of approximately 0.8 percent annually through FY2030, reflecting continued improvements in fuel economy and early-stage electric vehicle market penetration. The current authorization under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act expires on September 30, 2026.

Without additional legislative action, the Fund's balance is projected to decline from $52.3 billion in FY2024 to approximately $18.7 billion by FY2030, representing a negative CAGR of -16.4 percent over that six-year period. The Highway Account is projected to reach a zero balance by the second quarter of FY2028 under this scenario.

The Division of Trust Fund Accounting and Analysis recommends that Congress consider indexing the motor fuel excise tax to inflation or transitioning to a vehicle miles traveled fee structure to restore long-term organic revenue sustainability.
