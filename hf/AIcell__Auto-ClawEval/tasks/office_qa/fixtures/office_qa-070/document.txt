EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a five-year analysis of Commonwealth expenditure patterns across all major appropriation categories for fiscal years 2019-2020 through 2023-2024. Total General Fund expenditures grew from $34.1 billion in FY 2019-2020 to $44.7 billion in FY 2023-2024, representing a cumulative increase of 31.1 percent over the five-year period. The average annual growth rate of 5.6 percent outpaced the state's average inflation rate of 4.1 percent during the same period, indicating real expenditure growth of approximately 1.5 percentage points annually.

Secretary of the Budget Uri Monson directed this analysis in response to a legislative request from the House Appropriations Committee, chaired by Representative Seth Grove, following the passage of Act 54 of 2024, which mandated enhanced fiscal transparency reporting for all executive branch agencies.

SECTION 2: EXPENDITURE BY MAJOR CATEGORY

2.1 Human Services and Medicaid

Human Services expenditures represent the single largest category of General Fund spending, accounting for $18.9 billion in FY 2023-2024, or 42.3 percent of total General Fund outlays. The Department of Human Services (DHS), led by Secretary Dr. Val Arkoosh, administered $14.2 billion of this total through the Medical Assistance program alone.

Key findings in this category:

- Medical Assistance enrollment grew from 2.87 million recipients in FY 2019-2020 to 3.41 million in FY 2023-2024, an increase of 18.8 percent
- Per-enrollee costs rose from $8,940 to $10,760 over the same period, a 20.4 percent increase
- Long-term care expenditures within Medical Assistance totaled $4.1 billion in FY 2023-2024, representing 28.9 percent of total Medical Assistance spending
- The Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) served 176,000 children at a cost of $412 million in FY 2023-2024

The federal government reimbursed 54.7 percent of total Medical Assistance costs in FY 2023-2024 through the Federal Medical Assistance Percentage (FMAP), compared to 51.2 percent in FY 2019-2020. This improvement in the federal match rate saved the Commonwealth an estimated $890 million in state funds during FY 2023-2024.

2.2 Education

Total education-related expenditures from the General Fund reached $14.1 billion in FY 2023-2024, representing 31.5 percent of total spending. This category encompasses Pre-K through 12 public education, higher education subsidies, and special education funding.

Basic Education Funding (BEF) allocations to school districts totaled $7.8 billion in FY 2023-2024, an increase of $567 million or 7.8 percent over the prior year. This increase followed the landmark Commonwealth Court ruling in William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education, issued February 7, 2023, which found the existing school funding system unconstitutional. In response, the General Assembly approved an additional $567 million in BEF through Act 55 of 2023.

Higher education subsidies declined as a share of total education spending:

- State System of Higher Education (PASSHE) received $477 million in FY 2023-2024, down from $552 million in FY 2019-2020, a reduction of 13.6 percent
- University of Pittsburgh received $153.4 million in state appropriations in FY 2023-2024
- Temple University received $158.7 million in state appropriations in FY 2023-2024
- Penn State University received $242.6 million in state appropriations in FY 2023-2024

Special education funding grew to $1.24 billion in FY 2023-2024, a 34.8 percent increase from the $920 million appropriated in FY 2019-2020.

2.3 Corrections and Public Safety

The Department of Corrections, under Secretary Dr. Laurel Harry, expended $2.87 billion in FY 2023-2024, representing 6.4 percent of General Fund spending. This reflects a modest increase of 3.1 percent from the prior year's $2.78 billion.

Notable trends in this category:

- The state prison population declined from 45,200 inmates in FY 2019-2020 to 38,700 in FY 2023-2024, a reduction of 14.4 percent
- Despite declining population, per-inmate costs rose from $42,800 to $74,100 annually, driven primarily by healthcare costs and staffing shortages requiring overtime expenditures
- Overtime costs alone totaled $287 million in FY 2023-2024, compared to $164 million in FY 2019-2020, an increase of 75.0 percent
- The Pennsylvania State Police budget grew from $1.31 billion in FY 2019-2020 to $1.68 billion in FY 2023-2024, a 28.2 percent increase

2.4 Debt Service

General obligation debt service expenditures totaled $1.02 billion in FY 2023-2024, representing 2.3 percent of total General Fund spending. The Commonwealth's total outstanding general obligation debt stood at $11.4 billion as of June 30, 2024. The average interest rate on outstanding bonds was 3.87 percent, reflecting the favorable refinancing environment of 2020 and 2021 when the Commonwealth refinanced $2.1 billion in higher-rate obligations.

SECTION 3: WORKFORCE AND PERSONNEL COSTS

Personnel expenditures across all General Fund agencies totaled $8.3 billion in FY 2023-2024, representing 18.6 percent of total spending. This includes salaries, wages, and employer-paid benefits for approximately 78,400 full-time equivalent (FTE) employees.

The Commonwealth's workforce declined from 83,200 FTEs in FY 2019-2020 to 78,400 in FY 2023-2024, a reduction of 5.8 percent. Despite this workforce reduction, total personnel costs increased by $1.1 billion or 15.3 percent over the five-year period, driven by:

- Negotiated wage increases averaging 3.2 percent annually under collective bargaining agreements with AFSCME Council 13 and other employee organizations
- Healthcare benefit costs per employee rising from $18,400 to $23,700 annually, a 28.8 percent increase
- Pension contribution rates increasing from 34.1 percent of payroll in FY 2019-2020 to 36.8 percent in FY 2023-2024 under the Public School Employees' Retirement System (PSERS) and State Employees' Retirement System (SERS) funding schedules

SECTION 4: CAPITAL EXPENDITURES AND INFRASTRUCTURE

Capital Budget expenditures, funded separately from the General Fund through bond proceeds, totaled $1.87 billion in FY 2023-2024. The Department of General Services administered $643 million in facility renovation and construction projects, while PennDOT managed $8.4 billion in total transportation expenditures when combining state and federal highway funds.

The Multimodal Transportation Fund, established under Act 89 of 2013, distributed $144 million to local governments and transit agencies in FY 2023-2024 for rail, port, aviation, and bicycle infrastructure improvements.

SECTION 5: OUTLOOK AND PROJECTIONS

The Independent Fiscal Office (IFO), directed by Matthew Knittel, projects General Fund expenditures will reach $47.2 billion in FY 2024-2025, representing a 5.6 percent increase. Key pressure points include:

- Medical Assistance costs projected to grow 6.8 percent due to continued enrollment growth and pharmaceutical cost inflation
- Pension contributions expected to remain elevated through FY 2027-2028 before declining as the PSERS and SERS funding schedules mature
- Education funding increases mandated by the ongoing legislative response to the William Penn court decision, with the Governor's office estimating a $4.5 billion total increase in BEF over seven years will be required to achieve constitutional compliance

The Commonwealth's General Fund reserve balance stood at $3.6 billion as of June 30, 2024, representing 8.1 percent of annual expenditures, above the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) recommended minimum of 5.0 percent.

METHODOLOGY NOTE

All expenditure figures are reported on a modified accrual basis consistent with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) as applied to governmental funds. Data was compiled from the Commonwealth's SAP-based financial management system (known as the Commonwealth Financial System, or CFS) and reconciled against the Comprehensive Annual Financial Report (CAFR) prepared by the Office of the Comptroller. Figures may not sum precisely due to rounding. Inquiries regarding this report should be directed to the Bureau of Financial Management at (717) 787-6960.
