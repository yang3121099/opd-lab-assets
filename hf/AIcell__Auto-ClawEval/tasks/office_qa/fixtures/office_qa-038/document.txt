EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Total state tax revenue for fiscal year 2023 reached $29.4 billion, representing a decline of 3.8 percent from the fiscal year 2022 total of $30.6 billion. This marks the first year-over-year decrease in state tax collections since fiscal year 2010, when revenues fell 6.2 percent during the aftermath of the Great Recession. Despite this contraction, collections remain 18.7 percent above pre-pandemic levels recorded in fiscal year 2019 ($24.8 billion), reflecting structural growth in the state's tax base over the intervening period.

The decline is attributable primarily to reduced individual income tax receipts driven by lower capital gains realizations, a softening in consumer spending affecting sales tax collections, and a one-time corporate income tax adjustment resulting from federal conformity changes enacted under the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022.

SECTION 1: REVENUE BY MAJOR TAX CATEGORY

1.1 Individual Income Tax

Individual income tax remains the single largest source of state revenue, accounting for $14.2 billion in fiscal year 2023, or 48.3 percent of total tax collections. This represents a decrease of $1.1 billion (7.2 percent) from the fiscal year 2022 total of $15.3 billion.

The primary driver of this decline was a 34.6 percent reduction in capital gains income reported on Minnesota returns. In calendar year 2021, capital gains realizations reached a record $28.9 billion statewide, fueled by elevated asset prices and pandemic-era portfolio activity. By calendar year 2022, realizations fell to $18.9 billion as equity markets contracted and real estate transaction volumes declined.

Wage and salary withholding, which constitutes the most stable component of income tax revenue, grew by 4.1 percent to $10.7 billion, consistent with statewide employment growth of 2.3 percent and average wage increases of 5.4 percent reported by the Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development for calendar year 2022.

Minnesota's top individual income tax rate of 9.85 percent, which applies to taxable income above $183,340 for single filers and $304,970 for married filing jointly, generated approximately $2.3 billion from the top bracket alone.

1.2 Sales and Use Tax

General sales and use tax collections totaled $8.1 billion in fiscal year 2023, a modest decrease of $210 million (2.5 percent) from the $8.31 billion collected in fiscal year 2022. Minnesota's general sales tax rate of 6.875 percent remained unchanged throughout the fiscal year.

Consumer spending on taxable goods softened in the second half of fiscal year 2023 as inflation eroded purchasing power and households shifted expenditures toward services, which are largely exempt from Minnesota sales tax. Motor vehicle sales taxes, collected separately at a rate of 6.5 percent, generated $912 million, down 8.3 percent from $994 million in the prior year, reflecting a 9.1 percent decline in new vehicle registrations statewide.

The ten highest-revenue sales tax categories for fiscal year 2023 were:

  Motor vehicles and parts: $1.84 billion
  Food and beverages (taxable): $1.21 billion
  General merchandise retail: $1.09 billion
  Building materials and construction: $876 million
  Clothing and apparel: $743 million
  Electronics and appliances: $618 million
  Restaurants and food service: $591 million
  Fuel and petroleum products: $487 million
  Telecommunications: $342 million
  Recreational equipment and sporting goods: $298 million

1.3 Corporate Franchise Tax

Corporate franchise (income) tax collections totaled $3.8 billion in fiscal year 2023, a decrease of $640 million (14.4 percent) from the record $4.44 billion collected in fiscal year 2022. Minnesota imposes a flat corporate income tax rate of 9.8 percent on net income apportioned to the state.

The decline reflects two primary factors. First, corporate profit margins compressed nationally as input costs remained elevated and consumer demand moderated. Second, a one-time adjustment of approximately $310 million resulted from Minnesota's partial conformity to federal bonus depreciation provisions under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, which allowed accelerated deductions that reduced taxable income for a significant number of large filers.

The top ten corporate taxpayers in Minnesota, whose identities are protected under Minnesota Statute 270B.02 but whose aggregate contribution is publicly reportable, accounted for $618 million in corporate franchise tax payments, representing 16.3 percent of total corporate collections.

1.4 Statewide Property Tax

The statewide general property tax, levied on commercial-industrial and seasonal recreational properties, generated $897 million in fiscal year 2023, an increase of $43 million (5.0 percent) from the prior year. This levy is set annually by the Legislature and does not fluctuate with market values in the same manner as local property taxes. The fiscal year 2023 levy was established at $896.8 million under the provisions of the 2021 Tax Bill (Chapter 14, Article 6).

1.5 Other Tax Sources

Remaining tax revenues totaling $2.4 billion were collected from the following sources:

  Statewide motor fuels tax (28.5 cents per gallon): $891 million
  Cigarette and tobacco taxes: $412 million
  Alcoholic beverage taxes: $118 million
  Insurance premiums tax: $634 million
  Deed and mortgage registry taxes: $198 million
  Lawful gambling taxes: $147 million

SECTION 2: COMPARISON TO FORECAST

The February 2023 Budget and Economic Forecast, prepared by Minnesota Management and Budget under the direction of Commissioner Erin Campbell, projected total fiscal year 2023 tax revenues of $29.1 billion. Actual collections exceeded this forecast by $300 million (1.0 percent), driven primarily by stronger-than-anticipated withholding tax receipts in the final quarter of the fiscal year (April through June 2023).

Individual income tax collections came in $180 million above forecast. Corporate franchise tax collections were $95 million above forecast. Sales tax collections were $25 million below forecast, the only major category to underperform projections.

SECTION 3: FIVE-YEAR REVENUE TREND

The following table presents total state tax revenue for fiscal years 2019 through 2023:

Fiscal Year | Total Revenue | Year-Over-Year Change
FY 2019     | $24.8 billion | +3.2%
FY 2020     | $23.9 billion | -3.6%
FY 2021     | $26.7 billion | +11.7%
FY 2022     | $30.6 billion | +14.6%

The compound annual growth rate of total tax revenue from fiscal year 2019 to fiscal year 2023 is 4.3 percent, outpacing the average annual rate of inflation (as measured by the Consumer Price Index for the Minneapolis-St. Paul metropolitan area) of 3.7 percent over the same period.

SECTION 4: COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT

The Department of Revenue conducted 14,847 individual income tax audits in fiscal year 2023, resulting in $312 million in additional assessments. The audit closure rate was 94.3 percent, and the average assessment per closed audit was $22,200. Corporate audit activity yielded an additional $187 million in assessments from 1,204 completed examinations.

The voluntary compliance rate for individual income tax filers, defined as the percentage of tax legally owed that is paid without enforcement action, was estimated at 87.4 percent for tax year 2021 (the most recent year for which complete data is available), consistent with the national average of 85.0 percent reported by the Internal Revenue Service in its most recent Tax Gap study.

Delinquent tax collections, managed by the Collections Division under Director Tamara Gustafson, recovered $743 million in fiscal year 2023, a 6.2 percent increase from $699 million in fiscal year 2022. The total outstanding delinquent tax balance as of June 30, 2023 stood at $1.84 billion.

SECTION 5: OUTLOOK FOR FISCAL YEAR 2024

The November 2023 Budget and Economic Forecast, scheduled for release on November 30, 2023, will provide updated projections for fiscal year 2024. Based on preliminary economic indicators available as of the publication date of this report, the Revenue Research Division anticipates the following conditions will shape fiscal year 2024 collections:

- Continued wage growth, projected at 3.8 percent statewide, supporting stable withholding tax receipts
- Modest recovery in capital gains realizations as equity markets stabilized in the first half of calendar year 2023
- Continued softness in consumer durable goods spending, partially offset by growth in taxable services if the Legislature acts on pending proposals to expand the sales tax base
- Corporate profit normalization following the one-time depreciation adjustments that suppressed fiscal year 2023 collections

The Division's preliminary internal estimate projects total fiscal year 2024 tax revenues in the range of $29.8 billion to $30.5 billion, representing growth of 1.4 to 3.7 percent over fiscal year 2023 actuals.
