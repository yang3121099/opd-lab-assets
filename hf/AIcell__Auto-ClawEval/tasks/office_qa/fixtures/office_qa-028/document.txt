EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Internal Revenue Service, in coordination with the Office of Management and Budget and the Bureau of Fiscal Service, presents this comprehensive analysis of federal tax revenue collections for Fiscal Year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023). Total federal tax revenues reached $4.44 trillion during this period, representing a decline of 9.3 percent from the record $4.90 trillion collected in Fiscal Year 2022. This report examines revenue trends across all major tax categories, compares performance against Congressional Budget Office projections, and identifies key factors contributing to year-over-year changes.

This analysis was prepared under the direction of IRS Commissioner Daniel Werfel, who assumed leadership of the agency on April 4, 2023, and reflects data compiled by the Statistics of Income Division in coordination with the Treasury's Office of Tax Analysis.

SECTION 1: TOTAL REVENUE OVERVIEW

Federal tax collections for FY2023 totaled $4.44 trillion, down from $4.90 trillion in FY2022 and $4.05 trillion in FY2021. The FY2023 figure represents 16.5 percent of Gross Domestic Product, compared to 19.6 percent in FY2022. The Congressional Budget Office had projected FY2023 revenues of $4.71 trillion in its January 2023 baseline, meaning actual collections fell $270 billion, or 5.7 percent, below that projection.

The primary driver of the year-over-year decline was a significant reduction in individual income tax receipts, which fell by $456 billion compared to FY2022. This decrease was largely attributable to lower capital gains realizations as equity markets declined, reduced bonus and stock compensation payouts in the technology sector, and the expiration of certain pandemic-era provisions that had temporarily elevated prior-year collections.

SECTION 3: INDIVIDUAL INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Individual income taxes remained the largest single source of federal revenue at $2.176 trillion, constituting 49.0 percent of total collections. Despite the year-over-year decline, this figure remains 6.5 percent above the FY2021 level and 34.2 percent above the pre-pandemic FY2019 level of $1.620 trillion.

Key contributing factors to the FY2023 decline include:

- Capital gains realizations fell an estimated 35 percent in calendar year 2022 (which flows into FY2023 filing season), driven by a 19.4 percent decline in the S&P 500 during calendar year 2022. The IRS estimates this reduced capital gains tax receipts by approximately $180 billion compared to the prior year.

- Withheld income and employment taxes, by contrast, grew by 4.2 percent, reflecting continued labor market strength. Average weekly earnings rose 4.4 percent year-over-year through September 2023 according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data.

- Non-withheld payments, which include estimated tax payments and final settlement payments, declined by $512 billion or 38.7 percent, reflecting both lower capital gains and reduced pass-through business income among high-income filers.

- Refunds issued during FY2023 totaled $659 billion, a decrease of $42 billion from the $701 billion issued in FY2022, partially offsetting the gross receipt decline.

The top 1 percent of income earners, defined as those with adjusted gross income above approximately $548,000 based on the most recent Statistics of Income data, accounted for an estimated 45.8 percent of total individual income tax liability.

SECTION 4: PAYROLL TAX PERFORMANCE

Payroll taxes, which fund Social Security and Medicare programs, reached a record $1.614 trillion in FY2023, surpassing the prior record of $1.484 trillion set in FY2022. This 8.8 percent increase reflects strong employment conditions throughout the fiscal year.

The Social Security taxable wage base increased from $147,000 in calendar year 2022 to $160,200 in calendar year 2023, a 8.98 percent increase representing the largest single-year adjustment since 1983. This change alone is estimated to have generated an additional $22 billion in Social Security tax receipts.

Total nonfarm payroll employment averaged 155.9 million jobs during FY2023, compared to 151.4 million in FY2022. The unemployment rate averaged 3.6 percent during the fiscal year, remaining near historically low levels.

Medicare tax receipts, including the 0.9 percent Additional Medicare Tax applicable to earnings above $200,000 for single filers, totaled $387 billion, up 7.1 percent from $361 billion in FY2022.

SECTION 5: CORPORATE INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Corporate income tax receipts totaled $420 billion in FY2023, a modest decline of $5 billion or 1.2 percent from the $425 billion collected in FY2022. The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, signed into law on August 16, 2022, introduced a 15 percent Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT) applicable to corporations with average annual adjusted financial statement income exceeding $1 billion. The CAMT became effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2022, and is estimated to have generated approximately $4.8 billion in additional receipts during FY2023, its first partial year of implementation.

The statutory corporate income tax rate remains 21 percent, as established by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of December 2017. The effective average corporate tax rate for FY2023 is estimated at 14.2 percent based on preliminary data, compared to 14.8 percent in FY2022.

S&P 500 corporate profits before tax declined approximately 4.6 percent in calendar year 2022, contributing to lower estimated tax payments flowing into the early months of FY2023. However, corporate profits recovered through the second half of FY2023, limiting the overall annual decline.

SECTION 6: EXCISE TAXES AND OTHER RECEIPTS

Excise tax collections totaled $87 billion in FY2023, a decrease of $2 billion from FY2022. Highway-related excise taxes, including the federal gasoline tax of 18.4 cents per gallon and diesel tax of 24.4 cents per gallon, contributed $40.3 billion. Aviation excise taxes generated $17.1 billion, reflecting a full recovery in air travel demand to pre-pandemic levels. Tobacco excise taxes continued their long-term structural decline, generating $12.1 billion compared to $13.0 billion in FY2022.

Customs duties fell to $77 billion from $99 billion in FY2022, a 22.2 percent decline, primarily reflecting reduced import volumes as consumer goods demand normalized following the pandemic-era surge and as certain Section 301 tariff exclusions were extended.

Estate and gift tax receipts increased modestly to $34 billion, up from $33 billion in FY2022. The basic exclusion amount for estate tax purposes was $12.92 million per individual in calendar year 2023, up from $12.06 million in 2022.

SECTION 7: ENFORCEMENT AND COMPLIANCE REVENUE

The Inflation Reduction Act provided the IRS with $79.6 billion in supplemental funding over ten years, of which $45.6 billion was designated for enforcement activities. During FY2023, the IRS collected $63.1 billion through enforcement activities including audits, collections, and document matching programs, compared to $60.2 billion in FY2022.

The overall individual audit rate for FY2022 returns (the most recent year for which complete data is available) was 0.49 percent, up slightly from 0.38 percent in FY2021 but still well below the 0.90 percent rate recorded in FY2010. The IRS processed 271.5 million tax returns during FY2023 and issued 93.4 million refunds totaling $659 billion, with an average refund of $3,167 for individual filers.

SECTION 8: OUTLOOK FOR FISCAL YEAR 2024

The Congressional Budget Office's February 2024 baseline projects FY2024 federal revenues of $4.93 trillion, representing an 11.0 percent increase over FY2023 actuals. This projection assumes continued labor market strength, a partial recovery in capital gains realizations, and the first full year of CAMT collections. Individual income taxes are projected to recover to $2.45 trillion, payroll taxes are expected to reach $1.68 trillion, and corporate income taxes are projected at $0.46 trillion.

Key risks to the FY2024 outlook include potential equity market volatility, the trajectory of interest rates as the Federal Reserve navigates monetary policy, and the degree to which the IRS successfully implements its enhanced enforcement capabilities funded under the Inflation Reduction Act.
