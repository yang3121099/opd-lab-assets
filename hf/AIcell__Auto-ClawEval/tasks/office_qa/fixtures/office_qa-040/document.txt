EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of Commonwealth expenditure patterns across all major appropriation categories for fiscal years 2019 through 2024. Total general fund expenditures grew from $32.7 billion in FY2019 to $44.1 billion in FY2024, representing a cumulative increase of 34.9 percent over the five-year period. The average annual growth rate of 6.2 percent exceeded the state's average inflation rate of 4.1 percent during the same period, indicating real expenditure growth of approximately 2.1 percent annually. This analysis was prepared under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and reflects data certified by the Office of the Comptroller as of August 31, 2024.

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF TOTAL EXPENDITURES

General fund spending has increased in each of the five fiscal years under review. The most significant single-year increase occurred between FY2021 and FY2022, when expenditures rose by $3.4 billion, or 9.8 percent, driven primarily by pandemic recovery appropriations and expanded Medicaid enrollment. The smallest year-over-year increase was recorded between FY2019 and FY2020, when spending grew by only $612 million, or 1.9 percent, as the Commonwealth enacted a partial-year budget in response to revenue uncertainty during the early months of the COVID-19 pandemic.

SECTION 2: EXPENDITURE BY MAJOR CATEGORY

2.1 Health and Human Services

The Department of Human Services (DHS) remains the single largest recipient of general fund appropriations, accounting for $18.3 billion in FY2024, or 41.5 percent of total general fund spending. This represents an increase of $5.9 billion from the $12.4 billion appropriated in FY2019, a cumulative growth rate of 47.6 percent. The primary driver of this growth has been the Medical Assistance (Medicaid) program, which expanded enrollment from 2.87 million recipients in FY2019 to 3.54 million recipients in FY2024, an increase of 23.3 percent. The average cost per Medicaid enrollee rose from $7,210 in FY2019 to $9,840 in FY2024.

Long-term care expenditures within DHS grew at a particularly elevated rate, increasing from $3.1 billion in FY2019 to $4.8 billion in FY2024. The Office of Long-Term Living administered 94,700 nursing facility residents and 162,000 home and community-based waiver participants as of June 30, 2024.

2.2 Education

The Department of Education received $14.2 billion in FY2024, representing 32.2 percent of total general fund expenditures. This compares to $11.8 billion in FY2019, a cumulative increase of 20.3 percent. The Basic Education Funding formula, revised under Act 55 of 2023, directed an additional $567 million to the 100 lowest-wealth school districts beginning in FY2024. The Pennsylvania State System of Higher Education (PASSHE) received $477 million in FY2024, up from $431 million in FY2019.

Pre-kindergarten funding through the Keystone STARS program increased from $68 million in FY2019 to $116 million in FY2024, a 70.6 percent increase, reflecting the administration's stated priority of expanding early childhood access.

2.3 Public Safety and Corrections

The Department of Corrections (DOC) expended $2.96 billion in FY2024, compared to $2.71 billion in FY2019, an increase of 9.2 percent. The daily cost per inmate rose from $107.43 in FY2019 to $128.67 in FY2024. The state prison population declined from 47,200 in FY2019 to 38,900 in FY2024, a reduction of 17.6 percent, which partially offset per-unit cost increases. The Pennsylvania State Police received $1.44 billion in FY2024, up from $1.19 billion in FY2019.

2.4 Transportation

The Pennsylvania Department of Transportation (PennDOT) received $2.3 billion from the general fund in FY2024. However, total transportation spending including federal pass-through funds and the Motor License Fund reached $9.7 billion. The I-95 emergency reconstruction project in Philadelphia, initiated following the June 2023 collapse, required an emergency appropriation of $138 million from the general fund in FY2024.

2.5 Debt Service

Debt service expenditures totaled $1.08 billion in FY2024, representing 2.4 percent of total general fund spending. This is a decrease from $1.21 billion in FY2021, reflecting the retirement of several general obligation bond series. The Commonwealth's outstanding general obligation debt stood at $10.4 billion as of June 30, 2024, down from $11.7 billion in FY2019.

SECTION 3: WORKFORCE AND PERSONNEL COSTS

Personnel costs across all agencies totaled $7.2 billion in FY2024, representing 16.3 percent of general fund expenditures. This compares to $6.4 billion in FY2019. The Commonwealth employed 78,340 full-time equivalent positions as of July 1, 2024, compared to 80,910 in FY2019, a reduction of 3.2 percent. Average compensation per FTE, including salary and benefits, increased from $79,100 in FY2019 to $91,900 in FY2024, a 16.2 percent increase. Employee health insurance costs rose from $1.4 billion in FY2019 to $1.9 billion in FY2024. Pension contributions to the State Employees' Retirement System (SERS) totaled $1.06 billion in FY2024, reflecting an employer contribution rate of 34.87 percent of payroll, compared to a rate of 30.03 percent in FY2019.

SECTION 4: FEDERAL FUNDS AND SPECIAL APPROPRIATIONS

The Commonwealth received $24.6 billion in federal funds during FY2024, compared to $17.3 billion in FY2019. Federal Medicaid matching payments accounted for $11.2 billion of FY2024 federal receipts. American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds, totaling $7.3 billion allocated to Pennsylvania in 2021, were substantially obligated by the December 31, 2024 federal deadline, with $6.9 billion committed across infrastructure, housing, and public health programs as of the report date.

SECTION 5: EXPENDITURE EFFICIENCY METRICS

The Bureau of Financial Management tracks cost-efficiency indicators across major program areas. Selected metrics for FY2024 include:

- Average cost per public school student funded through the Basic Education subsidy: $8,940, up from $7,620 in FY2019
- Average Medicaid cost per enrollee per month: $820, up from $601 in FY2019
- Administrative overhead as a percentage of DHS total spending: 4.2 percent, down from 5.1 percent in FY2019
- PennDOT cost per lane-mile of highway maintained: $14,200, up from $11,800 in FY2019
- DOC recidivism rate (three-year return to incarceration): 38.4 percent in FY2024, compared to 41.7 percent in FY2019

SECTION 6: FIVE-YEAR OUTLOOK AND STRUCTURAL CONSIDERATIONS

The Office of the Budget projects general fund expenditures will reach approximately $47.8 billion by FY2026, assuming continuation of current program commitments and a 4.5 percent average annual growth rate. The primary structural pressure remains Medicaid, where enrollment is projected to stabilize at approximately 3.6 million recipients through FY2026 but per-capita costs are expected to increase by 5.8 percent annually due to pharmaceutical price inflation and long-term care demand.

Pension obligations represent a second structural concern. SERS and the Public School Employees' Retirement System (PSERS) combined unfunded liability stood at $44.2 billion as of December 31, 2023, down from a peak of $72.4 billion in FY2020 following investment gains and reform measures enacted under Act 5 of 2017.

SECTION 7: METHODOLOGY AND DATA SOURCES

All expenditure figures are drawn from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES) financial management platform and reflect final appropriation closeout data. Year-end adjustments and lapses are incorporated into all figures. Inflation comparisons use the Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Price Index for the Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metropolitan area as a regional proxy. Federal fund figures are reconciled against Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA) submissions. This report was reviewed by Deputy Budget Secretary Jennifer Swails and certified by Chief Accounting Officer David Greer on September 10, 2024.
