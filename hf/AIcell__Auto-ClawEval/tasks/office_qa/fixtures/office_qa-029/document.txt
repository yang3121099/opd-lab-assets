Published by the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) in coordination with the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)
Release Date: February 14, 2024
Report Reference Number: CFTC-STAT-2024-003

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents comprehensive commodity price statistics for fiscal year 2023 (January 1 through December 31, 2023), compiled from exchange-traded data, federal procurement records, and international benchmark indices. The data reflects significant volatility across energy, agricultural, and metals sectors, driven by ongoing geopolitical disruptions, Federal Reserve monetary policy adjustments, and shifting global supply chains. Overall, the Commodity Research Bureau (CRB) Index closed fiscal year 2023 at 272.4 points, representing a 7.3 percent decline from the December 31, 2022 closing value of 293.8 points.

SECTION 1: ENERGY COMMODITIES

1.1 Crude Oil

West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil averaged $77.58 per barrel across all trading sessions in calendar year 2023, compared to $94.33 per barrel in 2022, representing a year-over-year decline of 17.8 percent. The annual high of $93.68 per barrel was recorded on September 27, 2023, following OPEC+ production cut announcements by Saudi Arabia and Russia totaling 1.3 million barrels per day. The annual low of $66.74 per barrel was recorded on March 17, 2023, amid banking sector instability following the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank.

Brent crude, the international benchmark, averaged $82.17 per barrel in 2023, down from $101.32 per barrel in 2022, a decline of 18.9 percent.

U.S. domestic crude oil production averaged 12.9 million barrels per day in 2023, the highest annual average in U.S. history, surpassing the previous record of 12.8 million barrels per day set in 2019.

1.2 Natural Gas

Henry Hub natural gas spot prices averaged $2.57 per million British thermal units (MMBtu) in 2023, a dramatic decline of 62.4 percent from the 2022 average of $6.83 per MMBtu. The 2022 spike was attributed primarily to European demand surges following the Russian invasion of Ukraine. The 2023 annual high of $3.49 per MMBtu occurred on January 12, 2023, while the annual low of $1.73 per MMBtu was recorded on March 3, 2023, reflecting above-average storage inventories and mild winter temperatures across the continental United States.

1.3 Gasoline and Distillates

Retail regular-grade gasoline averaged $3.53 per gallon nationally in 2023, compared to $3.99 per gallon in 2022, a decrease of 11.5 percent. Ultra-low sulfur diesel averaged $4.05 per gallon in 2023, down from $5.12 per gallon in 2022, a decline of 20.9 percent.

SECTION 2: AGRICULTURAL COMMODITIES

Wheat prices experienced the most significant decline among major grains, falling 34.2 percent from the 2022 average of $9.01 per bushel to $5.93 per bushel in 2023. The 2022 peak was driven by supply disruptions from Ukraine, which accounts for approximately 10 percent of global wheat exports. The resumption of partial Black Sea grain corridor operations through July 2023 contributed to price normalization before the corridor's termination on July 17, 2023, following Russia's withdrawal from the agreement.

Corn futures reached their 2023 annual high of $6.82 per bushel on April 28, 2023, and their annual low of $4.41 per bushel on October 6, 2023, as the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) projected total U.S. corn production at 15.234 billion bushels, up 10.4 percent from 2022.

2.2 Livestock and Meat

Live cattle futures averaged $1.7340 per pound in 2023, an increase of 14.6 percent from the 2022 average of $1.5130 per pound, driven by historically tight cattle supplies. The U.S. cattle herd as of January 1, 2024, was estimated at 87.2 million head, the smallest since 1951.

Lean hog futures averaged $0.7812 per pound in 2023, compared to $0.9245 per pound in 2022, a decline of 15.5 percent.

2.3 Soft Commodities

Arabica coffee futures (ICE) averaged $1.6847 per pound in 2023, down 10.2 percent from $1.8762 per pound in 2022. Raw sugar No. 11 futures averaged $0.2389 per pound in 2023, up 22.7 percent from $0.1947 per pound in 2022, reaching a 12-year high of $0.2765 per pound on November 9, 2023, due to production shortfalls in India and Thailand.

Cotton No. 2 futures averaged $0.8312 per pound in 2023, compared to $1.0241 per pound in 2022, a decline of 18.8 percent.

SECTION 3: METALS AND MINERALS

3.1 Precious Metals

Gold (COMEX spot) averaged $1,940.54 per troy ounce in 2023, an increase of 8.2 percent from the 2022 average of $1,793.12 per troy ounce. Gold reached an all-time intraday high of $2,135.39 per troy ounce on December 4, 2023, surpassing the previous record of $2,089.20 set on August 7, 2020. The surge was attributed to expectations of Federal Reserve interest rate cuts in 2024 and continued central bank purchases, with global central banks acquiring 1,037 metric tons of gold in 2023.

Silver averaged $23.35 per troy ounce in 2023, compared to $21.73 per troy ounce in 2022, an increase of 7.5 percent.

Zinc experienced the largest percentage decline among base metals, falling 23.9 percent from $3,479 per metric ton in 2022 to $2,648 per metric ton in 2023. Nickel, which had reached a historic high of $101,365 per metric ton on March 8, 2022, due to a short squeeze involving Tsingshan Holding Group, normalized significantly through 2023 as Indonesian production expanded.

3.3 Industrial Minerals

Lithium carbonate prices, tracked through the Fastmarkets benchmark for battery-grade material, averaged $34,200 per metric ton in 2023, a decline of 74.3 percent from the 2022 average of $133,000 per metric ton, reflecting rapid expansion of lithium mining capacity in Australia, Chile, and Argentina.

SECTION 4: PRODUCER PRICE INDEX — COMMODITY COMPONENTS

The Bureau of Labor Statistics Producer Price Index (PPI) for crude goods declined 9.8 percent on a year-over-year basis in December 2023, following a 14.2 percent increase recorded in December 2022. The PPI for processed goods for intermediate demand fell 4.1 percent year-over-year in December 2023.

Key PPI sub-indices for December 2023 (year-over-year):
- Crude petroleum: -14.3%
- Natural gas: -37.8%
- Iron and steel scrap: -18.6%
- Slaughter cattle: +12.4%
- Raw cotton: -22.1%
- Corn: -28.9%

SECTION 5: FEDERAL PROCUREMENT IMPACT

Federal agencies reported aggregate commodity procurement expenditures of $47.3 billion in fiscal year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023), compared to $52.8 billion in fiscal year 2022, a reduction of $5.5 billion or 10.4 percent. The Department of Defense accounted for $31.6 billion of total commodity procurement, with petroleum products representing $18.9 billion of that total. The Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) Energy division reported an average fuel acquisition cost of $3.47 per gallon across all fuel types in fiscal year 2023, compared to $4.21 per gallon in fiscal year 2022.

SECTION 6: OUTLOOK AND METHODOLOGY NOTES

Data in this report is sourced from the following primary databases: CME Group daily settlement prices, London Metal Exchange official cash prices, USDA Agricultural Marketing Service daily reports, U.S. Energy Information Administration weekly petroleum status reports, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics Producer Price Index release series. All averages are calculated as arithmetic means of daily closing or settlement prices for the full calendar year unless otherwise noted. Federal procurement data is sourced from USASpending.gov and DLA Energy transaction records.

Projections for fiscal year 2024 are outside the scope of this statistical summary. Readers seeking forward-looking analysis are directed to the CFTC's Commitments of Traders reports and the USDA's World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates (WASDE), published monthly.
