EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Internal Revenue Service collected $4.68 trillion in gross taxes during Fiscal Year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023), representing a decline of 9.3 percent from the $5.16 trillion collected in Fiscal Year 2022. This contraction reflects the normalization of economic conditions following the extraordinary revenue environment of FY2022, which was inflated by elevated capital gains realizations, pandemic-era business profits, and deferred tax payments. Net tax revenue after refunds totaled $4.44 trillion, compared to $4.90 trillion in FY2022.

This report was prepared by the Office of Research, Applied Analytics, and Statistics under the direction of Chief Statistician Patrick Lanigan and Deputy Commissioner for Services and Enforcement Douglas O'Donnell. The analysis draws on data from approximately 271.5 million tax returns processed during the fiscal year.

SECTION 1: REVENUE BY TAX CATEGORY

1.1 Individual Income Taxes

Individual income taxes remained the single largest source of federal tax revenue in FY2023, generating $2.18 trillion in gross collections. This figure represents a decrease of 12.4 percent from the $2.49 trillion collected in FY2022. The decline is attributable primarily to a 32.1 percent reduction in capital gains realizations, as equity markets contracted and real estate transaction volumes fell sharply following Federal Reserve interest rate increases beginning in March 2022.

Withholding taxes on wages and salaries totaled $1.74 trillion, an increase of 4.2 percent over FY2022, consistent with continued labor market strength and average wage growth of 5.1 percent across the private sector. Estimated tax payments from self-employed individuals and investors totaled $312.4 billion, down 18.7 percent year-over-year.

1.2 Payroll Taxes

Social insurance and retirement receipts, commonly referred to as payroll taxes, contributed $1.61 trillion to gross collections in FY2023. This represents an increase of 6.8 percent from the $1.51 trillion collected in FY2022. The Social Security portion (OASDI) accounted for $1.19 trillion, while Medicare taxes (HI) contributed $421.3 billion. The payroll tax base expanded in line with employment growth, with total covered wages reaching approximately $10.4 trillion during the calendar year.

1.3 Corporate Income Taxes

Corporate income tax collections totaled $529.8 billion in FY2023, a decrease of 9.8 percent from the $587.3 billion recorded in FY2022. The decline reflects compressed corporate profit margins as input costs remained elevated and revenue growth slowed. The financial sector, which had contributed disproportionately to FY2022 corporate receipts, saw tax payments fall by an estimated 22.4 percent. The technology sector similarly experienced reduced taxable income following significant workforce reductions and asset write-downs throughout calendar year 2022 and early 2023.

The corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT), enacted under the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 and effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2022, generated approximately $4.8 billion in its first year of implementation, below the Joint Committee on Taxation's initial projection of $9.0 billion for FY2023.

1.4 Excise Taxes

Federal excise taxes generated $87.4 billion in FY2023, a modest increase of 1.2 percent from $86.4 billion in FY2022. Highway-related excise taxes, including the motor fuels tax of 18.4 cents per gallon for gasoline and 24.4 cents per gallon for diesel, contributed $38.9 billion. Tobacco excise taxes declined to $11.2 billion, continuing a long-term downward trend reflecting reduced smoking rates. The Superfund chemical excise tax, reinstated by the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act of 2021, contributed $1.4 billion.

1.5 Estate and Gift Taxes

Estate and gift taxes totaled $34.1 billion in FY2023, compared to $34.9 billion in FY2022. The unified credit exemption amount for FY2023 was $12.92 million per individual, adjusted for inflation under Internal Revenue Code Section 2010(c). Approximately 6,080 taxable estate tax returns were filed during the fiscal year.

SECTION 3: REFUNDS AND NET COLLECTIONS

The IRS issued $236.9 billion in individual income tax refunds during FY2023, processing approximately 161.9 million individual returns. The average individual refund amount was $2,812, compared to $3,039 in FY2022, a decrease of 7.5 percent. The reduction in average refund size reflects IRS guidance encouraging taxpayers to adjust withholding following the expiration of expanded Child Tax Credit provisions that had generated larger-than-usual refunds in the prior year.

Corporate refunds totaled $3.8 billion, and other refunds across all categories summed to $239.1 billion, yielding net tax revenue of $4.44 trillion for FY2023.

SECTION 4: COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT REVENUE

4.1 Audit and Examination Activity

The IRS conducted 582,944 individual return audits in FY2023, representing an audit rate of 0.44 percent of all individual returns filed, up from 0.38 percent in FY2022. This increase reflects the deployment of additional examination personnel funded through the $79.6 billion in IRS enforcement appropriations provided under the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022. Audits of returns reporting income above $1 million increased by 31 percent compared to FY2022.

Examination activity generated $31.9 billion in recommended additional taxes, of which $17.4 billion was assessed and $14.5 billion remained under appeal or litigation as of September 30, 2023.

4.2 Criminal Investigation

The IRS Criminal Investigation division initiated 2,676 investigations in FY2023 and obtained 1,986 indictments. The conviction rate for prosecuted cases was 90.2 percent. Financial crimes identified through CI activity totaled an estimated $8.9 billion in tax fraud and related financial crimes.

4.3 Tax Gap Estimate

The IRS most recent Tax Gap estimate, covering tax year 2019 and published in October 2022, placed the gross tax gap at $688 billion annually, with a net tax gap of $540 billion after enforcement and late payments. The voluntary compliance rate was estimated at 85.1 percent. Updated Tax Gap estimates incorporating tax years 2020 and 2021 are expected to be released in the third quarter of FY2024.

SECTION 5: NOTABLE LEGISLATIVE AND POLICY IMPACTS

The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, signed into law on August 16, 2022, by President Biden, contained several provisions with direct FY2023 revenue implications:

- The 1 percent excise tax on corporate stock buybacks, effective January 1, 2023, generated $8.6 billion in its first year of applicability, exceeding the Joint Committee on Taxation's initial estimate of $7.4 billion.
- The Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax of 15 percent on book income of corporations with average annual adjusted financial statement income exceeding $1 billion generated $4.8 billion, as noted in Section 1.3.
- Enhanced IRS funding of $79.6 billion over ten years, with $3.2 billion allocated to taxpayer services and $45.6 billion to enforcement activities in the initial spending plan.

The SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022, enacted December 29, 2022, modified required minimum distribution rules and expanded Roth contribution options, with estimated revenue effects of negative $24.3 billion over the ten-year budget window according to the Joint Committee on Taxation.

The FY2023 ratio of 16.5 percent returned to levels more consistent with the post-2001 historical average of approximately 16.3 percent.

SECTION 7: OUTLOOK FOR FISCAL YEAR 2024

The Office of Management and Budget projects FY2024 gross tax collections of approximately $4.92 trillion, representing a recovery of 5.1 percent from FY2023 levels. This projection assumes continued labor market stability, moderate corporate profit recovery, and partial recovery in capital gains realizations as equity markets stabilized during calendar year 2023. The Congressional Budget Office's independent projection, published January 2024, estimates FY2024 net revenues of $4.77 trillion.

Key risks to the FY2024 revenue outlook include potential economic slowdown associated with lagged effects of monetary tightening, uncertainty regarding the resolution of approximately $14.5 billion in contested examination assessments, and the scheduled expiration of numerous Tax Cuts and Jobs Act provisions after December 31, 2025, which may affect taxpayer behavior in the near term.
