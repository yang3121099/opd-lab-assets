EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of federal government expenditure patterns across fiscal years 2019 through 2024. Total federal outlays grew from $4.45 trillion in FY2019 to $6.75 trillion in FY2024, representing a cumulative increase of 51.7 percent over the five-year period. The average annual growth rate in nominal spending was 8.7 percent, substantially exceeding the average annual inflation rate of 4.2 percent over the same period. This document was prepared by the Office of Fiscal Analysis and Budget Review under the direction of Deputy Assistant Secretary Margaret Holloway and is intended to inform congressional budget deliberations for FY2026.

SECTION 1: TOTAL OUTLAYS BY FISCAL YEAR

The following table summarizes total federal outlays for each fiscal year under review:

Fiscal Year | Total Outlays | Year-over-Year Change | % Change
FY2019      | $4.45 trillion | —                    | —
FY2020      | $6.55 trillion | +$2.10 trillion       | +47.2%
FY2021      | $6.82 trillion | +$0.27 trillion       | +4.1%
FY2022      | $6.27 trillion | -$0.55 trillion       | -8.1%
FY2023      | $6.13 trillion | -$0.14 trillion       | -2.2%
FY2024      | $6.75 trillion | +$0.62 trillion       | +10.1%

The extraordinary spike in FY2020 reflects emergency pandemic-related appropriations, including the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act signed on March 27, 2020, which alone authorized $2.2 trillion in emergency spending. The subsequent moderation in FY2022 and FY2023 reflects the expiration of temporary relief programs, while the FY2024 increase is attributable primarily to mandatory spending growth in Social Security and Medicare, as well as elevated net interest payments on the national debt.

SECTION 2: EXPENDITURE BY MAJOR CATEGORY

2.1 Mandatory Spending

Mandatory spending, which includes Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and other entitlement programs, constituted the largest share of federal outlays throughout the review period. In FY2024, mandatory programs accounted for $4.11 trillion, or 60.9 percent of total outlays. This compares to $2.73 trillion in FY2019, representing a 50.5 percent increase over five years.

Social Security outlays alone reached $1.46 trillion in FY2024, up from $1.06 trillion in FY2019. The 2024 cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) of 3.2 percent, announced by the Social Security Administration on October 12, 2023, contributed approximately $18.7 billion in additional annual expenditure.

Medicare spending totaled $1.05 trillion in FY2024, compared to $644 billion in FY2019, a 63.0 percent increase. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) attributed this growth primarily to increased enrollment driven by the aging of the Baby Boomer generation, with 67.3 million beneficiaries enrolled in Medicare as of December 2024, up from 61.2 million in December 2019.

Medicaid outlays in FY2024 reached $616 billion, reflecting continued federal-state cost-sharing arrangements and the gradual unwinding of continuous enrollment provisions that had been in place since March 2020.

2.2 Discretionary Spending

Total discretionary spending in FY2024 was $1.71 trillion, representing 25.3 percent of total outlays. This breaks down into:

Defense Discretionary:
- FY2019: $676 billion
- FY2024: $886 billion
- Five-year increase: 31.1%

Non-Defense Discretionary:
- FY2019: $638 billion
- FY2024: $824 billion
- Five-year increase: 29.2%

The Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2024, signed on March 23, 2024, set the defense discretionary cap at $886 billion and non-defense discretionary at $773 billion, though emergency supplemental appropriations brought the non-defense total to $824 billion by fiscal year end.

2.3 Net Interest on the Public Debt

Net interest payments represent the fastest-growing expenditure category in percentage terms. In FY2019, net interest totaled $375 billion. By FY2024, this figure had climbed to $892 billion, a 137.9 percent increase in five years. Net interest now constitutes 13.2 percent of total federal outlays, compared to 8.4 percent in FY2019.

This dramatic increase reflects two compounding factors: the substantial growth in total debt outstanding, which reached $34.6 trillion as of September 30, 2024, and the Federal Reserve's rate-hiking cycle that began in March 2022, which raised the federal funds rate from near zero to a target range of 5.25 to 5.50 percent by July 2023. The average interest rate on Treasury securities held by the public rose from 1.6 percent in FY2021 to 3.1 percent in FY2024.

SECTION 3: EXPENDITURE AS A PERCENTAGE OF GDP

Analyzing outlays relative to gross domestic product provides a measure of the government's fiscal footprint independent of nominal growth.

Fiscal Year | Total Outlays | GDP         | Outlays as % of GDP
FY2019      | $4.45 trillion | $21.43 trillion | 20.8%
FY2020      | $6.55 trillion | $21.06 trillion | 31.1%
FY2021      | $6.82 trillion | $23.32 trillion | 29.2%
FY2022      | $6.27 trillion | $25.74 trillion | 24.4%
FY2023      | $6.13 trillion | $27.36 trillion | 22.4%
FY2024      | $6.75 trillion | $28.18 trillion | 23.9%

The FY2020 ratio of 31.1 percent represents the highest level of federal spending as a share of GDP since World War II, surpassing the previous post-war peak of 25.2 percent recorded in FY2009 during the financial crisis response. The post-pandemic normalization brought the ratio closer to the 40-year historical average of approximately 21.5 percent, though FY2024 remains elevated relative to pre-pandemic norms.

SECTION 4: SELECTED AGENCY EXPENDITURE TRENDS

The following agencies demonstrated the most significant expenditure changes during the review period:

Department of Health and Human Services (HHS):
Total HHS outlays grew from $1.19 trillion in FY2019 to $1.72 trillion in FY2024. The peak year was FY2021 at $2.07 trillion, driven by $487 billion in COVID-19 relief disbursements administered through HHS programs, including the Provider Relief Fund, which distributed $178 billion to healthcare providers between April 2020 and December 2022.

Department of Defense (DoD):
DoD total budget authority (including both discretionary and mandatory components) reached $858 billion in FY2024, compared to $738 billion in FY2019. The FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act, signed December 22, 2023, authorized a 5.2 percent increase over the prior year, the largest single-year percentage increase since FY2003.

Department of Veterans Affairs (VA):
VA discretionary appropriations increased from $93.1 billion in FY2019 to $135.2 billion in FY2024, a 45.2 percent increase. The PACT Act, signed August 10, 2022, is projected to add an estimated $280 billion in VA obligations over the following decade, with initial-year costs of approximately $3.5 billion in FY2023 rising to $11.8 billion in FY2024 as eligibility determinations were processed.

Department of Education:
Outlays declined from $71.4 billion in FY2019 to $68.9 billion in FY2024, a 3.5 percent decrease, reflecting the expiration of Elementary and Secondary School Emergency Relief (ESSER) funds totaling $189.5 billion that had been distributed between 2020 and 2024.

SECTION 5: DEFICIT IMPLICATIONS

The expenditure trends documented in this report must be considered alongside revenue performance. Federal revenues in FY2024 totaled $4.92 trillion, producing a unified budget deficit of $1.83 trillion. This represents a deterioration from the FY2022 deficit of $1.38 trillion, which had itself improved significantly from the FY2021 deficit of $2.78 trillion.

The FY2024 deficit of $1.83 trillion equals 6.5 percent of GDP, compared to the 40-year historical average deficit of approximately 3.5 percent of GDP. The Congressional Budget Office, in its February 2025 Budget and Economic Outlook, projected that without legislative changes, cumulative deficits over the FY2025–FY2034 period would total $20.2 trillion.

SECTION 6: KEY FINDINGS AND OBSERVATIONS

1. Net interest is now the third-largest expenditure category, exceeding total non-defense discretionary spending for the first time in recorded federal budget history, a threshold crossed in the second quarter of FY2024.

2. Mandatory spending has grown as a share of total outlays from 61.3 percent in FY2019 to 60.9 percent in FY2024, remaining relatively stable despite significant nominal growth.

3. The five-year compound annual growth rate of 8.7 percent in nominal spending significantly exceeds the 4.2 percent average inflation rate, indicating real expenditure growth of approximately 4.3 percent annually.

4. Per capita federal spending reached approximately $19,940 in FY2024, based on a U.S. population of 338.6 million, compared to $13,560 per capita in FY2019.
