EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This technical report presents the results of a comprehensive statistical distribution fitting analysis applied to five major Pennsylvania state revenue categories over the fiscal years ending June 30, 2019 through June 30, 2023. The analysis was conducted to improve the accuracy of revenue forecasting models used in the Governor's Executive Budget process and to quantify uncertainty bounds around point estimates submitted to the General Assembly. Using maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) and goodness-of-fit testing across 14 candidate distributions, the Division of Quantitative Methods identified optimal distributional families for each revenue stream. Key findings indicate that Personal Income Tax receipts are best characterized by a normal distribution with mean $14.87 billion and standard deviation $1.23 billion, while Corporate Net Income Tax receipts exhibit significant right skewness consistent with a log-normal distribution. These results will be incorporated into the FY2025–2026 biennial forecast beginning in September 2024.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. INTRODUCTION AND POLICY CONTEXT

Pennsylvania's General Fund receives revenue from approximately 28 distinct tax and non-tax sources. Accurate forecasting of these streams is essential for budget planning, debt service scheduling, and compliance with the Commonwealth's balanced budget requirement under Article VIII, Section 13 of the Pennsylvania Constitution. Historically, the Office of Fiscal Analysis has relied on point forecasts generated by econometric regression models calibrated against macroeconomic indicators published by the Bureau of Economic Analysis and the Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry.

Beginning in fiscal year 2022, the Secretary of Revenue, Patrick Browne, directed the Division of Quantitative Methods to supplement point forecasts with probabilistic confidence intervals. This directive followed a forecast error of $1.14 billion in FY2021, when actual Personal Income Tax receipts of $16.02 billion exceeded the official estimate of $14.88 billion by 7.7 percent, partly attributable to pandemic-era capital gains realizations that were not captured in baseline models.

The present analysis addresses this gap by formally characterizing the statistical distributions underlying monthly revenue observations and propagating distributional uncertainty into annual forecast intervals.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The analysis draws on 60 monthly observations per revenue category, spanning July 2018 through June 2023. Data were obtained from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES) financial ledger and cross-validated against published Comptroller General monthly statements. 3. METHODOLOGY

3.1 Candidate Distributions

Fourteen theoretical distributions were evaluated for each revenue category: normal, log-normal, gamma, inverse Gaussian, Weibull, beta (applied to normalized series), exponential, Pareto, generalized extreme value (GEV), logistic, log-logistic, Gumbel, Burr Type XII, and Student's t with estimated degrees of freedom. Monthly observations were seasonally adjusted using the U.S. Census Bureau's X-13ARIMA-SEATS procedure prior to fitting.

3.2 Estimation and Testing

Parameters were estimated via maximum likelihood estimation using the fitdistrplus package (version 1.1-11) implemented in R version 4.3.1. Goodness-of-fit was assessed using three complementary criteria:

- Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test statistic and associated p-value
- Akaike Information Criterion (AIC)
- Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)

A distribution was selected as the best fit when it minimized both AIC and BIC and produced a KS test p-value exceeding 0.10, indicating failure to reject the null hypothesis that the data follow the candidate distribution at the 10 percent significance level.

3.3 Bootstrap Validation

To assess parameter stability, 2,000 bootstrap resamples of each 60-observation series were drawn with replacement. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of bootstrapped parameter estimates define the 95 percent confidence intervals reported in Section 4.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

4. RESULTS

4.1 Personal Income Tax

The normal distribution provided the best fit for seasonally adjusted monthly Personal Income Tax receipts. Estimated parameters: mean μ = $1,238.4 million per month, standard deviation σ = $102.7 million per month. Annualized, this corresponds to μ = $14.86 billion and σ = $1.23 billion. The KS test statistic was 0.071 with p-value 0.91, and the normal distribution achieved the lowest AIC (847.3) and BIC (851.1) among all 14 candidates. The 95 percent bootstrap confidence interval for the annual mean spans $14.54 billion to $15.18 billion.

4.2 Corporate Net Income Tax

Corporate Net Income Tax receipts exhibited pronounced right skewness (skewness coefficient = 1.87) and excess kurtosis (4.12), consistent with occasional large quarterly estimated payments from a small number of corporations. The log-normal distribution was selected as the best fit, with estimated parameters μ_ln = 19.84 (log-scale mean) and σ_ln = 0.31 (log-scale standard deviation). This implies a median annual receipt of $4.11 billion and a mean of $4.27 billion. The KS p-value was 0.23. The 90th percentile of the fitted distribution is $6.01 billion, which closely approximates the observed FY2022 peak of $6.27 billion.

4.3 Sales and Use Tax

Sales and Use Tax receipts, the Commonwealth's second-largest revenue source, were best described by a gamma distribution with shape parameter α = 118.4 and rate parameter β = 0.0112 per million dollars. The implied mean is $10,571 million annually and the standard deviation is $971 million. The KS p-value was 0.44. The gamma distribution outperformed the normal distribution on both AIC (831.6 versus 836.2) and BIC (835.4 versus 840.0), reflecting mild positive skewness in the monthly series.

4.4 Realty Transfer Tax

Realty Transfer Tax receipts showed the highest sensitivity to housing market conditions and were best fit by the generalized extreme value (GEV) distribution with location parameter ξ = $48.2 million per month, scale parameter σ_GEV = $12.7 million, and shape parameter κ = 0.18. The positive shape parameter confirms heavy-tailed behavior associated with episodic large commercial real estate transactions. The KS p-value was 0.17. The 95th percentile of the annual distribution is $873 million, suggesting that the FY2022 observation of $820 million, while elevated, was within the plausible range of the fitted model.

4.5 Inheritance Tax

Inheritance Tax receipts were best characterized by a log-normal distribution with μ_ln = 18.55 and σ_ln = 0.09, yielding an implied annual mean of $1.14 billion and standard deviation of $103 million. The KS p-value was 0.62. The narrow log-scale standard deviation reflects the relative stability of this revenue source, which is driven by demographic factors and estate settlement timelines that vary less than corporate or capital gains income.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

6. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS

Several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, the 60-month sample period encompasses the COVID-19 fiscal disruption of FY2020 and the subsequent recovery, which may distort distributional parameter estimates. Sensitivity analyses excluding FY2020 observations produced qualitatively similar results but increased the estimated standard deviation of Corporate Net Income Tax by approximately 12 percent. Second, the analysis treats each fiscal year as an independent draw, which may understate serial correlation in revenue levels driven by multi-year economic cycles. Third, the GEV fit for Realty Transfer Tax is sensitive to the three largest monthly observations, all occurring between October 2021 and March 2022 during the peak of the residential real estate market.

Future work will incorporate a regime-switching framework to distinguish between expansion and contraction phases of the Pennsylvania business cycle, as identified by the Philadelphia Federal Reserve's State Coincident Index. The Division also plans to extend the analysis to non-tax revenues, including lottery fund transfers averaging $1.37 billion annually over the study period, and federal reimbursements under the Medical Assistance program.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

7. CONCLUSIONS

This analysis establishes empirically grounded distributional characterizations for five major Pennsylvania General Fund revenue streams. The findings confirm that no single distributional family is appropriate across all revenue categories: normal and gamma distributions adequately describe high-volume, relatively stable taxes, while log-normal and generalized extreme value distributions are required for revenues subject to large, infrequent transactions. The probabilistic forecast framework developed here reduces reliance on single-point estimates and provides budget planners with actionable information about downside risk. At the 5th percentile of the joint simulation, total revenue from the five analyzed categories would be approximately $2.8 billion below the median forecast, a shortfall that would require either reserve fund drawdowns or expenditure adjustments under the Commonwealth's balanced budget requirement.
