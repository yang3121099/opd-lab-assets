EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive comparison of departmental budget allocations, expenditures, and variances for the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania for Fiscal Year 2023-2024 (July 1, 2023 through June 30, 2024). The total enacted General Fund budget for FY 2023-2024 was $47.6 billion, representing an increase of $1.9 billion, or 4.2 percent, over the FY 2022-2023 enacted budget of $45.7 billion. Actual General Fund expenditures for FY 2023-2024 totaled $46.8 billion, resulting in a statewide underspend of approximately $800 million, or 1.7 percent below the enacted appropriation.

Budget Secretary Uri Monson certified the final expenditure figures on September 30, 2024, following the close of the fiscal year and completion of departmental reconciliation processes.

SECTION 1: METHODOLOGY AND SCOPE

This report draws from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES), the official accounting platform managed by the Office of Comptroller Operations. Data was extracted on September 15, 2024, with final adjustments incorporated through September 30, 2024. All figures represent General Fund appropriations unless otherwise noted. Federal funds, augmentations, and restricted receipts are reported separately in Appendix C of the full budget document.

Departments are evaluated against three benchmarks:
1. FY 2023-2024 Enacted Budget (Governor Shapiro's signed budget, July 11, 2023)
2. FY 2022-2023 Actual Expenditures
3. Governor's Executive Budget Request submitted February 7, 2023

Variance calculations reflect the difference between enacted appropriations and actual expenditures. A negative variance indicates underspending; a positive variance indicates an overrun requiring supplemental appropriation.

SECTION 3: NOTABLE FINDINGS BY DEPARTMENT

3.1 Department of Human Services (DHS)

DHS remains the single largest General Fund expenditure, accounting for 37.2 percent of total actual spending in FY 2023-2024. The department's enacted budget of $17.614 billion represented a $772.2 million increase over FY 2022-2023 actuals, driven primarily by expanded Medical Assistance enrollment and a 6.8 percent increase in long-term care costs. Actual spending of $17.401 billion came in $213.3 million below the enacted figure, largely due to lower-than-projected Medicaid managed care utilization in the fourth quarter (April through June 2024). Secretary Dr. Val Arkoosh attributed the underspend to improved care coordination outcomes and a 3.1 percent reduction in inpatient hospital days among Medical Assistance recipients.

3.2 Department of Education

The Department of Education expended its full enacted appropriation of $8.756 billion, representing zero variance. This figure includes $6.1 billion in basic education funding, $1.4 billion in special education funding, and $1.256 billion in higher education and other program support. The basic education funding line increased by $567 million over FY 2022-2023, the largest single-year increase in the department's history, following the Commonwealth Court's February 7, 2023 ruling in William Penn School District v. Pennsylvania Department of Education, which found the existing school funding system unconstitutional.

3.3 Department of Corrections

The Department of Corrections was the only major agency to record a budget overrun, exceeding its enacted appropriation by $26.6 million, or 0.91 percent. Secretary Dr. Laurel Harry reported that the overrun resulted from unanticipated increases in pharmaceutical costs for the inmate population, which rose 14.3 percent year-over-year, and emergency facility repairs at SCI Graterford following a structural assessment completed in November 2023. The department's average daily inmate population for FY 2023-2024 was 37,842, compared to 36,914 in FY 2022-2023, an increase of 2.5 percent. A supplemental appropriation of $26.6 million was approved by the General Assembly on August 14, 2024.

3.4 Department of Health

The Department of Health's enacted budget of $1.388 billion represented a 15.2 percent increase over FY 2022-2023 actuals of $1.205 billion, the largest percentage increase among major departments. This growth reflected new investments in maternal and infant health programs ($42.3 million), expanded opioid treatment and recovery services ($68.7 million), and public health infrastructure grants to county health departments ($31.4 million). Actual spending of $1.363 billion was $25.5 million below the enacted level due to delayed hiring for 214 new public health nurse positions, with recruitment ongoing as of the report date.

3.5 Pennsylvania State Police

The Pennsylvania State Police expended $1.538 billion against an enacted budget of $1.544 billion, finishing $5.6 million under budget. The agency employed 4,718 sworn officers as of June 30, 2024, compared to a budgeted complement of 4,780, with 62 vacancies contributing to the underspend. Personnel costs represent 78.4 percent of the agency's total expenditures.

SECTION 4: YEAR-OVER-YEAR BUDGET GROWTH ANALYSIS

Total General Fund spending grew by $3.099 billion, or 7.1 percent, from FY 2022-2023 actuals to FY 2023-2024 actuals. The five departments with the highest year-over-year expenditure growth rates were:

1. Department of Health: +13.1% (+$158.1 million)
2. Department of Education: +8.1% (+$652.7 million)
3. Department of Human Services: +3.3% (+$558.9 million)
4. Department of Agriculture: +8.3% (+$14.8 million)
5. Department of Labor & Industry: +6.9% (+$22.2 million)

The Department of Transportation recorded the smallest growth rate among major agencies at 3.5 percent, reflecting the maturation of several capital projects funded in prior fiscal years.

SECTION 5: RESERVE AND RAINY DAY FUND STATUS

As of June 30, 2024, the Commonwealth's Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund) held a balance of $6.24 billion, equivalent to approximately 13.3 percent of annual General Fund expenditures. This represents an increase of $440 million from the June 30, 2023 balance of $5.80 billion. The fund's current balance is the highest in Commonwealth history. Under Act 48 of 2003, deposits to the Rainy Day Fund are required when General Fund revenues exceed the official estimate by more than 2 percent. FY 2023-2024 General Fund revenues totaled $48.1 billion, exceeding the official estimate of $47.3 billion by $800 million, or 1.69 percent, which did not trigger a mandatory deposit but allowed for a discretionary transfer approved by the Governor on July 22, 2024.

SECTION 6: RECOMMENDATIONS

The Bureau of Financial Management and Analysis offers the following recommendations for FY 2024-2025 budget management:

- The Department of Corrections should submit a revised pharmaceutical cost projection by December 1, 2024, incorporating updated formulary data to prevent a second consecutive overrun.
- The Department of Health should report on recruitment progress for the 214 unfilled public health nurse positions by January 15, 2025, with a corrective action plan if vacancies exceed 100 positions at that date.
- All departments with vacancy-driven underspends exceeding $5 million should submit workforce plans to the Office of Administration by November 30, 2024.
