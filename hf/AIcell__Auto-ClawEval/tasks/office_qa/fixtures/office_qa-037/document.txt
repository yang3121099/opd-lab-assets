EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania's second quarter financial results for Fiscal Year 2023-2024 reflect a period of moderate revenue growth tempered by elevated expenditure pressures in healthcare and education. Total General Fund revenues for the quarter ending December 31, 2023 reached $11.84 billion, representing a 3.2 percent increase over the first quarter total of $11.47 billion. Year-over-year, second quarter revenues declined by 1.7 percent compared to the $12.04 billion recorded in Q2 of Fiscal Year 2022-2023, driven primarily by softness in personal income tax collections and reduced investment income.

Secretary of the Budget Uri Monson noted in his accompanying letter that while the Commonwealth remains on track to meet its annual revenue estimate of $45.9 billion, the narrowing margin between revenues and expenditures warrants continued fiscal discipline through the second half of the fiscal year.

Key Revenue Observations

Personal Income Tax (PIT): Collections of $4.489 billion in Q2 exceeded Q1 by $176.5 million, reflecting seasonal strength in employer withholding tied to year-end bonuses and the December payroll cycle. The PIT rate remains at 3.07 percent, unchanged since 2004. Withholding collections alone totaled $3.12 billion, up 5.3 percent from Q1's $2.96 billion.

Sales and Use Tax: The $3.342 billion collected in Q2 represents the strongest quarterly sales tax performance since Q2 of FY2021-2022. Holiday retail activity in November and December contributed an estimated $410 million above the monthly baseline average. Online marketplace facilitator collections, which became fully operational under Act 43 of 2017, contributed $287 million in Q2, up from $261 million in Q1.

Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT): The 9.2 percent quarter-over-quarter decline to $1.704 billion reflects the timing of estimated payments, which are concentrated in Q1 and Q3. The CNIT rate was reduced from 9.99 percent to 8.99 percent effective January 1, 2023, as part of a phased reduction schedule that will bring the rate to 4.99 percent by 2031. This structural rate change accounts for approximately $68 million of the year-over-year CNIT shortfall.

Realty Transfer Tax: Collections fell 11.3 percent to $176.3 million, consistent with the continued slowdown in residential real estate transactions. Pennsylvania home sales volume declined 14.2 percent in the October-December 2023 period compared to the same period in 2022, according to data from the Pennsylvania Association of Realtors.

Non-Tax Revenue: The $153.5 million quarter-over-quarter increase was driven largely by $94.7 million in additional federal reimbursements related to Medicaid administrative costs and a one-time $42.3 million settlement payment received from a pharmaceutical manufacturer in November 2023.

Key Expenditure Observations

Medical Assistance: Medicaid spending reached $3.390 billion in Q2, a 5.4 percent increase over Q1 and the largest single-quarter total in the program's history within Pennsylvania. Enrollment in the Medical Assistance program stood at 3.41 million individuals as of December 31, 2023, compared to 3.38 million at the end of September 2023. The per-member-per-month cost increased to $331.14 in Q2 from $317.30 in Q1, driven by higher pharmacy costs and increased utilization of behavioral health services.

Education: The $3.976 billion in PreK-12 education expenditures includes $2.84 billion in Basic Education Funding distributed to 500 school districts, $487 million in Special Education funding, and $312 million in reimbursements under the Ready to Learn Block Grant program. The quarter-over-quarter increase of $135.2 million reflects the timing of subsidy payments, with December distributions typically larger due to district cash flow requirements.

Corrections: The Department of Corrections spent $761.2 million in Q2, with an average daily inmate population of 37,842, down from 38,104 in Q1. The per-diem cost per inmate rose to $109.47 in Q2 from $107.14 in Q1, reflecting increased healthcare and staffing costs at State Correctional Institution facilities.

SECTION 3: FISCAL BALANCE AND CASH POSITION

The Q2 operating deficit of $210 million (total revenues of $11.840 billion minus total expenditures of $12.050 billion) compares to a Q1 operating surplus of $99 million. The cumulative six-month deficit through December 31, 2023 stands at $111 million against the annual budget, which projected a year-end balance of $252 million.

The Commonwealth's General Fund unappropriated balance as of December 31, 2023 was $1.387 billion, down from $1.598 billion at September 30, 2023. The Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund) held $1.83 billion as of December 31, 2023, unchanged from the prior quarter. Pennsylvania's Rainy Day Fund balance represents approximately 4.0 percent of annual General Fund appropriations, below the Government Finance Officers Association recommended threshold of 5 to 15 percent.

Short-term borrowing through Tax Anticipation Notes (TANs) was not required during Q2, consistent with Q1. The Commonwealth last issued TANs in FY2019-2020.

SECTION 4: OUTLOOK AND RISK FACTORS

Second-Half Revenue Projections

The Independent Fiscal Office (IFO) revised its FY2023-2024 General Fund revenue forecast downward by $312 million on January 11, 2024, citing weaker-than-expected corporate tax collections and a projected slowdown in consumer spending. The revised IFO estimate of $45.59 billion remains below the Administration's official estimate of $45.90 billion.

Key risks to second-half revenue performance include:

- Federal Medicaid enhanced match rate expiration: The 6.2 percentage point Federal Medical Assistance Percentage (FMAP) enhancement that provided Pennsylvania approximately $1.1 billion in additional federal funds during the COVID-19 public health emergency has fully phased out as of December 31, 2023, increasing the Commonwealth's share of Medicaid costs beginning in Q3.

- Personal income tax estimated payments: Q3 estimated payments due April 15, 2024 will be the first significant test of whether individual taxpayers experienced capital gains realizations sufficient to meet projections. The IFO estimates capital gains-related PIT revenue of $1.24 billion for the full fiscal year.

- Interest rate environment: The Commonwealth's short-term investment portfolio earned an annualized yield of 5.31 percent in Q2, generating $187 million in investment income. Any Federal Reserve rate reductions in calendar year 2024 would reduce this income stream in Q3 and Q4.

SECTION 5: SPECIAL FUNDS SUMMARY

The Motor License Fund received $1.127 billion in Q2, a 2.1 percent increase over Q1's $1.104 billion, reflecting higher liquid fuels tax collections tied to increased vehicle miles traveled during the fall travel season. The Lottery Fund transferred $286.4 million to senior programs in Q2, compared to $271.8 million in Q1. The Unemployment Compensation Fund maintained a positive balance of $3.42 billion as of December 31, 2023, sufficient to pay approximately 14 weeks of benefits at current claim levels.
