FISCAL YEAR 2023 TAX REVENUE ANALYSIS
Annual Report to Congress
Published: February 14, 2024

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Total federal tax revenue collected during Fiscal Year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023) reached $4.44 trillion, representing a decline of 9.3 percent from the record $4.90 trillion collected in Fiscal Year 2022. This contraction marks the first year-over-year decrease in federal tax receipts since Fiscal Year 2020, when collections fell amid the economic disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite the decline, FY2023 collections remain 16.8 percent above the pre-pandemic FY2019 baseline of $3.80 trillion.

This report, prepared under the direction of IRS Commissioner Daniel Werfel, provides a comprehensive breakdown of revenue by source, examines contributing factors to the year-over-year decline, and projects near-term collection trends through FY2025.

SECTION 1: TOTAL REVENUE BY SOURCE

The following table summarizes federal tax collections by major category for FY2023 compared to FY2022:

Revenue Source | FY2023 Collections | FY2022 Collections | Change (%)
Individual Income Tax | $2.18 trillion | $2.63 trillion | -17.1%
Payroll Taxes (FICA) | $1.61 trillion | $1.48 trillion | +8.8%
Corporate Income Tax | $0.42 trillion | $0.42 trillion | 0.0%
Excise Taxes | $0.09 trillion | $0.10 trillion | -10.0%
Estate and Gift Taxes | $0.03 trillion | $0.03 trillion | 0.0%
Customs Duties | $0.08 trillion | $0.10 trillion | -20.0%
Miscellaneous Receipts | $0.03 trillion | $0.04 trillion | -25.0%
TOTAL | $4.44 trillion | $4.90 trillion | -9.3%

Individual income taxes remain the single largest revenue source, accounting for 49.1 percent of all federal receipts in FY2023. Payroll taxes constitute the second-largest category at 36.3 percent, followed by corporate income taxes at 9.5 percent.

SECTION 2: INDIVIDUAL INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

The $450 billion decline in individual income tax collections represents the most significant driver of the overall revenue decrease. Three primary factors account for this contraction:

2.1 Capital Gains Realization Decline

Capital gains realizations fell sharply in calendar year 2022 following the broad equity market correction. The S&P 500 declined 19.4 percent in 2022, its worst annual performance since 2008. As a result, net long-term capital gains reported on individual returns filed in 2023 (covering tax year 2022) dropped to an estimated $1.1 trillion, down from $1.7 trillion reported in the prior year — a decline of approximately 35.3 percent. Capital gains taxes collected fell by an estimated $180 billion year-over-year.

2.2 Expiration of Pandemic-Era Tax Provisions

Several temporary tax provisions enacted under the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021 expired after tax year 2021. The expanded Child Tax Credit, which had been increased from $2,000 to $3,600 per qualifying child under age six and $3,000 per child ages six through seventeen, reverted to its pre-expansion level. This reversion reduced refundable credit outlays but also affected net revenue calculations. Additionally, the enhanced Earned Income Tax Credit for childless workers, which had temporarily increased the maximum credit from $543 to $1,502, expired, reducing refund amounts and modestly increasing net collections from lower-income filers.

2.3 Withholding and Estimated Payment Trends

Total individual income tax withholding from wages and salaries reached $1.89 trillion in FY2023, an increase of 4.2 percent over FY2022's $1.81 trillion, reflecting continued wage growth and low unemployment. The national unemployment rate averaged 3.6 percent across FY2023. However, estimated tax payments — which capture income from self-employment, investments, and pass-through business entities — declined by 31.4 percent, falling from $812 billion in FY2022 to $557 billion in FY2023, consistent with reduced investment income and lower pass-through distributions.

SECTION 3: PAYROLL TAX PERFORMANCE

Payroll tax collections of $1.61 trillion in FY2023 represent the strongest performance on record for this revenue category and reflect robust labor market conditions throughout the fiscal year. Key metrics include:

- Total wages and salaries subject to FICA taxation: $9.87 trillion
- Social Security (OASDI) tax collections: $1.10 trillion (6.2% employee + 6.2% employer rate applied to wages up to $160,200 wage base)
- Medicare (HI) tax collections: $0.38 trillion (1.45% employee + 1.45% employer, plus 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on earnings above $200,000 for single filers)
- Self-employment tax collections: $0.13 trillion
- Total nonfarm payroll employment averaged 155.8 million jobs during FY2023

The Social Security wage base increased from $147,000 in calendar year 2022 to $160,200 in calendar year 2023, a 8.98 percent increase, which contributed approximately $14 billion in additional OASDI collections.

SECTION 4: CORPORATE INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Corporate income tax collections held steady at $420 billion in FY2023, unchanged from FY2022. This stability is notable given the broader economic headwinds and reflects several offsetting factors:

The corporate tax rate remains at 21 percent, as established by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017. The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, signed by President Biden on August 16, 2022, introduced a new 15 percent Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT) applicable to corporations with average adjusted financial statement income exceeding $1 billion. The CAMT generated approximately $6.8 billion in additional collections during FY2023, its first full year of implementation.

The 1 percent excise tax on corporate stock buybacks, also enacted under the Inflation Reduction Act and effective January 1, 2023, generated $8.4 billion in FY2023 collections. Total corporate stock repurchases subject to the tax were estimated at $840 billion during the applicable period.

S-corporation and partnership pass-through income, which is taxed at the individual level rather than the corporate level, totaled an estimated $2.3 trillion in tax year 2022, contributing to individual income tax collections rather than corporate receipts.

SECTION 5: EXCISE TAXES AND CUSTOMS DUTIES

Federal excise tax collections totaled $87.4 billion in FY2023, down from $97.1 billion in FY2022. The largest components include:

- Highway Trust Fund excise taxes (motor fuel): $38.2 billion
- Tobacco taxes: $12.1 billion
- Alcohol taxes: $10.4 billion
- Air transportation taxes: $16.8 billion
- Other excise taxes: $9.9 billion

The decline in motor fuel excise revenues reflects a 2.1 percent reduction in taxable fuel consumption, consistent with increased fuel efficiency standards and modest growth in electric vehicle adoption. The federal gasoline tax rate remains at 18.4 cents per gallon, unchanged since 1993.

Customs duties fell to $80.2 billion from $99.8 billion in FY2022, a 19.6 percent decline, primarily attributable to reduced import volumes as consumer goods demand normalized following the post-pandemic surge and as supply chain disruptions eased.

SECTION 6: COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT

The IRS processed 271.5 million tax returns in FY2023, including 162.0 million individual income tax returns. The electronic filing rate for individual returns reached 93.8 percent, up from 91.2 percent in FY2022.

The gross tax gap — defined as the difference between taxes legally owed and taxes voluntarily and timely paid — was most recently estimated at $688 billion annually based on the IRS's Tax Gap Estimates for Tax Years 2014-2016, published in October 2019. Updated estimates incorporating more recent tax years are expected to be released in late 2024.

Enforcement activities in FY2023 generated $63.6 billion in additional revenue through audits, collections, and document matching programs. The IRS employed 89,700 full-time equivalent staff as of September 30, 2023, reflecting initial hiring under the $80 billion, ten-year funding authorization provided by the Inflation Reduction Act.

SECTION 7: PROJECTIONS FOR FY2024 AND FY2025

Based on current economic forecasts from the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) January 2024 Budget and Economic Outlook, federal tax revenues are projected as follows:

- FY2024 projected collections: $4.79 trillion (increase of 7.9% from FY2023)
- FY2025 projected collections: $5.04 trillion (increase of 5.2% from FY2024)

The projected recovery in FY2024 is driven primarily by anticipated stabilization in capital gains realizations, continued payroll tax growth consistent with projected GDP growth of 1.5 percent in calendar year 2024, and full-year CAMT and stock buyback excise tax collections.

Individual income taxes are projected to recover to $2.47 trillion in FY2024, representing a 13.3 percent increase from FY2023 levels, as equity market performance in calendar year 2023 — the S&P 500 gained 24.2 percent — is expected to generate higher capital gains realizations on returns filed in 2024.

CONCLUSION

Fiscal Year 2023 federal tax collections of $4.44 trillion, while representing a 9.3 percent decline from the prior year record, reflect a normalization from the exceptional conditions that elevated FY2022 revenues rather than a structural deterioration in the tax base. The underlying drivers — strong labor markets, stable corporate profitability, and continued expansion of the payroll tax base — remain intact. The primary headwind, reduced capital gains realizations, is expected to partially reverse in FY2024 based on improved equity market performance.
