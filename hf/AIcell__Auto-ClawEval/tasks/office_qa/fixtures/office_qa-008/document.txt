EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Total state tax revenue for fiscal year 2023 reached $29.4 billion, representing a 3.2 percent decline from the fiscal year 2022 record of $30.4 billion. Despite this year-over-year decrease, collections remained 18.7 percent above the pre-pandemic baseline established in fiscal year 2019, when total revenues stood at $24.8 billion. The decline was driven primarily by softening individual income tax receipts and a modest contraction in corporate franchise tax collections, partially offset by continued strength in sales and use tax revenues.

This report provides a comprehensive breakdown of revenue by source, year-over-year comparisons, economic context, and projections for fiscal year 2024.

SECTION 2: INDIVIDUAL INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Individual income tax remains the single largest revenue source, accounting for 50.9 percent of total state tax collections in fiscal year 2023. The $14.96 billion collected represents a $860 million decline from fiscal year 2022.

Key factors contributing to the decline include:

- Capital gains realizations fell sharply in calendar year 2022 following equity market corrections. The S&P 500 declined 19.4 percent in 2022, and Minnesota taxpayers reported approximately $4.1 billion less in capital gains income compared to the prior year.

- Wage and salary withholding, by contrast, remained relatively stable, growing 2.1 percent to $11.3 billion, reflecting continued labor market strength with Minnesota's unemployment rate averaging 2.8 percent through fiscal year 2023.

- Estimated payments from self-employed individuals and pass-through business owners declined 8.6 percent, consistent with reduced business income in sectors including technology, real estate, and financial services.

- The top marginal income tax rate in Minnesota remains 9.85 percent, applicable to taxable income above $183,340 for single filers and $304,970 for married filing jointly, as established under Minnesota Statutes Section 290.06.

Refunds issued during fiscal year 2023 totaled $2.87 billion, an increase of $143 million or 5.2 percent over fiscal year 2022, further reducing net income tax collections.

---

SECTION 3: SALES AND USE TAX ANALYSIS

Sales and use tax collections of $8.71 billion represent the strongest performance in this category on record, surpassing the previous high of $8.34 billion set in fiscal year 2022. The 4.4 percent growth rate outpaced the national average sales tax growth rate of 2.1 percent reported by the National Conference of State Legislatures for the same period.

Contributing factors include:

- Consumer spending on services continued to recover, with taxable service revenues increasing 6.8 percent year-over-year.
- Remote seller collections under Minnesota's economic nexus law, enacted following the 2018 South Dakota v. Wayfair Supreme Court decision, generated $1.14 billion in fiscal year 2023, up from $980 million in fiscal year 2022, a 16.3 percent increase.
- Food and prescription drugs remain exempt from Minnesota sales tax, representing an estimated $1.9 billion in foregone revenue annually.
- The general state sales tax rate of 6.875 percent has remained unchanged since 2009.

County and city sales taxes, which are collected by the state but remitted to local governments, totaled an additional $1.23 billion and are not included in the state revenue figures above.

---

SECTION 4: CORPORATE FRANCHISE TAX ANALYSIS

Corporate franchise tax collections declined to $2.98 billion in fiscal year 2023, a decrease of $310 million or 9.4 percent from the prior year. This category had experienced exceptional growth in fiscal years 2021 and 2022, when collections reached $2.61 billion and $3.29 billion respectively, driven by pandemic-era profit surges in manufacturing, logistics, and technology sectors.

The fiscal year 2023 decline reflects:

- Margin compression across multiple industries as input costs remained elevated while consumer demand moderated.
- Increased federal bonus depreciation deductions flowing through to state returns, reducing taxable net income.
- Minnesota's corporate tax rate of 9.8 percent, the third-highest in the nation, continues to generate policy discussion. The Legislature's Tax Committee, chaired by Representative Aisha Gomez, held hearings in February 2023 examining competitiveness implications.
- Audit and compliance activities generated $187 million in additional assessments during fiscal year 2023, compared to $162 million in fiscal year 2022.

---

SECTION 5: PROPERTY TAX AND DEED TRANSFER TAX

The statewide property tax, levied on commercial-industrial and seasonal recreational properties, generated $912 million in fiscal year 2023, a modest 2.5 percent increase. The levy is set annually by the Legislature and does not fluctuate with market values in the same manner as local property taxes.

Deed transfer tax collections fell 12.7 percent to $178 million, reflecting the significant slowdown in real estate transaction volume. The Minnesota Association of Realtors reported that existing home sales declined 22.4 percent in calendar year 2022 compared to 2021, with the trend continuing into early 2023. The deed transfer tax is levied at a rate of 0.33 percent of the net consideration on property transfers.

---

SECTION 6: CIGARETTE AND TOBACCO TAX

Cigarette and tobacco tax revenues declined to $396 million, continuing a multi-year downward trend driven by declining smoking rates. Minnesota levies a tax of $3.04 per pack of 20 cigarettes, among the highest rates in the Midwest. Taxable cigarette sales volume fell 5.8 percent to approximately 130 million packs in fiscal year 2023. E-cigarette and vapor product taxes, implemented under 2023 legislation effective October 1, 2023, are expected to generate an estimated $48 million in fiscal year 2024.

---

SECTION 7: YEAR-OVER-YEAR TREND ANALYSIS (FY2019–FY2023)

Total Tax Revenue Trend (in billions):
- FY2019: $24.8 billion
- FY2020: $23.6 billion (COVID-19 impact; -4.8%)
- FY2021: $27.1 billion (+14.8%)
- FY2022: $30.4 billion (+12.2%)
- FY2023: $29.4 billion (-3.3%)

The five-year compound annual growth rate from FY2019 to FY2023 is 4.3 percent, exceeding the average annual inflation rate of 3.8 percent over the same period as measured by the Consumer Price Index for the Minneapolis-St. Paul metropolitan area.

---

SECTION 8: FISCAL YEAR 2024 PROJECTIONS

The February 2023 Budget and Economic Forecast, prepared jointly by Minnesota Management and Budget and the Department of Revenue, projected fiscal year 2024 total tax revenues of $28.9 billion. Updated projections released in this report revise that estimate upward to $29.7 billion, reflecting stronger-than-anticipated sales tax performance in July and August 2023 and stabilization in income tax withholding.

Key assumptions underlying the revised projection:
- Minnesota GDP growth of 1.8 percent in calendar year 2023
- Unemployment rate remaining below 3.5 percent through June 2024
- No significant changes to federal tax law affecting state conformity
- Capital gains realizations recovering modestly, contributing an estimated additional $320 million compared to fiscal year 2023

---

SECTION 9: CONCLUSION AND POLICY CONSIDERATIONS

While fiscal year 2023 represented a modest contraction from the record collections of fiscal year 2022, Minnesota's tax revenue base remains structurally sound. The diversification across income, sales, and corporate sources provides resilience against sector-specific economic shocks.

The Department of Revenue recommends continued monitoring of remote seller compliance, estimated at 94.3 percent for registered sellers but potentially lower among unregistered entities. Additionally, the ongoing shift in consumer spending from goods to services presents both opportunities and challenges for sales tax base maintenance.

This report will be updated following the close of the first quarter of fiscal year 2024 (October 2023). Questions regarding methodology or data should be directed to the Revenue Research Division at revenue.research@state.mn.us.
