EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) analysis of the Federal Highway Trust Fund (HTF) covering the ten-year period from fiscal year 2014 through fiscal year 2024. The analysis encompasses both the Highway Account and the Mass Transit Account, examining revenue streams, expenditure patterns, and the impact of supplemental general fund transfers authorized by Congress. The HTF, established under the Highway Revenue Act of 1956, remains the primary financing mechanism for surface transportation infrastructure in the United States.

Over the ten-year analytical window, the Highway Account demonstrated a CAGR of 3.2 percent in total receipts, while the Mass Transit Account recorded a CAGR of 2.8 percent. These figures, while modest, reflect the structural challenges facing excise-tax-dependent trust funds in an era of increasing fuel efficiency and the gradual electrification of the vehicle fleet.

---

SECTION 1: FUND OVERVIEW AND STATUTORY AUTHORITY

The Federal Highway Trust Fund receives its primary revenue from federal motor fuel excise taxes established under 26 U.S.C. § 9503. The current federal gasoline tax rate of 18.4 cents per gallon has remained unchanged since 1993, and the diesel fuel tax rate of 24.4 cents per gallon has similarly been static for over three decades. The Mass Transit Account receives 2.86 cents of the gasoline tax and 2.86 cents of the diesel tax per gallon sold.

For fiscal year 2024, total HTF receipts from excise taxes reached $44.3 billion, with the Highway Account receiving $38.1 billion and the Mass Transit Account receiving $6.2 billion. These figures represent the baseline revenue before accounting for investment income and general fund transfers.

CAGR Computation Methodology:

The CAGR is calculated using the standard formula:

CAGR = [(Ending Value / Beginning Value) ^ (1 / Number of Years)] - 1

Highway Account CAGR (FY2014-FY2024):
CAGR = [($38.1B / $28.4B) ^ (1/10)] - 1
CAGR = [(1.3415) ^ (0.10)] - 1
CAGR = 1.0298 - 1
CAGR = 2.98%, rounded to 3.0%

Mass Transit Account CAGR (FY2014-FY2024):
CAGR = [($6.2B / $4.6B) ^ (1/10)] - 1
CAGR = [(1.3478) ^ (0.10)] - 1
CAGR = 1.0303 - 1
CAGR = 3.03%, rounded to 3.0%

Combined HTF CAGR (FY2014-FY2024):
CAGR = [($44.3B / $33.0B) ^ (1/10)] - 1
CAGR = [(1.3424) ^ (0.10)] - 1
CAGR = 1.0299 - 1
CAGR = 2.99%, rounded to 3.0%

Note: Fiscal year 2020 reflects a significant revenue contraction of 12.4 percent relative to fiscal year 2019, attributable to reduced vehicle miles traveled during the COVID-19 pandemic. This anomalous year depresses the ten-year CAGR by an estimated 0.4 percentage points relative to a trend-adjusted baseline.

SECTION 3: GENERAL FUND TRANSFERS AND THEIR IMPACT ON SOLVENCY

Congress has authorized eleven separate general fund transfers to the HTF since fiscal year 2008 to prevent insolvency. The most significant transfers relevant to this analytical period include:

- Fixing America's Surface Transportation (FAST) Act of 2015: Authorized $70.0 billion in general fund transfers over five years (FY2016-FY2020)
- Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) of November 15, 2021: Authorized $118.0 billion in general fund transfers to supplement HTF spending authority through fiscal year 2026
- Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2023: Provided an additional $4.6 billion supplemental transfer effective October 1, 2022

When general fund transfers are included in the total resources calculation, the effective CAGR of available HTF resources rises to 5.7 percent over the same ten-year period, reflecting the substantial congressional intervention required to maintain fund solvency.

---

SECTION 4: EXPENDITURE ANALYSIS

Total HTF outlays for fiscal year 2024 reached $67.4 billion, comprising $57.9 billion from the Highway Account and $9.5 billion from the Mass Transit Account. The expenditure CAGR over the ten-year period was 4.8 percent, substantially exceeding the organic revenue CAGR of 3.0 percent. This 1.8 percentage point gap between expenditure growth and revenue growth represents the structural imbalance that necessitates recurring general fund transfers.

Key expenditure categories for fiscal year 2024:

- National Highway Performance Program: $24.3 billion
- Surface Transportation Block Grant Program: $17.2 billion
- Highway Safety Improvement Program: $3.4 billion
- Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality Improvement Program: $2.7 billion
- Federal Transit Formula Grants: $7.1 billion
- Capital Investment Grants (New Starts): $2.4 billion
- Administrative and oversight costs: $0.8 billion

---

SECTION 5: INVESTMENT INCOME ANALYSIS

The HTF maintains uninvested balances in special-issue Treasury securities. Investment income for fiscal year 2024 totaled $1.84 billion, compared to $0.31 billion in fiscal year 2014, representing a CAGR of 19.6 percent. This dramatic growth reflects both higher interest rates following Federal Reserve policy actions beginning in March 2022 and larger average fund balances maintained as a buffer against insolvency.

The average interest rate earned on HTF investments in fiscal year 2024 was 4.73 percent, compared to 1.12 percent in fiscal year 2014.

---

SECTION 6: FORWARD PROJECTIONS AND SUSTAINABILITY CONCERNS

The Congressional Budget Office, in its August 2024 Budget and Economic Outlook, projected that the Highway Account will face a funding shortfall of approximately $22.0 billion annually by fiscal year 2028 if no new revenue measures are enacted following the expiration of IIJA supplemental transfers. The Mass Transit Account faces a projected annual shortfall of $4.1 billion over the same horizon.

Three primary factors are expected to suppress organic revenue growth below the historical 3.0 percent CAGR:

1. Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) standards requiring passenger vehicles to achieve 49.0 miles per gallon by model year 2026, reducing per-mile fuel consumption and associated tax revenue
2. Electric vehicle penetration projected to reach 18.4 percent of new vehicle sales by calendar year 2027, according to the Energy Information Administration's October 2024 Annual Energy Outlook
3. Continued population migration toward urban areas with lower per-capita vehicle miles traveled

The Office of Fiscal Analysis projects that organic HTF receipts will grow at a CAGR of only 1.4 percent between fiscal years 2024 and 2034 under current law, compared to the 3.0 percent CAGR observed in the preceding decade.

---

SECTION 7: COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS WITH SOCIAL SECURITY AND MEDICARE TRUST FUNDS

For contextual purposes, the HTF's 3.0 percent ten-year CAGR compares favorably with the Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund, which recorded a receipt CAGR of 4.1 percent over the same period, and unfavorably with the Hospital Insurance Trust Fund, which achieved a 5.3 percent CAGR driven by payroll tax base expansion. Unlike payroll-tax-funded trust funds, the HTF's fixed nominal tax rates create an inherent structural disadvantage in inflationary environments.

---

SECTION 8: CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The ten-year CAGR analysis confirms that the Federal Highway Trust Fund's organic revenue growth of 3.0 percent is structurally insufficient to support expenditure growth of 4.8 percent without sustained congressional intervention. The fund's dependence on a 31-year-old nominal tax rate represents the central policy challenge facing surface transportation finance.

The Office of Fiscal Analysis recommends that Congress consider the following options, which have been scored by the Joint Committee on Taxation:

- Indexing the 18.4 cents-per-gallon gasoline tax to the Consumer Price Index beginning January 1, 2026, which JCT estimates would generate an additional $8.3 billion annually by fiscal year 2030
- Establishing a vehicle miles traveled fee pilot program for electric vehicles, projected to generate $1.2 billion annually at full implementation
- Expanding the HTF's investment authority to include short-term agency securities, potentially increasing investment income by $0.4 billion annually
