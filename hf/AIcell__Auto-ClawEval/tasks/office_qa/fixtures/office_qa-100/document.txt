EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of federal government expenditure patterns across the five-year period from fiscal year 2019 through fiscal year 2024. Total federal outlays grew from $4.45 trillion in FY2019 to $6.13 trillion in FY2024, representing a cumulative increase of 37.8 percent over the review period. The analysis identifies mandatory spending as the dominant driver of growth, with discretionary spending increases concentrated primarily in defense and infrastructure. The Department of Health and Human Services recorded the single largest year-over-year increase in any category, rising 41.2 percent between FY2020 and FY2021 due to pandemic-related emergency appropriations.

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF TOTAL FEDERAL OUTLAYS

Federal expenditure has followed an accelerating upward trajectory since FY2019, with the most pronounced single-year increase occurring in FY2021 when total outlays reached $6.82 trillion, driven by emergency COVID-19 relief legislation including the American Rescue Plan Act signed on March 11, 2021. Outlays subsequently contracted in FY2022 and FY2023 as emergency programs expired, before stabilizing at $6.13 trillion in FY2024.

TOTAL FEDERAL OUTLAYS BY FISCAL YEAR (in trillions USD)

FY2019: $4.45 trillion
FY2020: $6.55 trillion (increase of 47.2%)
FY2021: $6.82 trillion (increase of 4.1%)
FY2022: $6.27 trillion (decrease of 8.1%)
FY2023: $6.13 trillion (decrease of 2.2%)
FY2024: $6.13 trillion (change of 0.0%, preliminary estimate)

The FY2020 spike of 47.2 percent represents the largest single-year percentage increase in federal outlays since World War II, attributable primarily to the CARES Act enacted on March 27, 2020, which authorized $2.2 trillion in emergency spending. The Congressional Budget Office projected in its January 2024 Budget and Economic Outlook that outlays will resume growth beginning in FY2025, reaching an estimated $6.49 trillion.

SECTION 2: MANDATORY SPENDING ANALYSIS

Mandatory spending, which encompasses Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and other entitlement programs, constituted 62.4 percent of total federal outlays in FY2024, compared to 61.1 percent in FY2019. This category has demonstrated consistent growth independent of annual appropriations cycles.

MANDATORY SPENDING BREAKDOWN — FY2024 (in billions USD)

Social Security: $1,348.7 billion (21.9% of total outlays)
Medicare: $869.4 billion (14.2% of total outlays)
Medicaid: $616.2 billion (10.0% of total outlays)
Income Security Programs: $412.8 billion (6.7% of total outlays)
Federal Civilian and Military Retirement: $183.6 billion (3.0% of total outlays)
Veterans Benefits: $172.4 billion (2.8% of total outlays)
Other Mandatory: $225.9 billion (3.7% of total outlays)

TOTAL MANDATORY SPENDING FY2024: $3,829.0 billion

Social Security outlays grew from $1,059.0 billion in FY2019 to $1,348.7 billion in FY2024, an increase of 27.4 percent over five years. The Social Security Administration attributed this growth to the retirement of approximately 10,000 Baby Boomers per day reaching eligibility age, a demographic trend expected to continue through 2030. The Cost-of-Living Adjustment applied in January 2023 was 8.7 percent, the largest COLA since 1981, adding an estimated $92 billion to annual Social Security outlays.

Medicare expenditure increased from $644.1 billion in FY2019 to $869.4 billion in FY2024, a 35.0 percent increase. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services reported that Medicare enrollment reached 65.7 million beneficiaries as of December 2023, up from 61.2 million in December 2019.

SECTION 3: DISCRETIONARY SPENDING ANALYSIS

Discretionary spending totaled $1,704.3 billion in FY2024, representing 27.8 percent of total outlays. Defense discretionary spending accounted for $858.5 billion of this total, while non-defense discretionary spending accounted for $845.8 billion.

DEFENSE DISCRETIONARY SPENDING (in billions USD)

FY2019: $676.1 billion
FY2020: $737.9 billion
FY2021: $753.5 billion
FY2022: $782.0 billion
FY2023: $820.3 billion
FY2024: $858.5 billion

Defense spending grew at a compound annual growth rate of 4.9 percent between FY2019 and FY2024. The National Defense Authorization Act for FY2024, signed by President Biden on December 22, 2023, authorized $886 billion in defense spending, though actual outlays lag authorizations due to multi-year procurement contracts. The Department of Defense's budget for FY2024 included $145.9 billion allocated to research, development, testing, and evaluation, a 6.2 percent increase from the FY2023 level of $137.4 billion.

NON-DEFENSE DISCRETIONARY HIGHLIGHTS — FY2024

Department of Transportation: $105.4 billion (reflecting Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act allocations)
Department of Education: $79.6 billion
Department of Veterans Affairs (discretionary portion): $119.8 billion
Department of Housing and Urban Development: $71.3 billion
National Institutes of Health: $47.2 billion
Department of Homeland Security: $63.7 billion

The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, enacted November 15, 2021, committed $1.2 trillion over ten years, with $550 billion representing new federal investment. Transportation Department outlays increased 34.1 percent between FY2021 and FY2024 as infrastructure funds began flowing to states and localities.

SECTION 4: NET INTEREST EXPENDITURE

Net interest on the federal debt has emerged as one of the fastest-growing expenditure categories, driven by rising interest rates and expanding debt levels. The Federal Reserve's rate-hiking cycle, which began in March 2022 and raised the federal funds rate from 0.25 percent to 5.50 percent by July 2023, substantially increased borrowing costs on newly issued and refinanced Treasury securities.

NET INTEREST EXPENDITURE (in billions USD)

FY2019: $375.6 billion
FY2020: $345.0 billion (decrease due to low interest rate environment)
FY2021: $352.2 billion
FY2022: $475.6 billion
FY2023: $659.8 billion
FY2024: $870.0 billion (preliminary estimate)

Net interest expenditure in FY2024 is projected to exceed $870 billion, surpassing defense discretionary spending for the first time in modern budget history. This represents a 131.6 percent increase from the FY2019 level of $375.6 billion. The Treasury Department reported that the average interest rate on outstanding federal debt rose from 1.6 percent in January 2022 to 3.1 percent in December 2023. Total federal debt held by the public reached $26.9 trillion as of September 30, 2023.

SECTION 5: DEPARTMENTAL EXPENDITURE COMPARISON

YEAR-OVER-YEAR CHANGE IN SELECTED DEPARTMENT OUTLAYS: FY2023 TO FY2024

Department of Health and Human Services: $1,712.4B to $1,698.3B (-0.8%)
Department of Defense: $820.3B to $858.5B (+4.7%)
Social Security Administration: $1,284.6B to $1,348.7B (+5.0%)
Department of Transportation: $98.7B to $105.4B (+6.8%)
Department of Veterans Affairs: $301.4B to $319.6B (+6.0%)
Department of Education: $76.2B to $79.6B (+4.5%)

The Department of Health and Human Services recorded a marginal decline in FY2024 as Medicaid enrollment corrections following the end of continuous enrollment provisions took effect. The Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2023 ended the COVID-19 public health emergency continuous enrollment requirement on March 31, 2023, resulting in approximately 8.7 million individuals being disenrolled from Medicaid by December 2023 according to KFF Health News tracking data.

SECTION 6: EXPENDITURE AS A PERCENTAGE OF GDP

Federal outlays as a share of gross domestic product provide a normalized measure of government spending relative to economic output.

FY2019: $4.45T outlays / $21.43T GDP = 20.8% of GDP
FY2020: $6.55T outlays / $21.06T GDP = 31.1% of GDP
FY2021: $6.82T outlays / $23.32T GDP = 29.2% of GDP
FY2022: $6.27T outlays / $25.74T GDP = 24.4% of GDP
FY2023: $6.13T outlays / $27.36T GDP = 22.4% of GDP
FY2024: $6.13T outlays / $28.18T GDP = 21.7% of GDP (estimated)

The FY2020 ratio of 31.1 percent of GDP was the highest recorded since FY1945, when wartime expenditure reached 41.9 percent of GDP. The post-pandemic normalization trend is evident, though the FY2024 ratio of 21.7 percent remains above the 50-year historical average of approximately 20.5 percent of GDP.

SECTION 7: KEY FINDINGS AND POLICY IMPLICATIONS

1. Net interest expenditure is the fastest-growing budget category, increasing 131.6 percent from FY2019 to FY2024, and is projected to consume 14.2 percent of total outlays in FY2024.

2. Mandatory spending programs are structurally expanding due to demographic pressures, with Social Security and Medicare together accounting for 36.1 percent of total FY2024 outlays.

3. Emergency pandemic spending added approximately $5.2 trillion in cumulative outlays above pre-pandemic baseline projections across FY2020 and FY2021.

4. Defense spending has grown at a 4.9 percent compound annual rate, outpacing inflation in most years of the review period.

5. Infrastructure investment is producing measurable increases in Transportation Department outlays, with a 34.1 percent increase from FY2021 to FY2024.

The Office of Budget Management recommends that the Joint Committee on Taxation and the Senate Budget Committee, chaired by Senator Sheldon Whitehouse as of January 2024, prioritize a comprehensive review of mandatory spending growth trajectories in conjunction with the FY2026 budget formulation process beginning in September 2024.
