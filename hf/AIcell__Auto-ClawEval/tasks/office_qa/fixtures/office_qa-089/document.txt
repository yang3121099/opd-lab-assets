EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents comprehensive commodity price statistics for the United States fiscal year ending September 30, 2023. Data was compiled from 47 primary commodity exchanges, federal procurement databases, and international trade records maintained by the U.S. Census Bureau. The findings reflect significant price normalization across most commodity sectors following the extraordinary volatility of 2021–2022, though select agricultural and critical mineral categories continued to exhibit elevated pricing relative to pre-pandemic baselines established in fiscal year 2019.

Overall, the Producer Price Index (PPI) for all commodities declined 4.7 percent year-over-year from FY2022 to FY2023, representing the largest single-year decline since FY2015, when the index fell 6.2 percent. Despite this aggregate decline, 14 of the 38 tracked commodity subcategories recorded price increases during the same period.

SECTION 1: ENERGY COMMODITIES

1.1 Crude Oil

West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil averaged $77.58 per barrel during FY2023, a decrease of 18.3 percent from the FY2022 average of $94.96 per barrel. The annual high of $93.68 per barrel was recorded on September 27, 2023, while the annual low of $66.74 per barrel occurred on March 17, 2023.

Brent crude, the international benchmark, averaged $82.14 per barrel in FY2023, down 16.9 percent from $98.84 per barrel in FY2022. The spread between Brent and WTI averaged $4.56 per barrel, slightly wider than the $3.88 average spread recorded in FY2022.

U.S. crude oil production reached 12.9 million barrels per day in September 2023, the highest monthly output since November 2019. Total domestic crude production for FY2023 was approximately 4.4 billion barrels, valued at approximately $341.4 billion at average annual prices.

1.2 Natural Gas

Henry Hub natural gas spot prices averaged $2.57 per million British thermal units (MMBtu) in FY2023, a dramatic decline of 59.4 percent from the FY2022 average of $6.33 per MMBtu. This represents the lowest annual average since FY2020, when prices averaged $2.13 per MMBtu.

The annual peak of $3.98 per MMBtu was recorded on January 13, 2023, driven by a cold weather system affecting the northeastern United States. The annual low of $1.73 per MMBtu occurred on April 3, 2023.

1.3 Refined Products

Retail gasoline prices averaged $3.53 per gallon nationally in FY2023, compared to $4.07 per gallon in FY2022, a decrease of 13.3 percent. No. 2 heating oil averaged $3.18 per gallon, down 22.7 percent from $4.11 per gallon in FY2022. Jet fuel averaged $2.89 per gallon, a decline of 24.8 percent from $3.84 per gallon in the prior fiscal year.

SECTION 2: AGRICULTURAL COMMODITIES

2.1 Grains and Oilseeds

Hard Red Winter (HRW) wheat experienced the largest percentage decline among major grains, falling 25.5 percent to $6.88 per bushel. This decline was attributed primarily to improved Black Sea export flows following diplomatic negotiations in July 2023 and above-average yields in Kansas, which produced 327 million bushels, up 38 percent from the drought-reduced 2022 harvest of 237 million bushels.

Rough rice was the notable exception, rising 9.1 percent to $17.90 per hundredweight (cwt), driven by export restrictions imposed by India beginning August 20, 2023, which affected approximately 40 percent of global rice trade.

2.2 Livestock and Meat

Live cattle futures averaged $166.43 per hundredweight in FY2023, an increase of 11.2 percent from $149.67 per hundredweight in FY2022. This marked the highest annual average on record, surpassing the previous record of $158.22 per hundredweight set in FY2015.

Lean hog prices averaged $82.17 per hundredweight, a decrease of 6.4 percent from $87.80 per hundredweight in FY2022. Feeder cattle averaged $224.56 per hundredweight, up 18.7 percent year-over-year, reflecting tight cattle supplies stemming from prolonged drought conditions in Texas and Oklahoma that reduced the national beef cow herd to 28.2 million head as of January 1, 2023, the smallest inventory since 1962.

2.3 Soft Commodities

Coffee (Arabica) averaged $1.74 per pound in FY2023, down 14.7 percent from $2.04 per pound in FY2022. Cotton (No. 2) averaged $0.83 per pound, a decline of 22.4 percent from $1.07 per pound. Cocoa prices rose 27.3 percent to $3,142 per metric ton, driven by adverse weather conditions in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana, which together account for approximately 65 percent of global cocoa production.

Sugar (No. 11) averaged $0.2263 per pound in FY2023, an increase of 31.8 percent from $0.1717 per pound in FY2022, reaching the highest annual average since FY2011.

SECTION 3: METALS AND MINERALS

3.1 Precious Metals

Gold averaged $1,928.70 per troy ounce in FY2023, an increase of 5.4 percent from $1,829.80 per troy ounce in FY2022. The annual high of $2,049.90 per troy ounce was recorded on May 4, 2023, while the annual low of $1,810.50 per troy ounce occurred on October 6, 2022.

Silver averaged $23.35 per troy ounce, a modest increase of 1.8 percent from $22.93 per troy ounce in FY2022. The gold-to-silver ratio averaged 82.6:1 during FY2023, compared to 79.8:1 in FY2022, indicating relative underperformance of silver versus gold.

Platinum averaged $1,001.40 per troy ounce, down 3.2 percent from $1,034.60 per troy ounce in FY2022. Palladium experienced the most significant decline among precious metals, falling 40.7 percent from $2,113.50 per troy ounce in FY2022 to $1,252.80 per troy ounce in FY2023, as automotive manufacturers accelerated the transition from palladium-intensive catalytic converter designs.

Nickel fell 30.9 percent to $10.23 per pound following a significant increase in Indonesian refined nickel supply, which grew by an estimated 35 percent in calendar year 2023.

3.3 Critical Minerals

Lithium carbonate (battery grade) averaged $27,450 per metric ton in FY2023, a decline of 68.4 percent from the FY2022 average of $86,900 per metric ton, as new production capacity in Chile, Australia, and China came online. Despite this decline, lithium prices remained 312 percent above the FY2019 pre-pandemic average of $6,660 per metric ton.

Cobalt averaged $16.82 per pound in FY2023, down 51.3 percent from $34.53 per pound in FY2022.

SECTION 4: GOVERNMENT PROCUREMENT IMPLICATIONS

Federal agencies procured approximately $84.7 billion in commodity-linked contracts during FY2023, compared to $96.3 billion in FY2022, a reduction of $11.6 billion or 12.0 percent. The Department of Defense accounted for $41.2 billion of total commodity procurement, primarily in petroleum products ($18.6 billion), metals ($12.4 billion), and food commodities ($10.2 billion).

The Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) conducted net purchases of 26.1 million barrels during FY2023 at an average cost of $76.98 per barrel, totaling approximately $2.01 billion in expenditures. This followed the historic drawdown of 180 million barrels authorized between November 2021 and December 2022.

SECTION 5: OUTLOOK AND RISK FACTORS

The CFTC and BLS project continued price moderation in energy commodities through the first half of FY2024, contingent on OPEC+ production decisions and Northern Hemisphere winter demand. Agricultural markets face elevated uncertainty due to the developing El Niño weather pattern, which the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) classified as a "strong" event as of October 2023, with potential adverse impacts on South American soybean and corn production.

Critical mineral prices, particularly lithium and cobalt, are expected to stabilize in the $22,000–$30,000 per metric ton and $15–$20 per pound ranges respectively, as demand from electric vehicle manufacturers continues to grow at an estimated 28 percent annually while new supply capacity absorbs near-term market pressure.

METHODOLOGY NOTE

Price data was sourced from the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME), Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), London Metal Exchange (LME), and USDA Agricultural Marketing Service daily price reports. All prices represent front-month futures settlement prices unless otherwise noted. Fiscal year calculations span October 1 through September 30. Currency conversions used the Federal Reserve H.10 statistical release average annual exchange rates.
