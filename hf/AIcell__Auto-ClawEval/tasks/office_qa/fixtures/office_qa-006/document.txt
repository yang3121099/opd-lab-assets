EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive computation and analysis of the Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) for the Federal Highway Trust Fund (HTF) across a ten-year period spanning fiscal years 2014 through 2024. The analysis encompasses both the Highway Account and the Mass Transit Account, examining revenue inflows, expenditure patterns, and net fund balances. The findings are intended to inform congressional budget deliberations, support the Federal Highway Administration's long-range planning activities, and fulfill statutory reporting requirements under 31 U.S.C. § 9601.

The Highway Trust Fund recorded a beginning balance of $8.37 billion as of October 1, 2013 (the opening of FY2014) and a closing balance of $56.84 billion as of September 30, 2024. The computed ten-year CAGR for the total fund balance is 21.1 percent, reflecting the substantial impact of general fund transfers authorized by Congress under the Fixing America's Surface Transportation (FAST) Act of 2015 and the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) of November 2021.

---

SECTION 1: BACKGROUND AND STATUTORY AUTHORITY

The Federal Highway Trust Fund was established by the Highway Revenue Act of 1956 and is funded primarily through excise taxes on motor fuels and related transportation products. The federal gasoline tax rate has remained fixed at 18.4 cents per gallon since 1993, while the diesel fuel tax rate stands at 24.4 cents per gallon. These rates have not been adjusted for inflation in over three decades, creating a structural revenue shortfall that has necessitated repeated congressional intervention through general fund transfers.

The Mass Transit Account, a sub-account of the HTF, receives 2.86 cents of every 18.4 cents collected from the gasoline excise tax, as established under the Surface Transportation Assistance Act of 1982.

This report was prepared pursuant to the requirements of the Federal Trust Fund Accountability Act and in accordance with guidance issued in Treasury Financial Manual Volume I, Part 2, Chapter 4700.

SECTION 2: METHODOLOGY FOR CAGR COMPUTATION

The Compound Annual Growth Rate is computed using the standard financial formula:

CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value) ^ (1 / Number of Years) — 1

For this analysis:
- Beginning Value (BV): Fund balance as of September 30, 2013 = $8,370,000,000
- Ending Value (EV): Fund balance as of September 30, 2024 = $56,840,000,000
- Number of Years (n): 10

CAGR = ($56,840,000,000 / $8,370,000,000) ^ (1/10) — 1
CAGR = (6.7909) ^ (0.10) — 1
CAGR = 1.2112 — 1
CAGR = 0.2112, or approximately 21.1 percent

Separate CAGR computations were performed for the Highway Account and the Mass Transit Account, as well as for excise tax revenues in isolation, to distinguish organic growth from legislatively appropriated transfers.

SECTION 4: CAGR BY ACCOUNT AND REVENUE CATEGORY

4.1 Highway Account CAGR

Beginning Balance (FY2013 close): $2,210 million
Ending Balance (FY2024 close): $38,950 million
Ten-Year CAGR: 33.0 percent

The Highway Account experienced the most dramatic growth, driven primarily by the $70 billion general fund transfer authorized under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act signed by President Biden on November 15, 2021. This single legislative action accounts for approximately 61 percent of the cumulative balance increase in the Highway Account over the analysis period.

4.2 Mass Transit Account CAGR

Beginning Balance (FY2013 close): $6,160 million
Ending Balance (FY2024 close): $17,890 million
Ten-Year CAGR: 11.2 percent

The Mass Transit Account demonstrated more moderate but consistent growth, reflecting both dedicated excise tax receipts and proportional shares of general fund transfers. The account received $10.4 billion under the IIJA allocation framework.

4.3 Excise Tax Revenue CAGR (Organic Revenue Only)

To isolate the performance of dedicated excise tax revenues from legislative transfers, the following CAGR was computed using only motor fuel tax receipts deposited to the HTF:

FY2014 Excise Tax Revenue: $38,820 million
FY2024 Excise Tax Revenue: $43,670 million
Ten-Year CAGR: 1.2 percent

This figure reflects the structural weakness of the HTF's organic revenue base. The 1.2 percent CAGR for excise tax receipts falls significantly below the 10-year average Consumer Price Index increase of 3.1 percent over the same period, confirming that the real purchasing power of dedicated HTF revenues has declined materially since FY2014.

---

SECTION 5: GENERAL FUND TRANSFERS — CUMULATIVE IMPACT

Congress has authorized the following general fund transfers to the Highway Trust Fund since FY2008. The transfers relevant to this analysis period are:

- Moving Ahead for Progress in the 21st Century Act (MAP-21), July 6, 2012: $18.8 billion (partially affecting FY2014 balances)
- FAST Act, December 4, 2015: $70.0 billion over five years, with $13.2 billion deposited in FY2016
- Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2021, December 27, 2020: $13.6 billion
- Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, November 15, 2021: $118.0 billion total HTF authorization, with $80.4 billion in direct general fund transfers over FY2022–FY2026

The cumulative general fund transfers deposited during FY2014 through FY2024 total approximately $97.3 billion, representing 58.4 percent of the total FY2024 fund balance.

SECTION 6: COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS — HTF VERSUS SOCIAL SECURITY TRUST FUNDS

For context, the Bureau of Fiscal Service computed comparable CAGR figures for the two Social Security Trust Funds over the same ten-year period:

Fund | FY2013 Balance | FY2024 Balance | 10-Year CAGR
OASI Trust Fund | $2,764.4 billion | $2,788.9 billion | 0.09%
DI Trust Fund | $90.4 billion | $196.7 billion | 8.1%
Federal Highway Trust Fund | $8.37 billion | $56.84 billion | 21.1%

The HTF's elevated CAGR relative to the Social Security trust funds reflects the extraordinary legislative interventions rather than superior organic financial performance. The OASI Trust Fund's near-zero CAGR reflects the well-documented demographic pressures on the program, as the fund is projected by the Social Security Board of Trustees to reach depletion by 2033.

---

SECTION 7: LIMITATIONS AND ANALYTICAL CAVEATS

The following limitations should be considered when interpreting the CAGR figures presented in this report:

1. The CAGR computation is sensitive to the selection of beginning and ending dates. The FY2014 opening balance was unusually depressed following the near-depletion of the HTF in FY2008, which required emergency transfers and artificially suppresses the denominator.

2. Fund balances do not represent liquid assets available for immediate obligation. Significant portions of the reported balances are committed to multi-year project agreements under the IIJA.

3. The CAGR does not capture intra-year volatility. The HTF balance fell below $4 billion in August 2014 before the FAST Act transfers stabilized the fund.

4. Inflation adjustment: In constant FY2014 dollars, applying the GDP deflator, the real CAGR of the total HTF balance is approximately 17.8 percent, compared to the nominal 21.1 percent.

---

SECTION 8: CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The ten-year CAGR of 21.1 percent for the Federal Highway Trust Fund total balance, while numerically impressive, is primarily a function of legislative appropriations rather than organic revenue growth. The excise tax revenue CAGR of 1.2 percent underscores the urgent need for structural reform of the HTF's dedicated revenue base.

The Office of Trust Fund Management recommends that the Office of Management and Budget and the Congressional Budget Office incorporate these CAGR figures into the FY2026 budget baseline projections. Furthermore, this office recommends that future HTF solvency analyses distinguish between legislatively transferred balances and organically generated revenues to provide a more accurate picture of the fund's financial health.
