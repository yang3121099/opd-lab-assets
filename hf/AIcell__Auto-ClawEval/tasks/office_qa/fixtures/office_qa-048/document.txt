FISCAL YEAR 2023 TAX REVENUE ANALYSIS
Annual Financial Report — Published March 15, 2024

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The United States federal government collected $4.44 trillion in total tax revenue during Fiscal Year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023), representing a decline of 9.3 percent from the $4.90 trillion collected in Fiscal Year 2022. This report, prepared under the direction of IRS Commissioner Daniel Werfel and Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen, provides a comprehensive breakdown of revenue streams, year-over-year comparisons, and contributing economic factors. The decline marks the first significant revenue contraction since Fiscal Year 2020, when pandemic-related disruptions reduced collections by 1.2 percent.

SECTION 1: TOTAL REVENUE OVERVIEW

Total federal tax receipts for FY2023 reached $4.44 trillion against total federal outlays of $6.13 trillion, producing a budget deficit of $1.69 trillion. This deficit represents an increase of approximately $320 billion compared to the FY2022 deficit of $1.37 trillion.

The revenue decline was driven primarily by three factors: a sharp reduction in individual nonwithheld income tax payments, lower capital gains realizations as equity markets corrected from 2021 highs, and reduced corporate profit margins across several key sectors including technology and financial services.

SECTION 2: INDIVIDUAL INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Individual income taxes remain the single largest source of federal revenue, accounting for 49.1 percent of total receipts in FY2023, down from 53.7 percent in FY2022. The $450 billion year-over-year decline is the largest single-year nominal drop in individual income tax collections since records began in their current form in 1954.

Withheld income taxes — those deducted directly from employee paychecks — actually increased by 7.2 percent to $1.87 trillion, reflecting continued labor market strength. The national unemployment rate averaged 3.6 percent across FY2023, and total private-sector wages and salaries grew by an estimated 5.8 percent year-over-year, reaching approximately $10.9 trillion.

The dramatic overall decline in individual income taxes was driven almost entirely by nonwithheld payments, which fell from approximately $760 billion in FY2022 to roughly $310 billion in FY2023 — a drop of 59.2 percent. This category includes estimated tax payments and final payments made at filing, both of which are heavily influenced by capital gains income.

Capital gains realizations are estimated to have fallen from $1.7 trillion in calendar year 2021 to approximately $863 billion in calendar year 2022 (the tax year most relevant to FY2023 collections), a decline of 49.2 percent. The S&P 500 index fell 19.4 percent in calendar year 2022, its worst annual performance since 2008, substantially reducing taxable gains from equity sales.

Refunds issued to individual taxpayers totaled $334.8 billion in FY2023, compared to $310.2 billion in FY2022, an increase of 7.9 percent. The average individual refund for tax year 2022 was $2,753, slightly below the $2,827 average for tax year 2021.

SECTION 3: PAYROLL TAX ANALYSIS

Payroll taxes, which fund Social Security and Medicare programs, grew to $1.61 trillion in FY2023, representing 36.3 percent of total federal revenue. This marks the highest payroll tax share of total revenue since 1998.

Social Security taxes (OASDI) contributed $1.23 trillion, while Medicare taxes (HI) contributed $0.38 trillion. The Social Security wage base increased from $147,000 in calendar year 2022 to $160,200 in calendar year 2023, a 9.0 percent increase — the largest percentage increase in the wage base since 1983. This adjustment, driven by elevated wage growth and inflation, contributed meaningfully to the payroll tax revenue increase.

The number of W-2 wage earners subject to payroll taxes reached approximately 168.4 million in FY2023, up from 165.1 million in FY2022. Average wages subject to Social Security taxation grew by an estimated 4.3 percent.

SECTION 4: CORPORATE INCOME TAX ANALYSIS

Corporate income tax receipts totaled $419.7 billion in FY2023, a modest decline of 2.3 percent from $429.6 billion in FY2022. Corporate taxes represented 9.5 percent of total federal revenue.

The corporate tax rate has remained at 21 percent since the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 reduced it from 35 percent. The Inflation Reduction Act, signed by President Biden on August 16, 2022, introduced a new 15 percent Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT) applicable to corporations with average annual adjusted financial statement income exceeding $1 billion. The CAMT generated approximately $4.8 billion in its first partial year of applicability in FY2023.

Corporate profitability varied significantly by sector:

- Financial sector pre-tax profits declined approximately 18 percent year-over-year as rising interest rates increased credit loss provisions
- Technology sector profits fell an estimated 12 percent as advertising revenue contracted and companies absorbed restructuring charges
- Energy sector profits remained elevated, with the S&P 500 Energy sector reporting aggregate pre-tax income of approximately $214 billion, up 3.1 percent from FY2022

The number of corporate returns filed for tax year 2021 (the most recently finalized data) was 6.1 million, of which approximately 1.9 million reported positive net income.

SECTION 5: EXCISE TAXES AND OTHER RECEIPTS

Federal excise taxes generated $87.4 billion in FY2023, down from $97.1 billion in FY2022. The decline reflects lower fuel excise tax receipts tied to reduced highway fuel consumption and the temporary suspension of the federal gas tax from June 1 through September 30, 2022, which affected comparative baseline figures.

Highway-related excise taxes totaled $39.2 billion. Tobacco excise taxes contributed $11.6 billion, continuing a long-term declining trend as smoking rates fall. Alcohol excise taxes generated $10.1 billion. Aviation-related excise taxes recovered to $16.8 billion as air travel volumes returned to near pre-pandemic levels.

Customs duties fell sharply to $77.1 billion from $99.4 billion in FY2022, a 22.4 percent decline, reflecting reduced import volumes as consumer spending shifted from goods toward services and as some tariff exclusions were extended.

Estate and gift taxes generated $34.1 billion, a modest increase from $33.0 billion in FY2022. The estate tax exemption for 2023 was $12.92 million per individual, up from $12.06 million in 2022, indexed for inflation.

SECTION 6: COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT

The IRS processed approximately 271.5 million tax returns and supplemental documents in FY2023. Electronic filing continued to expand, with 93.8 percent of individual returns filed electronically, up from 91.4 percent in FY2022.

The gross tax gap — defined as the difference between taxes legally owed and taxes voluntarily and timely paid — was most recently estimated at $688 billion annually based on tax years 2014 through 2016, the latest period for which comprehensive estimates are available. The IRS collected an additional $63 billion through enforcement activities in FY2023, including audits, automated underreporter programs, and collection actions.

The IRS audit rate for individual returns was 0.38 percent in FY2022 (the most recently published figure), representing a continued decline from 0.45 percent in FY2019. Audit rates for high-income filers (those reporting $10 million or more in income) were 8.7 percent, compared to 0.19 percent for filers reporting under $200,000.

SECTION 7: TEN-YEAR REVENUE TREND

Federal tax receipts as a percentage of GDP in FY2023 were 16.5 percent, down from 19.6 percent in FY2022 and below the 50-year historical average of approximately 17.4 percent. The FY2022 figure of 19.6 percent was the highest since 2000, when receipts reached 20.0 percent of GDP during the dot-com boom.

Nominal revenue has grown from $2.16 trillion in FY2013 to $4.44 trillion in FY2023, representing compound annual growth of approximately 7.5 percent over the decade.

SECTION 8: OUTLOOK FOR FISCAL YEAR 2024

The Congressional Budget Office, in its February 2024 Budget and Economic Outlook, projected FY2024 federal revenues of $4.93 trillion, representing a recovery of approximately 11.0 percent from FY2023 levels. This projection assumes continued labor market strength, partial recovery in capital gains realizations, and full-year implementation of the Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax.

Key risks to the revenue outlook include potential softening in labor markets, continued volatility in equity and real estate asset prices, and uncertainty surrounding the scheduled expiration of numerous individual income tax provisions from the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act at the end of calendar year 2025.
