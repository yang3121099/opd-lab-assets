EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents comprehensive commodity price statistics for fiscal year 2023, covering energy, agricultural, and metals markets across domestic and international trading platforms. Data was compiled from the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME), the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX), the Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), and federal agency price monitoring programs. The report reflects calendar year data from January 1, 2023 through December 31, 2023, with select comparisons to fiscal years 2021 and 2022.

Overall commodity price indices declined 12.4 percent in 2023 compared to 2022, reversing a two-year inflationary trend driven primarily by the post-pandemic supply chain recovery and the normalization of energy markets following the acute disruptions of 2022. Despite this broad decline, select agricultural commodities and base metals recorded year-over-year price increases, reflecting localized supply constraints and sustained demand from manufacturing sectors.

SECTION 1: ENERGY COMMODITIES

1.1 Crude Oil

West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil averaged $77.58 per barrel in 2023, representing a decline of 17.3 percent from the 2022 annual average of $93.82 per barrel. The highest single-day closing price for WTI in 2023 was $93.68 per barrel, recorded on September 27, 2023, following OPEC+ production cut announcements. The lowest closing price was $66.74 per barrel, recorded on March 17, 2023, amid banking sector instability and concerns over reduced industrial demand.

Brent crude, the international benchmark, averaged $82.17 per barrel in 2023, down 16.1 percent from $97.91 per barrel in 2022.

U.S. crude oil production reached a record 12.9 million barrels per day in December 2023, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), surpassing the previous record of 12.8 million barrels per day set in November 2019.

1.2 Natural Gas

Henry Hub natural gas spot prices averaged $2.74 per million British thermal units (MMBtu) in 2023, a dramatic decline of 59.6 percent from the 2022 average of $6.78 per MMBtu. This decline was attributed to record domestic production levels, mild winter temperatures across the northeastern United States, and elevated storage inventories. As of November 30, 2023, U.S. natural gas storage stood at 3,827 billion cubic feet, approximately 7.2 percent above the five-year seasonal average.

1.3 Refined Products

Retail gasoline prices averaged $3.53 per gallon nationally in 2023, compared to $3.99 per gallon in 2022, a decrease of 11.5 percent. The national average peaked at $3.88 per gallon on September 18, 2023, and reached a yearly low of $3.10 per gallon on December 26, 2023. Ultra-low sulfur diesel averaged $4.05 per gallon in 2023, down from $5.11 per gallon in 2022, a reduction of 20.7 percent.

SECTION 2: AGRICULTURAL COMMODITIES

2.1 Grains

TABLE 1: Annual Average Grain Prices, 2021-2023 (per bushel, CME front-month contracts)

Commodity | 2021 Avg | 2022 Avg | 2023 Avg | 2022-2023 Change
Corn | $5.62 | $6.54 | $4.93 | -24.6%
Soybeans | $13.04 | $14.72 | $13.38 | -9.1%
Wheat (CBOT) | $7.77 | $9.15 | $6.27 | -31.5%
Oats | $4.18 | $4.56 | $3.71 | -18.6%

Wheat prices experienced the sharpest decline among major grains, falling 31.5 percent year-over-year. This was primarily attributable to record harvests in Australia (a crop of approximately 34.1 million metric tons in the 2022-23 season) and improved Black Sea export flows following diplomatic negotiations in the first quarter of 2023. Corn prices fell 24.6 percent, driven by a U.S. corn harvest of 15.34 billion bushels in 2023, the third-largest on record according to the USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service (NASS).

2.2 Livestock and Dairy

Live cattle futures averaged $1.7285 per pound in 2023, an increase of 14.2 percent from the 2022 average of $1.5135 per pound, reflecting tight domestic cattle supplies and strong export demand, particularly from Japan and South Korea. Lean hog futures averaged $0.7812 per pound, a decline of 8.3 percent from 2022.

Class III milk futures, the benchmark for cheese pricing, averaged $15.97 per hundredweight (cwt) in 2023, down sharply from $22.11 per cwt in 2022, a decline of 27.8 percent. This decline contributed to significant financial stress among dairy producers in Wisconsin, California, and New York, with the USDA reporting a net reduction of 1,847 licensed dairy operations nationally during calendar year 2023.

2.3 Soft Commodities

Arabica coffee futures (ICE) averaged $1.6842 per pound in 2023, up 4.1 percent from $1.6178 per pound in 2022. Raw sugar No. 11 futures averaged 24.07 cents per pound, an increase of 18.4 percent from 20.33 cents per pound in 2022, driven by production shortfalls in India and Thailand. Cocoa futures reached a 46-year high, averaging $3,471 per metric ton in 2023, compared to $2,394 per metric ton in 2022, an increase of 45.0 percent, with prices continuing to rise into early 2024 due to severe crop disease in West Africa.

SECTION 3: METALS AND MINERALS

3.1 Precious Metals

Gold averaged $1,940.54 per troy ounce in 2023, an increase of 8.2 percent from the 2022 average of $1,793.40 per troy ounce. Gold reached an intraday record high of $2,135.39 per troy ounce on December 4, 2023, surpassing the previous record of $2,089.20 set in August 2020. Silver averaged $23.35 per troy ounce in 2023, essentially flat compared to $21.73 in 2022, a modest increase of 7.5 percent.

Nickel experienced the most significant price decline among base metals, falling 36.1 percent to an average of $16,382 per metric ton. This decline followed the extraordinary price spike of March 2022, when nickel briefly exceeded $100,000 per metric ton on the London Metal Exchange (LME), and reflected a substantial increase in Indonesian nickel production, which reached approximately 1.8 million metric tons in 2023.

3.3 Industrial Minerals

Lithium carbonate prices, tracked by the Benchmark Mineral Intelligence index, fell precipitously in 2023, declining from a January 2023 opening price of approximately $78,000 per metric ton to $16,500 per metric ton by December 31, 2023, a decline of 78.8 percent over the calendar year. This collapse followed a period of extreme speculation and reflected rapid capacity expansion in Chinese lithium processing facilities.

SECTION 4: PRODUCER PRICE INDEX — COMMODITY COMPONENTS

The Bureau of Labor Statistics Producer Price Index (PPI) for crude materials for further processing declined 14.7 percent on an annual average basis in 2023, following an increase of 22.3 percent in 2022. The PPI for processed goods for intermediate demand declined 4.9 percent in 2023 after rising 15.6 percent in 2022.

Key PPI commodity sub-indices for December 2023 (year-over-year change):
- Crude petroleum: -7.1%
- Natural gas: -38.4%
- Iron and steel scrap: -12.6%
- Softwood lumber: +3.2%
- Corn: -28.7%
- Soybeans: -11.4%
- Cattle: +13.9%

SECTION 5: POLICY IMPLICATIONS AND OUTLOOK

The broad-based commodity price deflation observed in 2023 provided meaningful relief to downstream manufacturers and consumers, contributing to the deceleration of the Consumer Price Index from 6.5 percent in January 2023 to 3.4 percent in December 2023. The Federal Reserve's Open Market Committee cited declining commodity input costs as a contributing factor in its December 13, 2023 policy statement.

However, several commodity markets present upside price risks entering 2024. Cocoa and sugar markets remain structurally tight. Cattle supplies are projected to remain constrained through at least mid-2025 given the current U.S. beef cow herd of approximately 28.2 million head, the smallest since 1962 according to the USDA January 2024 Cattle report. Geopolitical risks in the Middle East and ongoing OPEC+ production management continue to create uncertainty in crude oil markets.

The CFTC's Market Risk Advisory Committee will convene on March 19, 2024 to review speculative position limits in agricultural futures markets in light of 2023 trading patterns.

DATA SOURCES AND METHODOLOGY

Price data was sourced from CME Group daily settlement prices, ICE Futures U.S. end-of-day reports, London Metal Exchange official cash settlement prices, and the EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report. Agricultural production data was obtained from USDA NASS reports and the USDA World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates (WASDE) monthly series. All prices are denominated in U.S. dollars unless otherwise noted. Annual averages represent arithmetic means of daily settlement prices for the nearest active futures contract unless otherwise specified.
