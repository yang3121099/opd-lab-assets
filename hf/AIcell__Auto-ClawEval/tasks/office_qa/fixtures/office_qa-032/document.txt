EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comparative analysis of appropriated and expended budgets across major Commonwealth departments for fiscal year 2023–2024 (July 1, 2023 through June 30, 2024) relative to fiscal year 2022–2023. The analysis was prepared under the direction of Budget Secretary Uri Monson and reflects final certified expenditure data submitted by departmental comptrollers as of August 31, 2024.

Total general fund appropriations for FY2023–2024 reached $47.6 billion, representing an increase of $2.1 billion, or 4.6 percent, over the FY2022–2023 appropriated level of $45.5 billion. Actual expenditures across all departments totaled $46.8 billion, leaving an unspent balance of $812.4 million, or approximately 1.7 percent of total appropriations. This compares favorably to FY2022–2023, when unspent balances totaled $1.04 billion, or 2.3 percent of appropriations.

SECTION 1: METHODOLOGY AND DATA SOURCES

Budget figures presented in this report are drawn from the Commonwealth's Integrated Enterprise System (IES), the official financial management platform maintained by the Office of Comptroller Operations. Departmental expenditure data was certified by individual agency comptrollers and cross-referenced against Treasury disbursement records maintained by Treasurer Stacy Garrity's office.

All figures represent general fund appropriations unless otherwise noted. Federal funds, augmentations, and restricted receipts are reported separately in Appendix C of the full budget document. Year-over-year comparisons use the enacted budget as the baseline for each respective fiscal year. Supplemental appropriations approved mid-year are incorporated into the final appropriated totals.

SECTION 3: NOTABLE BUDGET CHANGES AND ANALYSIS

3.1 Department of Education — Largest Absolute Increase

The Department of Education recorded the largest absolute year-over-year increase among all departments, with appropriations rising $688.4 million, or 8.6 percent. This increase reflects the implementation of the Level Up funding formula adjustment mandated by Act 55 of 2023, which directed an additional $567 million toward the 100 lowest-wealth school districts in the Commonwealth. Secretary Khalid Mumin's department also received $84.3 million in new appropriations for early childhood education expansion, targeting enrollment growth in Pre-K Counts and Head Start Supplemental Assistance programs. The department's expenditure rate of 99.7 percent was the highest among all major agencies.

3.2 Department of Health — Largest Percentage Increase

The Department of Health recorded the largest percentage increase at 11.2 percent, with appropriations growing from $1,218.5 million to $1,344.7 million. The primary driver was a $97.6 million increase in the Medical Assistance — Community HealthChoices waiver program, which expanded home and community-based services to an additional 14,200 Pennsylvanians with physical disabilities. An additional $29.5 million was directed toward the Opioid Use Disorder Treatment and Recovery line item, reflecting the continued prioritization of substance use disorder services under Secretary Dr. Debra Bogen.

3.3 Department of Human Services — Largest Total Appropriation

The Department of Human Services retained the largest general fund appropriation of any Commonwealth agency at $14,771.8 million, representing 31.0 percent of total general fund spending. Medical Assistance accounted for $9,814.6 million of this total, or 66.4 percent of the department's budget. The Children and Youth line item increased by $43.2 million to support county child welfare agencies serving approximately 68,000 children statewide.

3.4 Office of Administration — Smallest Growth Rate

The Office of Administration recorded the smallest year-over-year appropriation increase at 3.1 percent, or $11.3 million. The office's expenditure rate of 94.5 percent was the lowest among the twelve departments reviewed, reflecting $18.1 million in unspent funds primarily attributable to delayed implementation of the Commonwealth's new cybersecurity infrastructure contract, which was awarded to Leidos Government Services on March 7, 2024, but did not reach full operational status until after fiscal year close.

SECTION 4: EXPENDITURE RATE ANALYSIS

Overall expenditure efficiency improved modestly in FY2023–2024. The aggregate expenditure rate across the twelve departments reviewed was 98.4 percent, compared to 97.7 percent in FY2022–2023. The following departments achieved expenditure rates above 99 percent: Department of Education (99.7%), Department of Human Services (99.4%), and Pennsylvania State Police (99.0%).

Departments with expenditure rates below 96 percent were flagged for review under the Governor's Efficiency in Government Initiative, launched by Governor Josh Shapiro on January 18, 2024. The Office of Administration (94.5%) and the Department of Labor and Industry (96.0%) will be required to submit variance explanations to the Bureau of Financial Management by October 31, 2024.

SECTION 5: PERSONNEL COST TRENDS

Personnel costs, including salaries, wages, and employer benefit contributions, represented $18.3 billion of total expenditures across all departments, or 39.1 percent of the general fund total. This compares to $17.6 billion, or 38.7 percent, in FY2022–2023. The increase of $700 million reflects the implementation of the SEIU Local 668 collective bargaining agreement ratified on November 4, 2022, which provided a 3.25 percent general wage increase effective July 1, 2023, and a 3.0 percent increase effective January 1, 2024.

The Department of Corrections reported the highest personnel cost as a share of total expenditures at 74.3 percent, or approximately $2,450.8 million, reflecting the labor-intensive nature of correctional facility operations across the Commonwealth's 23 state correctional institutions.

SECTION 6: FORWARD PROJECTIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

Based on current expenditure trends and enacted appropriations, the Bureau of Financial Management projects that FY2024–2025 general fund expenditures will reach approximately $49.1 billion, representing a projected growth rate of 4.9 percent. The Department of Human Services is expected to account for the largest share of growth, driven by projected Medical Assistance enrollment increases of 2.8 percent and continued expansion of long-term care waiver programs.

The Bureau recommends that departments with expenditure rates below 96 percent conduct quarterly variance reviews beginning in the first quarter of FY2024–2025 and submit corrective action plans where systemic underspending is identified. Additionally, the Bureau recommends that the Department of Transportation accelerate encumbrance of its $24.4 million unspent infrastructure maintenance allocation before the December 31, 2024 lapse deadline.
