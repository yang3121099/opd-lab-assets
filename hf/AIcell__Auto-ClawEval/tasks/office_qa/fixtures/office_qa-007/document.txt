EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania's financial performance for the third quarter of Fiscal Year 2023-2024 (January 1, 2024 through March 31, 2024), with comparative analysis against the second quarter (October 1, 2023 through December 31, 2023). Total General Fund revenues for Q3 FY2023-24 reached $11.84 billion, representing a 6.2 percent increase over Q2 revenues of $11.15 billion. Total expenditures for Q3 stood at $11.61 billion, compared to $12.38 billion in Q2, reflecting a 6.2 percent decrease driven primarily by reduced Medicaid disbursements and the timing of capital project payments.

The Commonwealth ended Q3 with a positive operating balance of $230 million, a significant improvement over the Q2 operating deficit of $1.23 billion. Secretary of the Budget Uri Monson noted that the Q3 results reflect stronger-than-anticipated personal income tax collections and continued moderation in human services spending growth.

1.2 Key Revenue Observations

Personal Income Tax (PIT) collections of $4.89 billion in Q3 represent the highest single-quarter PIT collection in Commonwealth history, surpassing the previous record of $4.71 billion set in Q3 FY2022-23. The 13.4 percent quarter-over-quarter increase reflects strong January and February wage withholding tied to year-end bonuses in the financial services and technology sectors, as well as estimated payments from self-employed individuals whose Q4 2023 federal estimated tax deadline fell within the quarter.

Sales and Use Tax collections declined 4.5 percent from Q2, falling from $3.19 billion to $3.04 billion. This decline is consistent with historical seasonal patterns, as the holiday shopping surge that inflates Q2 sales tax receipts normalizes in Q3. January 2024 retail sales in Pennsylvania were down 2.1 percent compared to December 2023, according to data from the Pennsylvania Department of Revenue's monthly tracking report.

Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT) collections increased 15.4 percent quarter-over-quarter to $1.39 billion. This increase is partially attributable to the continued phase-down of the CNIT rate, which dropped from 8.99 percent to 8.49 percent on January 1, 2024, under Act 53 of 2022. Counterintuitively, the rate reduction accelerated estimated payment timing as corporate taxpayers adjusted their payment schedules, resulting in a front-loading of Q3 receipts.

2.2 Key Expenditure Observations

Human Services spending, which encompasses the Department of Human Services' Medical Assistance (Medicaid), CHIP, and cash assistance programs, declined by $228.8 million (3.9 percent) from Q2 to Q3. The Q2 figure was elevated by a $312 million retroactive Medicaid managed care rate adjustment paid to UPMC Health Plan, Geisinger Health Plan, and other managed care organizations in November 2023. Absent this one-time payment, underlying Human Services spending grew approximately 1.4 percent quarter-over-quarter, consistent with enrollment trends.

The Department of Corrections expenditure increased 1.8 percent to $761.9 million, reflecting a state prison population of 38,247 as of March 31, 2024, up from 37,891 as of December 31, 2023. The average daily cost per inmate rose to $119.84 in Q3, compared to $118.62 in Q2.

The "Other Expenditures" category declined sharply by $306.9 million (32.2 percent), primarily because Q2 included a $289 million payment to the Pennsylvania Infrastructure Investment Authority (PENNVEST) for water and sewer infrastructure projects that was not replicated in Q3.

Higher Education appropriations increased 6.0 percent to $412.8 million, reflecting the second installment of the 7.1 percent base funding increase for the State System of Higher Education (PASSHE) approved in the FY2023-24 enacted budget.

SECTION 3: YEAR-TO-DATE PERFORMANCE VERSUS ESTIMATE

As of March 31, 2024 (nine months into the fiscal year), cumulative General Fund revenues total $34.21 billion against the full-year estimate of $44.39 billion established in the Governor's Executive Budget presented February 6, 2024. Year-to-date revenues represent 77.1 percent of the annual estimate, which is 1.3 percentage points ahead of the 75.8 percent pace recorded at the same point in FY2022-23.

Year-to-date expenditures of $35.44 billion represent 79.8 percent of the full-year appropriated amount of $44.39 billion. The year-to-date operating deficit of $1.23 billion is expected to be eliminated in Q4, which historically captures the highest revenue quarter due to final income tax return payments due April 15.

The Rainy Day Fund balance as of March 31, 2024 stands at $5.12 billion, unchanged from the December 31, 2023 balance. No deposits or withdrawals were made during Q3. This balance represents approximately 11.5 percent of annual General Fund revenues, exceeding the Government Finance Officers Association's recommended minimum of 16.7 percent of annual expenditures — a threshold Pennsylvania crossed for the first time in FY2021-22.

SECTION 4: SPECIAL FUNDS AND MOTOR LICENSE FUND

The Motor License Fund (MLF), which supports PennDOT highway and bridge programs, recorded Q3 revenues of $1.14 billion, a 2.8 percent increase over Q2 revenues of $1.11 billion. Motor fuel tax collections of $612.4 million accounted for 53.7 percent of MLF revenues. Vehicle registration and licensing fees contributed $287.6 million.

The Lottery Fund reported Q3 net revenues (after prize payments) of $314.7 million, compared to $298.3 million in Q2, a 5.5 percent increase. Instant ticket sales drove the improvement, with gross instant ticket sales reaching $891.4 million in Q3. All Lottery Fund net revenues are dedicated to programs for older Pennsylvanians, including PACE prescription assistance and senior centers.

SECTION 5: OUTLOOK AND RISK FACTORS

The Office of the Budget projects Q4 FY2023-24 General Fund revenues of approximately $12.55 billion, which would bring the full-year total to approximately $46.76 billion, exceeding the enacted estimate by roughly $2.37 billion (5.3 percent). This projection is contingent on the following assumptions:

- Final personal income tax return payments due April 15, 2024 meet or exceed the $3.8 billion baseline established from preliminary Department of Revenue modeling
- No extraordinary Medicaid expenditure acceleration occurs prior to June 30, 2024
- Federal reimbursement rates under the enhanced FMAP (Federal Medical Assistance Percentage) of 6.2 percent, extended through December 2023, do not generate retroactive clawback adjustments

Primary downside risks include potential softening in corporate profits if Q1 2024 earnings reports from Pennsylvania-headquartered firms underperform expectations, and the possibility of supplemental appropriations requests from the Department of Human Services related to behavioral health system transformation costs estimated at $180 million to $240 million.
