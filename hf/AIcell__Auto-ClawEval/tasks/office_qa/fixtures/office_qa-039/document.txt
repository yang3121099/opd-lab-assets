Published by the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) in coordination with the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)
Release Date: February 14, 2024
Report Reference Number: CFTC-BLS-2024-0047

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents comprehensive commodity price statistics for fiscal year 2023 (October 1, 2022 through September 30, 2023), compiled from data submitted by registered commodity exchanges, federal price monitoring programs, and international trade partners. The findings reflect significant volatility across energy, agricultural, and metals sectors, driven by geopolitical disruptions, supply chain normalization following the COVID-19 pandemic, and shifting monetary policy from the Federal Reserve.

The Producer Price Index (PPI) for all commodities declined 4.7 percent on a year-over-year basis in FY2023, reversing the 14.3 percent increase recorded in FY2022. Total commodity trading volume on U.S. exchanges reached $38.4 trillion in notional value during the reporting period.

SECTION 1: ENERGY COMMODITIES

1.1 Crude Oil

West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil averaged $78.26 per barrel during FY2023, representing a decline of 21.4 percent from the FY2022 average of $99.55 per barrel. The annual high of $93.68 per barrel was recorded on September 27, 2023, while the annual low of $66.74 per barrel occurred on March 17, 2023.

Brent crude oil, the international benchmark, averaged $82.49 per barrel for the fiscal year, down from $104.11 per barrel in FY2022. The spread between Brent and WTI averaged $4.23 per barrel, slightly wider than the $4.56 average recorded in FY2021.

U.S. crude oil production averaged 12.9 million barrels per day during FY2023, surpassing the previous record of 12.8 million barrels per day set in November 2019. The Permian Basin in Texas and New Mexico accounted for approximately 46 percent of total domestic production.

1.2 Natural Gas

Henry Hub natural gas spot prices averaged $2.67 per million British thermal units (MMBtu) in FY2023, a dramatic decline of 58.3 percent from the FY2022 average of $6.40 per MMBtu. This represented the lowest annual average price since FY2020, when prices averaged $2.14 per MMBtu.

The price collapse was attributed primarily to record domestic production levels, mild winter temperatures across the continental United States, and reduced European export demand following the stabilization of liquefied natural gas (LNG) supply chains. U.S. LNG exports averaged 11.4 billion cubic feet per day during the fiscal year.

1.3 Refined Products

Retail gasoline prices averaged $3.53 per gallon nationally in FY2023, compared to $4.06 per gallon in FY2022. The highest regional average was recorded in California at $4.89 per gallon, while the lowest was in Mississippi at $3.07 per gallon. Ultra-low sulfur diesel averaged $4.12 per gallon nationally, down from $5.14 per gallon in FY2022.

Wheat prices fell substantially as Black Sea export corridors partially reopened and global supply concerns eased relative to the acute disruptions of FY2022.

2.2 Livestock and Meat

Live cattle futures averaged $172.48 per hundredweight (cwt) in FY2023, an increase of 14.6 percent from $150.51 per cwt in FY2022. This represented the highest annual average on record, driven by tight cattle supplies resulting from prolonged drought conditions across the Southern Plains.

Lean hog futures averaged $82.34 per cwt, down 12.7 percent from $94.32 per cwt in FY2022. The USDA reported that commercial hog slaughter totaled 131.4 million head during the fiscal year.

2.3 Soft Commodities

- Arabica coffee (ICE front-month) averaged $1.74 per pound, down from $2.21 per pound in FY2022, a decline of 21.3 percent.
- Raw sugar (ICE No. 11 contract) averaged 22.8 cents per pound, up 18.8 percent from 19.2 cents per pound in FY2022, reaching the highest annual average since FY2011.
- Cotton (ICE No. 2 contract) averaged 83.6 cents per pound, down 22.4 percent from 107.7 cents per pound in FY2022.
- Cocoa (ICE front-month) averaged $2,987 per metric ton, up 14.2 percent from $2,616 per metric ton in FY2022, reflecting supply concerns from West Africa.

SECTION 3: METALS AND MINERALS

3.1 Precious Metals

Gold futures on the COMEX division of the New York Mercantile Exchange averaged $1,941.60 per troy ounce in FY2023, an increase of 6.2 percent from $1,828.40 per troy ounce in FY2022. Gold reached an intraday high of $2,009.40 per troy ounce on May 4, 2023, briefly surpassing the psychologically significant $2,000 threshold.

Silver averaged $23.44 per troy ounce, essentially flat compared to $23.71 per troy ounce in FY2022, representing a decline of 1.1 percent. The gold-to-silver ratio averaged 82.8:1 during the fiscal year, above the 20-year historical average of approximately 68:1.

Platinum averaged $1,003.20 per troy ounce, down 5.8 percent from $1,064.70 in FY2022. Palladium experienced the most dramatic decline among precious metals, falling 42.7 percent from $2,114.30 per troy ounce in FY2022 to $1,211.80 per troy ounce in FY2023, as electric vehicle adoption reduced demand for catalytic converters.

SECTION 4: COMPOSITE INDICES AND MARKET STRUCTURE

4.1 Producer Price Index for Commodities

The BLS Producer Price Index for all commodities (Series ID: WPU00000000) registered 202.4 in September 2023, compared to 212.4 in September 2022, representing a 4.7 percent year-over-year decline. The index peaked at 219.6 in June 2022 and has trended downward through the reporting period.

Sub-index performance for FY2023:
- Energy goods: -16.8 percent year-over-year
- Farm products: -8.3 percent year-over-year
- Processed foods and feeds: -5.1 percent year-over-year
- Metals and metal products: -3.2 percent year-over-year
- Chemicals and allied products: -6.4 percent year-over-year

4.2 Exchange Trading Volume

Total futures and options volume on U.S. designated contract markets reached 6.84 billion contracts in FY2023, up 3.1 percent from 6.63 billion contracts in FY2022. The CME Group accounted for 4.21 billion contracts, or 61.5 percent of total volume. The Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) accounted for 1.47 billion contracts, representing 21.5 percent of total volume.

SECTION 5: POLICY OBSERVATIONS AND OUTLOOK

The CFTC's Market Intelligence Branch, under the direction of Chief Economist Dr. Andrew Busch, notes that commodity markets in FY2023 demonstrated a broad normalization trend following the extraordinary price dislocations of FY2022. The Federal Reserve's cumulative interest rate increases of 525 basis points between March 2022 and July 2023 contributed to demand moderation across commodity-intensive sectors.

Looking ahead to FY2024, key risk factors include El Niño weather patterns affecting agricultural production in South America and Southeast Asia, OPEC+ production management decisions, and the pace of Chinese economic recovery following the removal of COVID-19 restrictions in December 2022.

DATA SOURCES AND METHODOLOGY

Price data were sourced from the following primary databases: CME Group DataMine, ICE Data Services, the USDA Agricultural Marketing Service, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) Weekly Petroleum Status Report, and the London Metal Exchange historical data archive. All prices reflect front-month futures contract settlement prices unless otherwise noted. Fiscal year averages are calculated as arithmetic means of daily settlement prices for the period October 1 through September 30.
