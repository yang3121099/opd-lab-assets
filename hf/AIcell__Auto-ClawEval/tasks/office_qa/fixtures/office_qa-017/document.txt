EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania recorded total General Fund revenues of $11.84 billion in the third quarter of fiscal year 2023-2024 (Q3 FY2023-24), representing an increase of $1.23 billion, or 11.6 percent, compared to the $10.61 billion collected in the second quarter (Q2 FY2023-24). Total expenditures for Q3 reached $12.07 billion, up from $11.49 billion in Q2, reflecting a quarter-over-quarter increase of $580 million, or 5.0 percent. The General Fund ended Q3 with a closing balance of $2.18 billion, compared to $2.41 billion at the close of Q2, a decrease of $230 million attributable to the revenue-expenditure gap during the quarter.

1.2 Key Revenue Highlights

Personal Income Tax (PIT): PIT collections of $4.89 billion in Q3 represented the strongest quarterly performance of the fiscal year. The increase reflects the January 2024 quarterly estimated payment deadline, which generated approximately $410 million in estimated payments from self-employed individuals and businesses. Withholding collections also rose by $262.6 million compared to Q2, consistent with seasonal employment patterns and a 3.1 percent increase in statewide payroll activity reported by the Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry.

Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT): The 31.2 percent quarter-over-quarter surge in CNIT collections to $1.34 billion reflects the February 15, 2024 estimated payment deadline for corporations with fiscal years ending in October 2023. Additionally, the CNIT rate reduction from 8.99 percent to 8.49 percent, effective January 1, 2024, under Act 53 of 2022's phased reduction schedule, did not materially suppress collections during this period as anticipated corporate profits remained elevated.

Sales and Use Tax: Collections of $3.29 billion reflected modest growth of 5.9 percent over Q2. January 2024 collections were slightly below projections at $1.06 billion, while February and March showed stronger performance at $1.08 billion and $1.15 billion respectively, suggesting improving consumer confidence heading into spring.

Realty Transfer Tax: The only major revenue category to decline quarter-over-quarter, falling $9.2 million or 6.2 percent to $139.4 million. This decline is consistent with the Pennsylvania Association of Realtors' report of a 4.8 percent reduction in residential property transactions statewide during Q3, attributed to mortgage rates averaging 6.87 percent during the quarter.

2.2 Key Expenditure Highlights

Department of Human Services: The $333.3 million increase in DHS spending was the largest driver of expenditure growth in Q3. Secretary Valerie Arkoosh attributed the increase to three primary factors: (1) a 2.1 percent increase in Medical Assistance enrollment, bringing total enrollment to 3.47 million Pennsylvanians as of March 31, 2024; (2) the January 2024 implementation of enhanced home and community-based services rates under the OBRA waiver program; and (3) increased utilization of behavioral health services, which rose 8.4 percent compared to Q2.

Debt Service: The $125.2 million quarter-over-quarter increase in debt service payments reflects the February 1, 2024 semi-annual payment on the Commonwealth's General Obligation bonds. Pennsylvania carried $10.84 billion in total outstanding GO bond debt as of March 31, 2024. The Treasurer's Office reported that the weighted average interest rate on outstanding GO bonds was 3.42 percent.

Department of Education: The $83.3 million increase reflects the February 2024 disbursement of the second installment of basic education funding to Pennsylvania's 500 school districts. The per-pupil allocation under the basic education funding formula averaged $8,214 for the 2023-24 school year, an increase of $312 per pupil compared to the prior year.

Department of Transportation: PennDOT expenditures declined $14.7 million in Q3, primarily due to the completion of several capital projects in Q2 and seasonal slowdowns in highway construction activity during January and February.

SECTION 3: FUND BALANCE AND LIQUIDITY

3.1 General Fund Balance

The General Fund closing balance of $2.18 billion as of March 31, 2024 remains above the Commonwealth's informal target of maintaining a balance equivalent to 5 percent of annual appropriations, which for FY2023-24 equals approximately $2.04 billion based on total appropriations of $40.8 billion.

3.2 Rainy Day Fund

The Commonwealth's Budget Stabilization Reserve Fund (Rainy Day Fund) maintained a balance of $1.83 billion as of March 31, 2024, unchanged from December 31, 2023. No transfers into or out of the Rainy Day Fund occurred during Q3. Pennsylvania's Rainy Day Fund balance represents approximately 4.5 percent of FY2023-24 appropriations, placing the Commonwealth in the middle tier of states nationally by this measure, according to the National Association of State Budget Officers' Spring 2024 Fiscal Survey.

SECTION 4: YEAR-TO-DATE PERFORMANCE AND OUTLOOK

Through the first three quarters of FY2023-24 (July 1, 2023 – March 31, 2024), total General Fund revenues reached $33.47 billion against the annual estimate of $44.12 billion, representing 75.9 percent of the full-year target. Expenditures through Q3 totaled $34.31 billion, or 84.1 percent of total appropriations.

The Office of the Budget projects Q4 FY2023-24 revenues of approximately $10.65 billion, which would bring the full-year total to $44.12 billion, consistent with the Governor's Executive Budget estimate submitted to the General Assembly on February 6, 2024. Key risks to this projection include potential softening in personal income tax withholding if labor market conditions deteriorate, and uncertainty surrounding federal Medicaid reimbursement rates for the final quarter.
