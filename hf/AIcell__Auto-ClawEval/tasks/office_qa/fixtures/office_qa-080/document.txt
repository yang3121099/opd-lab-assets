DEPARTMENT OF THE TREASURY
OFFICE OF FISCAL ANALYSIS AND BUDGET REVIEW

FEDERAL EXPENDITURE TREND ANALYSIS
Fiscal Years 2019 Through 2024

Published: March 14, 2025
Report Reference: OFA-2025-ETR-007

---

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of federal government expenditure trends across the six-fiscal-year period spanning FY2019 through FY2024. Total federal outlays grew from $4.45 trillion in FY2019 to $6.75 trillion in FY2024, representing a cumulative increase of 51.7 percent over the review period. The analysis identifies mandatory spending as the dominant driver of expenditure growth, accounting for 68.3 percent of total outlays in FY2024 compared to 61.4 percent in FY2019. This report is intended to inform congressional budget deliberations and support the President's FY2026 budget submission.

---

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF TOTAL FEDERAL OUTLAYS

Federal spending has undergone substantial transformation since FY2019, shaped by pandemic-era emergency appropriations, demographic pressures on entitlement programs, and elevated defense requirements following the deterioration of the international security environment beginning in 2022.

Total Federal Outlays by Fiscal Year (in billions of dollars):

FY2019: $4,447.2
FY2020: $6,552.0
FY2021: $6,818.2
FY2022: $6,271.6
FY2023: $6,134.4
FY2024: $6,751.8

The spike observed in FY2020 and FY2021 reflects emergency pandemic relief legislation, including the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act signed on March 27, 2020, which alone authorized $2.2 trillion in emergency spending. The subsequent decline in FY2022 and FY2023 represents the expiration of most temporary pandemic programs, while the FY2024 increase reflects resumed structural growth in mandatory programs and supplemental defense appropriations.

As a share of gross domestic product, federal outlays stood at 20.7 percent in FY2019, peaked at 31.2 percent in FY2020, and settled at 23.1 percent in FY2024. The 40-year historical average for federal outlays as a share of GDP is approximately 21.4 percent, indicating that current spending levels remain elevated relative to long-run norms.

---

SECTION 2: MANDATORY SPENDING TRENDS

Mandatory spending, which includes Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and other entitlement programs, has been the fastest-growing component of the federal budget over the review period.

Social Security

Social Security outlays totaled $1,058.9 billion in FY2019 and grew to $1,461.7 billion in FY2024, an increase of $402.8 billion or 38.1 percent. This growth is primarily attributable to the retirement of the Baby Boom generation, with the number of Social Security beneficiaries increasing from 68.1 million in FY2019 to 72.9 million in FY2024. The average monthly benefit for retired workers rose from $1,461 in January 2019 to $1,907 in January 2024, driven by cost-of-living adjustments including the 8.7 percent COLA applied in January 2023, the largest single adjustment since 1981.

Medicare

Medicare outlays grew from $644.8 billion in FY2019 to $1,022.4 billion in FY2024, representing a 58.6 percent increase and the highest growth rate among major mandatory programs. The program crossed the $1 trillion threshold for the first time in FY2024. Enrollment in Medicare reached 67.5 million beneficiaries in FY2024, up from 61.2 million in FY2019. Per-enrollee spending increased at an average annual rate of 4.3 percent over the period, reflecting rising prescription drug costs and increased utilization of outpatient services.

Medicaid

Federal Medicaid outlays increased from $389.1 billion in FY2019 to $616.3 billion in FY2024, a 58.4 percent increase. The Families First Coronavirus Response Act of March 2020 prohibited states from disenrolling Medicaid beneficiaries during the public health emergency, contributing to enrollment growth from 71.3 million in FY2019 to a peak of 94.5 million in FY2023. Following the end of the continuous enrollment requirement in April 2023, enrollment declined to approximately 84.7 million by the close of FY2024 as states conducted eligibility redeterminations.

Interest on the Public Debt

Net interest payments on the federal debt represent one of the most rapidly growing expenditure categories, rising from $375.6 billion in FY2019 to $892.2 billion in FY2024, an increase of 137.5 percent. This growth reflects both the expansion of debt outstanding and the sharp rise in interest rates beginning in March 2022, when the Federal Reserve initiated its rate-hiking cycle. The effective interest rate on federal debt held by the public rose from approximately 2.4 percent in FY2019 to 3.6 percent in FY2024. Net interest now constitutes 13.2 percent of total federal outlays, compared to 8.4 percent in FY2019.

SECTION 3: DISCRETIONARY SPENDING TRENDS

Total discretionary spending, which requires annual appropriations, declined as a share of total outlays from 30.2 percent in FY2019 to 26.1 percent in FY2024, though nominal dollar amounts increased modestly.

Defense Discretionary Spending

Base defense discretionary spending grew from $676.1 billion in FY2019 to $886.3 billion in FY2024, an increase of 31.1 percent. The FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act, signed December 22, 2023, authorized $886.3 billion in total defense spending, including a 5.2 percent increase over the FY2023 enacted level. Supplemental appropriations of $61.4 billion enacted in April 2024 provided additional funding for Ukraine security assistance and Indo-Pacific deterrence activities, bringing total defense-related outlays to approximately $947.7 billion in FY2024.

Non-Defense Discretionary Spending

Non-defense discretionary outlays totaled $683.4 billion in FY2019 and grew to $911.2 billion in FY2024, a 33.3 percent increase. Major contributors to this growth include:

- Department of Education: outlays increased from $68.2 billion in FY2019 to $97.4 billion in FY2024, driven by expanded Pell Grant funding and pandemic-era Elementary and Secondary School Emergency Relief (ESSER) disbursements totaling $189.5 billion across three legislative packages.

- Department of Transportation: outlays grew from $87.3 billion in FY2019 to $131.6 billion in FY2024, reflecting implementation of the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act signed November 15, 2021, which provided $550 billion in new infrastructure investment over five years.

- Department of Veterans Affairs: outlays increased from $86.5 billion in FY2019 to $135.7 billion in FY2024, a 56.9 percent increase, partly attributable to expanded healthcare eligibility under the PACT Act signed August 10, 2022.

---

SECTION 4: YEAR-OVER-YEAR GROWTH RATE ANALYSIS

Annual Expenditure Growth Rates:

FY2019 to FY2020: +47.3 percent (pandemic emergency spending)
FY2020 to FY2021: +4.1 percent (continued pandemic relief)
FY2021 to FY2022: -8.0 percent (expiration of temporary programs)
FY2022 to FY2023: -2.2 percent (continued normalization)
FY2023 to FY2024: +10.1 percent (structural growth and supplemental appropriations)

Excluding the pandemic years of FY2020 and FY2021, the compound annual growth rate for federal outlays from FY2019 to FY2024 is approximately 8.7 percent, substantially above the average inflation rate of 4.1 percent over the same period as measured by the Consumer Price Index.

---

SECTION 5: EXPENDITURE COMPOSITION SHIFT

The composition of federal spending has shifted materially over the review period. In FY2019, Social Security and Medicare together represented 38.4 percent of total outlays. By FY2024, these two programs alone accounted for 36.8 percent of a substantially larger budget, or $2,484.1 billion in combined outlays. When net interest is added, these three categories consumed 50.0 percent of total FY2024 outlays, leaving the remaining 50.0 percent to fund all other government activities including defense, education, transportation, and law enforcement.

The Congressional Budget Office projects that if current law is maintained, mandatory spending and net interest will account for 78 percent of total federal outlays by FY2034, leaving only 22 percent for all discretionary programs combined.

---

SECTION 6: CONCLUSIONS AND OBSERVATIONS

The six-year expenditure trend analysis reveals several structural concerns warranting attention from policymakers:

1. The growth in net interest payments from $375.6 billion to $892.2 billion represents the most rapid proportional increase of any major spending category and is largely insensitive to near-term policy intervention.

2. Medicare crossing the $1 trillion annual outlay threshold in FY2024 marks a significant fiscal milestone and reflects demographic and cost pressures that will intensify through the 2030s.

3. The normalization of spending following the pandemic has not returned outlays to pre-pandemic growth trajectories; FY2024 outlays of $6.75 trillion exceed the pre-pandemic CBO baseline projection for FY2024 of $5.82 trillion by approximately $930 billion.

4. Defense supplemental appropriations have become a recurring feature of the budget process, complicating multi-year planning and obscuring the true baseline for national security spending.

This report will be updated following release of final FY2024 audited financial statements by the Government Accountability Office, expected no later than November 15, 2025.
