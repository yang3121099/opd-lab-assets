REVIEWER ASSIGNMENTS

Reviewer 1
Name: Dr. Priya Ramanathan
Affiliation: Department of Computer Science, University of Edinburgh
Expertise: Neural machine translation, low-resource NLP, transformer architectures
Conflict of Interest Declared: None
Review Status: Submitted on April 19, 2024
Overall Recommendation: Major Revision
Confidence Score: 4 out of 5

Summary of Reviewer 1 Assessment:
Dr. Ramanathan found the paper's core contribution — a modified cross-attention weighting scheme reducing parameter count by 23% — to be technically sound but insufficiently validated. She noted that experiments were conducted on only three language pairs (Tamil-English, Swahili-English, and Quechua-Spanish), which she considers too narrow to support the generalizability claims made in Section 4.2. She requested additional ablation studies and comparison against the mBART-50 baseline, which was absent from Table 3.

Reviewer 2
Name: Prof. Heinrich Brauer
Affiliation: Computational Linguistics Group, Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich
Expertise: Attention mechanisms, multilingual models, model compression
Conflict of Interest Declared: None
Review Status: Submitted on April 22, 2024
Overall Recommendation: Accept with Minor Revisions
Confidence Score: 5 out of 5

Summary of Reviewer 2 Assessment:
Prof. Brauer praised the paper's clarity and the novelty of the proposed Sparse Contextual Attention (SCA) module. He confirmed that the reported BLEU score improvements of 4.7 points on Tamil-English and 3.2 points on Swahili-English are meaningful and reproducible based on his independent verification of the methodology. His minor concerns relate to the discussion section's treatment of computational overhead, specifically that training time increases of approximately 11% are underreported relative to inference gains.

Reviewer 3
Name: Dr. Amara Diallo
Affiliation: African Language Technology Initiative, Université Cheikh Anta Diop, Dakar
Expertise: African language NLP, corpus development, low-resource translation
Conflict of Interest Declared: Potential — co-authored a 2022 workshop paper with one of the manuscript's authors (Author 3, identified through institutional affiliation). Conflict reviewed and deemed non-disqualifying by Editor-in-Chief Dr. Marcus Feldstein on March 29, 2024.
Review Status: Submitted on April 25, 2024
Overall Recommendation: Major Revision
Confidence Score: 3 out of 5

Summary of Reviewer 3 Assessment:
Dr. Diallo raised substantive concerns about the Swahili corpus used in the study, noting that the 87,000-sentence training set draws heavily from the Helsinki-NLP corpus without adequate acknowledgment of its known domain biases toward news text. She recommended the authors supplement with at least one conversational or informal domain dataset and revisit their chrF++ scores, which she believes are inflated by approximately 2 to 3 points as a result.
