Reviewer 1

Review Status: Submitted April 22, 2024
Recommendation: Major Revision
Score (1–10): 5.5

Summary of Reviewer 1 Comments:
Dr. Ramasubramanian found the proposed hierarchical attention framework conceptually sound but raised concerns about experimental design. Specifically, she noted that the authors evaluated on only 3 language pairs (Tamil-English, Finnish-English, Swahili-English) and argued that a minimum of 5 to 6 pairs would be necessary to support the generalizability claims made in Section 4.3. She also flagged that the BLEU score improvements reported (averaging 3.2 points over baseline) fall within the margin of statistical uncertainty given the reported standard deviations. She requests additional ablation studies isolating the contribution of the hierarchical component from the standard attention baseline.

Reviewer 2
Name: Prof. Marcus Delacroix
Affiliation: INRIA Paris, Multilingual Systems Research Group
Expertise: Statistical machine translation, cross-lingual transfer learning, evaluation metrics
Conflict Check Completed: Yes — No conflicts identified
Review Status: Submitted April 19, 2024
Recommendation: Accept with Minor Revisions
Score (1–10): 7.0

Summary of Reviewer 2 Comments:
Prof. Delacroix praised the paper's thorough literature review and the novelty of applying hierarchical attention at the subword morpheme level. He noted that the training corpus sizes (ranging from 18,000 to 74,000 sentence pairs depending on the language) are clearly documented and appropriate for low-resource settings. His primary concern is the absence of human evaluation alongside automatic metrics. He recommends the authors conduct at minimum a small-scale human adequacy and fluency evaluation with 100 randomly sampled translations per language pair. He also requests clarification on the tokenization strategy used for Swahili.

Reviewer 3
Name: Dr. Yuki Tanaka
Affiliation: National Institute of Informatics, Tokyo
Expertise: Neural machine translation, attention mechanisms, Japanese and agglutinative language processing
Conflict Check Completed: Yes — Potential conflict flagged (co-authored with second author in 2019); waiver approved by Editor-in-Chief on March 29, 2024
Review Status: Submitted April 25, 2024
Recommendation: Reject
Score (1–10): 3.5

Summary of Reviewer 3 Comments:
Dr. Tanaka expressed significant reservations about the paper's core claims. He argues that the proposed method is insufficiently differentiated from prior work by Nguyen et al. (2022) and Bapna and Firat (2019), and that the authors do not adequately address these comparisons in their related work section. He also identified a reproducibility concern: the custom dataset used for Tamil-English contains 18,400 sentence pairs that are not publicly available, and no data release is planned. He considers this a critical flaw given the journal's reproducibility standards adopted in January 2023.
