REVIEW SUMMARIES

Reviewer 1 (Dr. Volkov) — Score: 3.5/5 — Recommendation: Major Revision
The reviewer praised the novel attention pruning strategy introduced in Section 3.2, noting that the reported 18.3% reduction in computational overhead is a meaningful contribution. However, Dr. Volkov raised concerns about the evaluation methodology, specifically that BLEU scores were reported without statistical significance testing across the 7 language pairs examined. The reviewer also questioned whether the baseline comparison against the 2022 Helsinki-NLP model was appropriate given architectural differences. Requested 3 additional ablation studies.

Reviewer 2 (Prof. Delacroix) — Score: 4.1/5 — Recommendation: Minor Revision
Prof. Delacroix expressed strong support for the paper's empirical contributions, particularly the experiments conducted on Yoruba, Swahili, and Tigrinya datasets totaling 2.4 million sentence pairs. The reviewer identified a minor inconsistency between Table 4 and Figure 6, where the reported chrF++ score for Swahili-English translation differs by 0.7 points. Requested clarification on data preprocessing steps and confirmation of the 80/10/10 train/validation/test split described on page 9.

Reviewer 3 (Dr. Adeyinka) — Score: 3.8/5 — Recommendation: Major Revision
Dr. Adeyinka provided the most detailed linguistic critique, noting that the paper's treatment of tonal languages is insufficient. The reviewer pointed out that the 14 low-resource languages in the study include 6 tonal languages, yet the attention visualization analysis in Section 4.4 does not address tonal feature representation. Additionally, the reviewer flagged that the Tigrinya dataset sourced from the OPUS corpus contains known annotation errors affecting approximately 3.2% of sentence pairs, which may have influenced the reported results.
