REVIEW SUMMARY

Reviewer 1 (Dr. Bhattacharya) awarded the manuscript a technical quality score of 3.2 out of 5.0. Primary concerns centered on the experimental validation, specifically that the authors tested their modified attention mechanism on only 4 language pairs (Swahili-English, Nepali-English, Kazakh-English, and Maltese-English) and that the BLEU score improvements reported — averaging 2.7 points over baseline — fall below the threshold of 3.0 points that the reviewer considers statistically meaningful without additional significance testing. Dr. Bhattacharya requested that the authors provide bootstrap resampling results across at least 1,000 iterations and expand evaluation to a minimum of 6 language pairs.

Reviewer 2 (Prof. Lindqvist) awarded a technical quality score of 4.1 out of 5.0. The reviewer praised the novel sparse attention variant introduced in Section 3.4, noting that the 23% reduction in computational overhead during inference is a genuine contribution to the field. Minor concerns included insufficient ablation studies and a request to clarify the parameter count of the proposed model (stated as 340 million parameters in the abstract but 347 million in Table 2 — a discrepancy requiring correction).

Reviewer 3 (Dr. Diallo) awarded a technical quality score of 3.5 out of 5.0. Given the manuscript's focus on low-resource languages, Dr. Diallo expressed concern that the training data sizes used — ranging from 50,000 to 180,000 sentence pairs — may not adequately represent true low-resource conditions, where corpora often contain fewer than 10,000 examples. The reviewer also noted that two of the four cited baseline systems (Nguyen et al., 2022 and Ferreira & Osei, 2023) use different tokenization strategies, which may confound the comparative results.
