EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of United States defense expenditures for Fiscal Year 2024 (October 1, 2023 through September 30, 2024), as compiled by the Office of Management and Budget in coordination with the Department of Defense Comptroller's office under Deputy Chief Financial Officer Michael J. McCord. Total obligated defense spending reached $886.4 billion in FY2024, representing a 3.2 percent increase over the FY2023 enacted level of $858.5 billion. This figure encompasses both the base defense budget and supplemental appropriations approved by Congress during the fiscal year.

SECTION 1: TOTAL DEFENSE BUDGET OVERVIEW

The FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act, signed into law by President Biden on December 22, 2023, authorized $886.3 billion in total defense spending. This authorization included:

  Base Department of Defense Budget: $841.4 billion
  Nuclear weapons activities (Department of Energy): $31.8 billion
  Defense-related activities (other agencies): $13.2 billion

Compared to the prior five fiscal years, the trajectory of defense spending has shown consistent growth:

  FY2020: $738.0 billion
  FY2021: $753.5 billion
  FY2022: $782.0 billion
  FY2023: $858.5 billion
  FY2024: $886.4 billion

The compound annual growth rate (CAGR) over this five-year period stands at 3.7 percent. When adjusted for inflation using the GDP deflator, real defense spending growth averaged 1.1 percent annually over the same period, indicating that a significant portion of nominal budget increases has been absorbed by rising personnel and procurement costs.

As a share of gross domestic product, defense spending in FY2024 represented approximately 3.1 percent, down from a post-9/11 peak of 4.7 percent in FY2010 but consistent with the 3.0 to 3.2 percent range maintained since FY2019.

SECTION 3: SPENDING BY APPROPRIATION CATEGORY

3.1 Personnel Costs

Military personnel costs totaled $176.3 billion in FY2024, accounting for 19.9 percent of the total defense budget. This represents an increase of $9.2 billion (5.5 percent) over FY2023, driven primarily by a 5.2 percent basic pay raise that took effect January 1, 2024 — the largest military pay increase in more than two decades. The active-duty end strength authorized for FY2024 was 1,284,500 personnel across all services, a reduction of approximately 3,100 from FY2023 authorized levels, reflecting ongoing recruiting challenges particularly in the Army, which fell short of its FY2023 enlisted recruiting goal by approximately 15,000 soldiers.

3.2 Operations and Maintenance

Operations and Maintenance (O&M) appropriations totaled $299.1 billion, representing 33.8 percent of the total defense budget and the single largest spending category. O&M funds day-to-day military readiness, base operations, training, and equipment sustainment. The Navy's O&M account grew by 6.1 percent to $68.4 billion, partially reflecting increased ship maintenance backlogs at public shipyards including Puget Sound Naval Shipyard in Bremerton, Washington, and Norfolk Naval Shipyard in Portsmouth, Virginia.

3.3 Procurement

Procurement accounts totaled $170.4 billion in FY2024, an increase of $7.8 billion (4.8 percent) from FY2023. Key procurement highlights include:

  F-35 Joint Strike Fighter (Lockheed Martin): $15.6 billion for 83 aircraft across all variants
  Virginia-class submarine (General Dynamics/Huntington Ingalls): $9.8 billion for 2 boats
  CH-47F Chinook Block II helicopter (Boeing): $1.1 billion for 24 aircraft
  Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System (GMLRS) munitions: $2.4 billion
  Patriot Advanced Capability-3 (PAC-3) missiles (Lockheed Martin): $1.8 billion

The significant investment in munitions replenishment reflects drawdown of stockpiles associated with security assistance to Ukraine, which totaled approximately $44.2 billion in Presidential Drawdown Authority and Foreign Military Financing between February 2022 and September 2024.

3.4 Research, Development, Test and Evaluation

RDT&E funding reached $145.9 billion in FY2024, representing 16.5 percent of the total defense budget and a 5.1 percent increase over FY2023. The largest single RDT&E program remains the Columbia-class ballistic missile submarine at $4.2 billion, followed by the Next Generation Air Dominance (NGAD) program at an estimated $3.1 billion (classified details withheld per 10 U.S.C. § 3208). Hypersonic weapons development programs collectively received approximately $6.9 billion across the Army, Navy, and Air Force.

3.5 Military Construction and Family Housing

Military Construction appropriations of $15.2 billion funded 263 individual construction projects at installations worldwide. Notable projects include $847 million for infrastructure improvements at Andersen Air Force Base in Guam, consistent with the Pacific Deterrence Initiative, and $412 million for barracks construction at Fort Liberty (formerly Fort Bragg) in North Carolina.

SECTION 4: SUPPLEMENTAL APPROPRIATIONS

Congress passed two supplemental defense appropriations packages during FY2024. The first, enacted April 24, 2024 as part of the National Security Supplemental (Public Law 118-50), provided $61.4 billion in additional defense-related funding, of which $13.8 billion was designated for direct DoD operations and procurement, $26.4 billion for Ukraine security assistance, and $4.0 billion for Indo-Pacific security initiatives. The second supplemental, enacted August 7, 2024, provided an additional $2.1 billion for emergency military construction and disaster recovery at installations affected by Hurricane Debby.

SECTION 5: COMPARATIVE INTERNATIONAL CONTEXT

The United States accounted for approximately 37 percent of total global defense expenditures in 2024, according to data compiled by the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI). The next largest defense budgets were:

  People's Republic of China: estimated $296 billion (official figure; independent estimates range to $450 billion)
  Russian Federation: approximately $109 billion
  India: $83.6 billion
  Saudi Arabia: $75.8 billion
  United Kingdom: $74.9 billion

All 32 NATO member nations collectively spent approximately $1.34 trillion on defense in 2024. Of the 32 members, 23 met or exceeded the NATO target of spending 2.0 percent of GDP on defense, compared to only 11 members who met the threshold in 2021, reflecting the significant shift in European defense posture following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

SECTION 6: AUDIT STATUS AND FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

The Department of Defense has now completed seven consecutive annual financial statement audits, having initiated its first full audit in FY2018. The FY2024 audit, conducted by independent public accounting firms including KPMG LLP and Ernst & Young LLP under contract to the DoD Inspector General, resulted in a disclaimer of opinion for the seventh consecutive year, meaning auditors were unable to express an opinion on the reliability of DoD's financial statements. However, the number of material weaknesses identified decreased from 28 in FY2022 to 24 in FY2024, indicating incremental progress. The Army resolved its longstanding material weakness related to General Fund property, plant, and equipment valuation, a significant milestone in the department's financial improvement efforts.

SECTION 7: OUTLOOK FOR FISCAL YEAR 2025

The President's FY2025 budget request, submitted to Congress on March 11, 2024, proposed $849.8 billion in base defense spending — a figure subsequently increased through the congressional appropriations process. As of the publication date of this report, FY2025 defense appropriations remain subject to a continuing resolution at FY2024 levels, with full-year appropriations pending final congressional action. The Congressional Budget Office projects that maintaining current defense programs and commitments through FY2030 will require average annual appropriations of approximately $920 billion, assuming inflation averaging 2.8 percent annually and no significant changes to force structure or strategic commitments.
