EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Department of Defense submitted a base budget request of $849.8 billion for Fiscal Year 2024, representing a 3.2 percent increase over the enacted FY2023 budget of $823.0 billion. When combined with $68.3 billion in supplemental funding for overseas contingency operations and emergency security assistance, total national defense outlays are projected to reach $918.1 billion. This report provides a comprehensive analysis of defense appropriations, spending trends, programmatic allocations, and comparative benchmarks against allied nations and historical precedent.

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF FY2024 DEFENSE APPROPRIATIONS

The National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2024, signed into law by President Biden on December 22, 2023, authorized $886.3 billion in total defense spending. This figure encompasses both the base Department of Defense budget and nuclear weapons activities administered by the Department of Energy's National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), which received $24.3 billion in FY2024 funding.

The base DoD budget of $849.8 billion is distributed across the following major appropriations categories:

MILITARY PERSONNEL: $176.2 billion (20.7% of base budget)
OPERATION AND MAINTENANCE: $310.4 billion (36.5% of base budget)
PROCUREMENT: $170.6 billion (20.1% of base budget)
RESEARCH, DEVELOPMENT, TEST AND EVALUATION (RDT&E): $145.0 billion (17.1% of base budget)
MILITARY CONSTRUCTION AND FAMILY HOUSING: $18.3 billion (2.2% of base budget)
REVOLVING AND MANAGEMENT FUNDS: $29.3 billion (3.4% of base budget)

SECTION 2: FIVE-YEAR SPENDING TREND ANALYSIS (FY2020–FY2024)

Defense spending has increased by approximately $174.3 billion, or 25.8 percent, over the five-year period from FY2020 through FY2024. The following table illustrates enacted base budgets across this period:

FISCAL YEAR | ENACTED BASE BUDGET | YEAR-OVER-YEAR CHANGE
FY2020      | $738.0 billion      | +3.1%
FY2021      | $753.5 billion      | +2.1%
FY2022      | $782.0 billion      | +3.8%
FY2023      | $823.0 billion      | +5.2%
FY2024      | $849.8 billion      | +3.2%

The largest single-year percentage increase during this period occurred in FY2023, driven primarily by a $28.4 billion increase in Operation and Maintenance accounts and a $14.7 billion increase in procurement to address readiness shortfalls identified by the Joint Chiefs of Staff in the FY2022 National Defense Strategy.

Adjusted for inflation using the DoD deflator index, real defense spending growth from FY2020 to FY2024 is estimated at 8.4 percent, reflecting that a significant portion of nominal budget growth has been absorbed by defense-specific inflation, particularly in fuel costs, shipbuilding, and military compensation.

SECTION 3: SERVICE-LEVEL BUDGET ALLOCATIONS

The FY2024 base budget is allocated among the military departments and defense-wide accounts as follows:

DEPARTMENT OF THE ARMY: $185.7 billion (21.9%)
DEPARTMENT OF THE NAVY (including Marine Corps): $230.8 billion (27.2%)
DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE (including Space Force): $258.4 billion (30.4%)
DEFENSE-WIDE ACCOUNTS: $174.9 billion (20.6%)

The Department of the Air Force received the largest single-service allocation for the fourth consecutive fiscal year, reflecting continued investment in the F-35A procurement program, B-21 Raider bomber development, and Space Force constellation expansion. The Air Force's RDT&E budget alone totaled $47.3 billion, the highest of any military department.

The Navy's $230.8 billion allocation includes $32.4 billion for shipbuilding and conversion, supporting the procurement of two Virginia-class submarines, two Arleigh Burke-class destroyers, and one San Antonio-class amphibious transport dock. The Navy's shipbuilding budget represents a $4.1 billion increase over FY2023 levels.

SECTION 4: MAJOR PROGRAM INVESTMENTS

The following programs represent the largest individual procurement and development investments in the FY2024 budget:

F-35 JOINT STRIKE FIGHTER PROGRAM
Total FY2024 funding: $15.6 billion
Units procured: 83 aircraft across all variants (48 F-35A, 20 F-35B, 15 F-35C)
Cumulative program cost to date: $412.7 billion
Program Executive Officer: Vice Admiral Karl Thomas

B-21 RAIDER BOMBER PROGRAM
Total FY2024 funding: $5.8 billion
Development phase: Engineering and Manufacturing Development (EMD)
Prime contractor: Northrop Grumman Corporation
Projected initial operational capability: 2027

COLUMBIA-CLASS BALLISTIC MISSILE SUBMARINE
Total FY2024 funding: $6.2 billion
Lead ship delivery: Scheduled for FY2031
Total program acquisition cost estimate: $132.0 billion (12 submarines)

GROUND-BASED STRATEGIC DETERRENT (GBSD / LGM-35A SENTINEL)
Total FY2024 funding: $3.7 billion
Prime contractor: Northrop Grumman Corporation
Replaces: LGM-30G Minuteman III intercontinental ballistic missile

NEXT GENERATION AIR DOMINANCE (NGAD)
Total FY2024 RDT&E funding: $1.8 billion
Program status: Technology maturation and risk reduction phase

SECTION 5: DEFENSE SPENDING AS A PERCENTAGE OF GDP AND FEDERAL BUDGET

At $849.8 billion, the FY2024 base defense budget represents approximately 3.1 percent of projected U.S. Gross Domestic Product of $27.4 trillion. When total national defense outlays of $918.1 billion are considered, the figure rises to 3.35 percent of GDP.

As a share of total federal outlays projected at $6.9 trillion for FY2024, defense spending accounts for approximately 13.3 percent of all federal expenditures. This compares to mandatory spending programs, which collectively represent 63.4 percent of total federal outlays, with Social Security alone accounting for $1.47 trillion.

Historical comparison points:
- FY2010 (peak post-9/11 spending): Defense represented 4.7% of GDP
- FY2015: Defense represented 3.3% of GDP
- FY2019 (pre-COVID baseline): Defense represented 3.2% of GDP
- FY2024: Defense represents 3.1% of GDP

SECTION 6: NATO BURDEN-SHARING AND ALLIED COMPARISONS

The United States continues to account for the largest share of NATO defense spending among alliance members. Based on NATO Headquarters data published in July 2023, U.S. defense expenditures represent approximately 68.7 percent of total NATO alliance defense spending.

Among the 31 NATO member nations, 11 met or exceeded the alliance benchmark of 2.0 percent of GDP devoted to defense in 2023, compared to only 7 nations in 2021. Key allied defense budgets for comparison:

UNITED KINGDOM: $73.5 billion (2.07% of GDP)
GERMANY: $66.8 billion (1.57% of GDP)
FRANCE: $61.3 billion (2.06% of GDP)
POLAND: $31.6 billion (3.90% of GDP)
CANADA: $26.9 billion (1.38% of GDP)

Poland's defense spending as a percentage of GDP, at 3.90 percent, exceeded that of the United States and represented the highest burden-sharing ratio among NATO members in 2023, driven by Warsaw's accelerated military modernization program in response to the ongoing conflict in Ukraine.

SECTION 7: UKRAINE SECURITY ASSISTANCE SUPPLEMENTAL

Congress approved $61.4 billion in supplemental security assistance for Ukraine through the Emergency National Security Supplemental Appropriations Act, signed April 24, 2024. Of this total, $13.8 billion was designated for direct military equipment transfers from DoD inventories, $26.4 billion for Ukraine Security Assistance Initiative (USAI) procurement, and $7.9 billion for Presidential Drawdown Authority replenishment.

Since the initiation of large-scale Russian military operations in February 2022 through December 31, 2023, the United States has committed approximately $44.2 billion in military assistance to Ukraine, including 31 M1A1 Abrams main battle tanks, 186 M2 Bradley infantry fighting vehicles, and 20 HIMARS multiple launch rocket systems.

SECTION 8: PROJECTED FUTURE REQUIREMENTS AND FISCAL OUTLOOK

The Congressional Budget Office projects that sustaining current defense programs and force structure through FY2033 will require cumulative defense appropriations of approximately $9.9 trillion over the ten-year period, averaging $990 billion annually. This projection assumes 2.0 percent annual real growth in the defense budget, consistent with the Biden administration's Future Years Defense Program (FYDP) submitted to Congress in March 2023.

Key cost drivers in the out-years include:
- Columbia-class submarine production ramp-up: estimated $8.0–10.0 billion annually by FY2028
- F-35 sustainment costs projected to reach $38.0 billion annually by FY2035
- Military compensation growth driven by the 5.2 percent Basic Pay increase enacted for January 2024
- Hypersonic weapons development programs across all three services: combined $4.7 billion in FY2024

SECTION 9: AUDIT AND FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT STATUS

The Department of Defense has now completed six consecutive department-wide financial statement audits, with the most recent audit for FY2023 completed on November 15, 2023. For the sixth consecutive year, the DoD received a disclaimer of opinion from independent auditors, indicating that the department could not provide sufficient documentation to support a clean audit opinion. The DoD Comptroller, Michael McCord, stated in testimony before the Senate Armed Services Committee on December 5, 2023, that the department has remediated 673 of 1,142 audit findings identified since the first full audit in FY2018, representing a 58.9 percent remediation rate.

CONCLUSION

The FY2024 defense budget of $849.8 billion reflects the Department of Defense's effort to balance near-term readiness requirements, long-term modernization investments, and alliance commitments within a constrained fiscal environment. The 3.2 percent nominal increase over FY2023 represents modest real growth when adjusted for defense-specific inflation. Continued investment in nuclear modernization, advanced air dominance, and undersea warfare capabilities reflects the strategic priorities outlined in the 2022 National Defense Strategy, which identifies the People's Republic of China as the pacing challenge and the Russian Federation as an acute threat to the international order.
